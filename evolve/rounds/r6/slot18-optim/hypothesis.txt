MECHANISM (one axis changed: optim; impl r6_crossenv_agreement_gate)

Imported from outside the domain: the AND-mask / Invariant Learning Consistency rule
(Parascandolo et al., "Learning explanations that are hard to vary"). There, a gradient
component is followed only when the environments agree on its sign,
    m_j = 1[ |sum_e sign(g_j^e)| >= tau * E ],
and the update is m (elementwise-times) sum_e g^e. The claim it makes is precisely the claim
this search needs: a direction that lowers the loss in only a few environments is an
environment-specific explanation (memorisation of one system's surface statistics), while a
direction the environments agree on is a mechanism. Our eval holds windows out BY IMAGE, so
only the second kind of direction can pay.

We cannot form per-environment gradients: the harness gives the optimiser one aggregate
gradient per step and passes no closure. But this genome's batcher is
r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum with n_block_images=1 and hard_frac_max=0.75, so after the ramp
three quarters of every batch is drawn from ONE system image, and the image is redrawn each
step. I confirmed against evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl that the "image" key is present
and that the sample carries 8 distinct images, so the batcher takes its blocked path rather
than its uniform fallback. Consecutive steps are therefore consecutive ENVIRONMENTS, and the
AND-mask consensus statistic can be accumulated over time instead of over a batch dimension:

    s_j  <- agree_beta * s_j + (1 - agree_beta) * sign(g_j)          (a third moment, beside m and v)
    a_j  =  |s_j| / (1 - agree_beta^t)                                (debiased, in [0,1])
    gate_j = agree_floor + (1 - agree_floor) * clip((a_j - agree_tau)/(1 - agree_tau), 0, 1)
    gate  <- gate / mean(gate),  clipped to agree_cap                 (mean-preserving)
    dtheta_j = -lr * gate_j * adamw_update_j

a_j is the exponentially weighted form of |mean_e sign(g_j^e)| over roughly the last
1/(1-agree_beta) ~ 33 environments. The gate multiplies the AdamW UPDATE, not the gradient, so
the second-moment statistics are untouched and the gate cannot feed back into its own
normaliser. The per-tensor mean division is deliberate and is what makes this an experiment
about direction rather than about step size: the average update magnitude per tensor is
preserved, and the step is only redistributed from coordinates the systems disagree about
toward coordinates they agree about. If every coordinate in a tensor has the same agreement,
the gate is uniformly 1 and the step is bit-comparable to the parent's.

WHY IT SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca is the paired difference between the native chain and the role-swapped chain. A
predictor that keys on which location is read contributes exactly zero. What is left is the
part of the model that actually carries a content's identity through the moves. Carrying
identity is a mechanism that must hold on a system the model has never seen: the same
delta-rule write/read geometry, the same transition operator, on alpine and on postgres. Its
gradient should therefore point the same way in image after image. Predicting from remembered
surface statistics of a particular image's filesystem lowers the training loss just as well
but points a different way in each image, so its sign EMA decays toward zero. Under the gate,
the first kind of direction gets up to agree_cap = 2.5x of the tensor's step budget and the
second kind gets pushed toward agree_floor. The prediction is that the trunk, the FiLM/fuse
path and the (D,D) transition readout — all of which sit in the gated group — spend more of
a fixed step budget on the transferable part of the fit.

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE

comp_ca above the parent's +0.0225, driven by a higher native_hit on held-out images with
swap_stayed roughly unchanged (the gate has no reason to move the chain-position shortcut,
which is exactly why it is worth measuring: if the gain arrives as a drop in swap_stayed
instead, the mechanism did something other than what I claim). Training loss at the end of the
budget should be equal to or slightly WORSE than the parent's — the gate deliberately declines
to spend the budget on directions that only fit some systems — while the held-out measurement
improves. If final training loss improves too, the gate is probably acting as a plain
preconditioner and the invariance story is not what happened. All of this is UNMEASURED; I
cannot run the harness from the jail.

WHY NO OTHER AXIS CHANGES

The mechanism is a statement about the optimiser's update rule only. It needs the batcher to
keep blocking batches by image, which the parent's batcher already does with the parameters it
already has, so the batcher is kept verbatim rather than co-designed; changing it would
confound the gate with a change in what "an environment" means. Every other axis is the
parent's, verbatim.

WHAT I KEPT FROM THE PARENT AND WHY

Everything else in the flat-basin regime is unchanged and untouched: Muon on the (key_d x d)
addressing projections, the soft spectral cap on the (D,D) transition readout, the carried
layerwise sharpness-aware displacement, the warmup/hold/cosine/floor schedule, and the
constant-LR tail averaging. This is a targeted edit; only the bulk optimiser's update rule
changed. In particular the Muon group is NOT gated: Muon's update is orthogonalised, and a
coordinatewise multiplier would destroy the spectral property that is the whole point of using
it on the addressing pairs. Gating there is a separate experiment, not this one.

CORRECTNESS NOTES

- No new model parameters are introduced, so there is nothing that could fail reachability by
  living under no_grad or on an untaken branch. The optimiser hyperparameters this impl adds
  all act on the live update of parameters that already receive gradient from the training
  loss and already affect the prediction at the scored read position; none of them is inert at
  the values the genome sets (agree_floor 0.2 < 1 and agree_renorm 1 keep the gate live).
- Nothing here indexes the token sequence, so the odd-length [cmd_0, obs_0, ..., cmd_r] layout
  is not a hazard for this axis; the impl only ever sees a flat parameter list.
- _AgreementAdamW subclasses torch.optim.Optimizer (required: LambdaLR type-checks its
  argument) and adds only gate_clock / gate_start / gate_ramp / _ramp, none of which collide
  with Optimizer's own names. It is not an nn.Module.
- With the gate branch skipped the update is arithmetically identical to torch.optim.AdamW:
  m/(1-b1^t) divided by sqrt(v/(1-b2^t)) + eps, with decoupled decay p *= 1 - lr*wd applied
  ungated. Bias correction is per-parameter (state "n"), as in torch.
- The gate is exactly 1 for the first gate_start steps and ramps linearly over gate_ramp
  steps, so it never acts on a sign EMA that has not filled; gate_start is floored at the end
  of LR warmup.
- No host synchronisation is added: there is no .item()/float() on any gated quantity, and the
  gate is built from elementwise ops and one reduction that stays on device. Cost per step is
  one extra sign, one EMA, and a handful of elementwise ops per bulk tensor, plus one extra
  state tensor per bulk parameter. This is negligible against the forward/backward, so the
  fixed step budget is unaffected — UNMEASURED, but bounded by inspection.
- Non-finite gradients are skipped per parameter, as the parent's Muon already does, and the
  gate is passed through nan_to_num with a fill of 1.0 so a degenerate tensor falls back to the
  ungated update rather than to a zero step.
