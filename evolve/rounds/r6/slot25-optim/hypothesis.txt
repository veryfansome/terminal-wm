AXES CHANGED: optim only (impl r18_spectral_capped_transition_readout -> r6_dualtimescale_slow_momentum_routing).
All six other axes keep the parent's impl and params verbatim, and the LR-shape hyperparameters
(warmup_frac 0.04, hold_frac 0.30, floor_ratio 0.05, beta2 0.95) and the parameter-grouping rule
(identical-shape (key_d, d) addressing pairs; (D, D) content readouts) are byte-identical to the
parent. The only thing that moves is the rule that turns a gradient into a step. No other axis
needs to change: the mechanism is a change of gradient timescale, and it reads nothing about the
model, the data layout, or the metric.

THE MECHANISM

Adam keeps one exponential average of the gradient with a horizon of ~1/(1-beta1) = 10 steps and
divides by the RMS of the raw gradient. This impl replaces that core, for every parameter, with a
two-timescale average taken from the AdEMAMix optimizer (Pagliardini et al., 2024) — a piece of
optimization literature outside this problem's field, translated here as equations rather than as
an analogy:

  m_fast_t = b1 * m_fast_{t-1} + (1-b1) * g_t
  m_slow_t = b3(t) * m_slow_{t-1} + (1-b3(t)) * g_t
  v_t      = b2 * v_{t-1} + (1-b2) * g_t^2
  step     = lr * ( m_fast_t/(1-b1^t) + alpha(t) * m_slow_t ) / ( sqrt(v_t/(1-b2^t)) + eps )
  decoupled weight decay: p <- p * (1 - lr*wd) before the step

with alpha(t) = alpha * min(1, t/T_alpha) and b3(t) the paper's inverted-log warmup from 0.9 to
b3_end, b3(t) = exp( ln(b3_0)*ln(b3_end) / ( (1-f)*ln(b3_end) + f*ln(b3_0) ) ), f = min(1, t/T_b3).
m_slow deliberately carries no bias correction, so its contribution is small until roughly one
slow horizon has elapsed.

Two things here are mine rather than the paper's, and both exist because this search trains under
a fixed step budget rather than a fixed wall clock. (1) The slow horizon is derived from `steps`:
b3_end = 1 - 1/(0.35*steps), clamped to [0.98, 0.9999], so the slow average always spans about a
third of whatever budget the harness passes instead of a hard-coded 10k-step horizon that a short
run would never reach. (2) T_alpha = T_b3 = 0.6*steps, so the first ~third of the run (the warmup
and the LR hold) is effectively plain Adam and the slow term only takes over across the cosine
anneal.

The addressing pairs get the same direction. The parent runs Muon on the identical-shape
(key_d, d) read/write projections: Nesterov momentum, then Newton-Schulz orthogonalization, then a
step whose size is fixed by rms_match*sqrt(max_dim). Here the vector fed to Newton-Schulz is the
same dual-timescale direction m_fast/(1-b1^t) + alpha(t)*m_slow instead of a 20-step heavy ball.
Because orthogonalization flattens the singular values, adding the slow term cannot change the
size of that step at all — only its direction — so this arm carries no stability cost. The
power-iteration soft spectral cap on (D, D) readouts is kept unchanged, as a norm guard, not as
the mechanism.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca RATHER THAN JUST THE LOSS

comp_ca rewards exactly one thing: carrying a particular content's identity through a chain of
silent moves, so that reading a location returns what is actually there. Location-keying cancels
per window by construction; chain-position keying cancels only in expectation.

The parameters that implement carrying are the ones whose gradient arrives through the recurrent
memory: tr_path / tr_in / tr_out / tr_read in the arch's per-path transition store, and the
delta-rule addressing pairs. That memory is written with a decay factor per step, so the gradient
that reaches an early write from a late read is discounted geometrically in the number of
intervening commands — it is a small, persistent signal riding under per-batch noise that is
several times larger. Adam's denominator normalizes by the RMS of the raw gradient, which contains
both, and its 10-step numerator averages away almost none of the noise: the routing signal and the
noise are boosted by the same factor. A long-horizon average is a matched filter for exactly this
component — noise that is independent across batches shrinks like 1/sqrt(horizon) inside m_slow
while a direction that is consistent over hundreds of steps survives at full amplitude. Adding
alpha*m_slow to the numerator while leaving the denominator on the fast second moment therefore
raises the effective step size *selectively*, on the consistent directions only: where gradients
decorrelate, m_slow is near zero and the update is unchanged Adam.

Three concrete consequences I expect, in order of how directly they are observable:
 1. Lower training loss at the same step count, since the budget is fixed and the paper's central
    claim is a per-step convergence gain.
 2. The gain concentrated on windows with more intervening moves (the d4+ half of the eligible
    slice rather than d3), because the discounting argument above is what the slow average is
    supposed to undo, and the d3 windows already get a comparatively undiscounted signal.
 3. comp_ca above the parent's +0.0187. I state this as unmeasured. No miniature run was performed
    and none would be informative here.

Secondarily, because alpha reaches full strength as the LR anneals, the last 40% of the run is an
implicit iterate average — the update direction is dominated by a mean of hundreds of past
gradients — which should behave like a Polyak tail while still steering the trajectory rather than
only overwriting the endpoint. This is a plausible route to better transfer across held-out
IMAGES, but it is a side effect of the timescale, not a separate mechanism, and I am not claiming
it independently.

FALSIFICATION

If the compositional signal is not a small-but-consistent gradient component — if instead it is
absent, or it is present but the model already fits it inside the budget — then the slow term adds
nothing beyond a modest effective-LR increase, training loss lands at or near the parent's, and
comp_ca moves within noise. If it is the opposite of consistent (the routing gradient flips sign
across batches because different windows want different addressing), m_slow will average toward
zero and the impl degenerates to plain Adam at a slightly lower lr, which would show as comp_ca
just below the parent's. Either outcome is a clean read on whether the timescale is the binding
constraint.

RISK AND HOW IT IS BOUNDED

The failure mode is instability: on a perfectly consistent direction the numerator can reach
(1+alpha)=3x Adam's, so the peak per-element step is up to 3*lr. I set lr = 4e-4, below the
parent's 5e-4, so the peak effective step is 1.2e-3 against the parent's 5e-4, and it is only
reached late in the run when the cosine multiplier is already falling. alpha = 2.0 is at the
conservative end of the published range (5-10 for large language models). The harness's global
grad-norm clip at 1.0 and the retained spectral cap are unchanged. This lr choice does mean the
comparison is not a pure single-variable swap; I chose the safety over the purity, and note that
holding lr at 5e-4 would have raised the effective step further rather than isolating anything.

COST: three optimizer buffers per parameter instead of two, and elementwise arithmetic only. On a
model of this size that is a few tens of MB and no measurable step-time change. The Newton-Schulz
arm does exactly the work the parent's Muon arm already did, on the same five-to-seven matrices.
