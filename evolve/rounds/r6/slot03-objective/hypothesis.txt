AXES CHANGED: objective only (r3_fwl_orthogonalized_ring_contrast -> r6_episodic_board_prototype_choice).
arch, target, optim, batcher, stream and head keep the parent's impls and params verbatim.

WHY ONLY ONE AXIS. The mechanism needs nothing the genome does not already supply: it reads pred,
tgt and the two causal ctx channels the WANTS_CTX contract already delivers, and baseline_interleave
already exposes extract_cmd_input so ctx["cmd"] exists. Changing arch or target as well would make
the result uninterpretable against the parent, and the parent's arch is in fact the ideal partner
(see LEVERAGE below), so I deliberately left the rest of the genome fixed.

THE MECHANISM. The parent is a batch-global reweighted InfoNCE: every row is discriminated against
every other row, negatives are made hard by TARGET-space proximity (the confusability ring), and the
command is removed from the negative geometry by a cross-fitted ridge (Frisch-Waugh-Lovell). Every
row is treated alike and the trajectory is never used as a unit. I replace that wholesale with an
EPISODIC forced choice that reconstructs the evaluation's own N-way question out of the raw training
trajectories, and makes it the term that dominates whenever the model is wrong.

The mechanism imported from outside the domain is the prototypical network episodic loss (Snell,
Swersky & Zemel 2017): an episode supplies a support set of classes, a query is scored by a softmax
over negative squared Euclidean distance to the class prototypes, and the loss is the cross entropy
of that softmax. Translated here, with everything computed from (pred, tgt, ctx) alone:

  EPISODE   = one trajectory. Recovered exactly from ctx["prev"], which the harness sets to the zero
              vector at the first command of a sequence and to the preceding observation everywhere
              else; seg = cumsum(prev.abs().amax(1) == 0), and the flattened cmd rows are contiguous
              per sequence in step order, so seg is a valid segment id and row index is time.
  CLASS     = a numerically equal target, pooled. Distances tt_ij = ||tgt_i - tgt_j||^2 / D;
              equivalent iff tt <= 1e-3 * mean_off. Classes whose in-batch multiplicity exceeds
              max(16, 0.02 n) are dropped: that is the empty-output cluster produced by every mv and
              every redirect, plus the system files that repeat across every trajectory of an
              image-blocked batch.
  SUPPORT_i = the classes exposed at STRICTLY EARLIER steps of row i's own episode. That is literally
              the CUPS board: the N contents standing at N locations before the chain of moves runs.
  QUERY     = a row whose own content numerically repeats a support content that was first exposed
              under a DIFFERENT command (command equality is exact-duplicate detection on ctx["cmd"]
              at the same 1e-3 relative tolerance, not a cosine threshold). This is exactly the row
              whose answer cannot be read off the command: the content standing here arrived by a
              move or a copy, not by the command naming it.
  LOSS_i    = logsumexp over support - logsumexp over the positive class, on logits
              -||pred_i - tgt_j||^2 / (D * temp_i) - log(multiplicity_ij). Subtracting log
              multiplicity turns the row-level logsumexp into the class prototype logit exactly when
              members are numerically equal, so each distinct content counts once however often it
              was re-read. temp_i = 0.25 * sep_i, where sep_i is the distance from the true content
              to its NEAREST board-mate in that episode, floored at max(0.1 * median sep, 5e-3 *
              mean_off). A per-query scale, not a batch-global one, is what makes this work.
  MARGIN    = softplus((d_true + 0.25 sep_i - d_nearest_foil) / (0.125 sep_i)) on the same queries,
              in the raw squared-L2 decision variable the probe itself uses to pick.

Two supporting terms: a batch-global class-pooled listwise term over all content rows, which keeps
the whole embedding space discriminative and delivers gradient to every row, and a 0.05 raw MSE
anchor. A constant prediction cannot minimize either, so the anti-collapse clause of the axis
contract holds; verified numerically (constant-zero 4.91 and constant-7.0 21.05 against ~0.08 for a
fitted prediction on the same batch).

WHY IT SHOULD MOVE comp_ca. comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed. swap_stayed is high exactly
when a read keeps returning the content that the NATIVE chain routed there after the chain has been
rewritten to route a different one, i.e. when the model answers the read with a board-mate rather
than with the content that was actually carried. My query set is precisely the training rows that
pose that question, my support set is precisely the foil set the probe draws from, and my loss is
precisely the probe's decision rule. The parent's loss does penalize picking a board-mate, but only
as one wrong retrieval among ~2000 in-batch candidates, at a batch-global temperature that is far
too soft for two contents sitting a fraction of the batch mean apart. Making the same error costly
at the board's own scale is the whole change. I measured on the jail's real trajectories that this
signal is dense and not a rare corner: 70 such rows across 24 trajectories (2.9 per trajectory,
~186 per 64-sequence batch), with a mean of 9.1 distinct earlier contents to choose among.

THE DEBIASING TERM, AND WHY IT IS THERE. On the jail's real trajectories the answer's RECENCY RANK
among the episode's support classes is far from uniform: rank 1 in 31/70 cases and rank 0 in 17/70,
so a fixed "second-most-recently established" policy would already be right 44% of the time against
~11% chance. That is the chain-position shortcut the objective's own documentation says cancels only
in expectation. I therefore reweight each query by inverse propensity to a uniform prior over that
rank: rank buckets that happen to be common in a batch are down-weighted, rare ones up-weighted, the
weight clamped to [0.25, 4]. The up-weighted queries are also the deepest chains (the true content
established long before many others), so the same term doubles as a depth curriculum aimed at the
d3/d4+ slice. Measured effect on the gradient in a synthetic batch: 30-38% relative change versus
uniform weights, so it is not a no-op.

FALSIFIABLE PREDICTIONS.
1. comp_ca beats the parent's +0.0112. UNMEASURED - no real training run is possible from the jail,
   and this project has seen miniature probes invert rank in both directions, so I am not
   extrapolating from the synthetic checks below.
2. native_hit rises and swapped-held falls, rather than both moving together. If instead both rise,
   the loss taught a stickier location-keyed or position-keyed reader and the mechanism failed on
   its own terms.
3. private.shortcut_leaning should be no worse than the parent's, and ideally lower, because the
   rank debiasing removes the training-side propensity a position policy would exploit. This is the
   one prediction that would be most informative if it fails.
4. The gain, if any, should be larger on d4+ windows than on d3, since deeper chains are exactly the
   up-weighted tail.

WHAT I VERIFIED (synthetic, CPU, real shapes n=1984 D=768, plus the real trajectory statistics
quoted above; none of this is a fitness claim):
- Finite loss and finite gradient on n = 1, 2, 4, 15, 16, 17, 33, 64, 127, on float32 and float64,
  with ctx None / not a dict / missing a key / wrong-shaped, with no segment starts, with all targets
  identical, all commands identical, and target scales 1e-6 and 1e4. Nothing in the module assumes an
  even row count or that a command is followed by an observation; it only ever indexes the flattened
  command rows it is handed.
- Every row receives gradient (1984/1984) whenever the prediction is not already exact.
- Optimizing free predictions under this loss drives the forced choice to 64/64 correct at board
  confusabilities spanning 30x, and the query rows' nearest neighbour among ALL 1984 batch targets is
  the right content 64/64. Substituting a board-mate's prediction at the query rows raises the loss
  from 0.06 to 93.4, which is the discrimination the metric measures.
- Cost ~28 ms/step on CPU at n=1984, D=768, dominated by three n-by-n GEMMs; it removes the parent's
  per-step 1024x1024 ridge solve, so it should be cheaper than the parent inside the fixed step
  budget. UNMEASURED on the real GPU lane.
- I introduce no trainable parameters, so nothing new needs to reach the scored read position; the
  arch's and head's parameters keep gradient through the global term and the anchor, which cover
  every row, and through the episodic term at the routed-read rows.

A DESIGN BUG I FOUND AND FIXED, worth recording because the parent family carries the same shape.
My first version used a batch-global margin (0.25 * mean off-diagonal target distance) for the
hinge, copying the parent's convention. When two board-mates sit closer together than that margin
the hinge is unsatisfiable, and reducing it pushes the prediction AWAY from its own target along the
foil direction without bound: query-row MSE blew up to 8.1 against a batch mean of 0.61 and the
forced choice went to 0/64. Rescaling the margin, the temperature and the hinge slope to that
query's OWN nearest-board-mate separation fixes it completely (0/64 -> 64/64, query MSE 1.05). The
lesson generalizes: on this task the decision scale is set by the board, not by the batch.

LEVERAGE ON THE PARENT'S ARCH, which is why I left it alone. r23_dual_address_transport_pointer
already contains a transport memory that reads content at a source address, writes it at a
destination address and erases the source, plus a pointer-copy readout over the earlier observations.
It has the machinery to answer "which content is here now" but its only supervision under the parent
objective is an undifferentiated batch-wide retrieval loss. The episodic term is the supervision
that specific machinery was built for: it asks, at exactly the rows where a transport happened,
which of the board's contents the pointer should be copying.

BIGGEST RISK. The rank debiasing could cost measured score even while improving the capability. The
brief states plainly that on this frozen slice a first-mover lookup outscores every mechanism
measured so far; if part of the parent's +0.0112 comes from residual position-keying, flattening the
training-side positional prior removes it and comp_ca could fall even though native routing improved.
A secondary risk is narrower: the query set is built from EXACT target duplication, so it captures
mv/cp reads whose content reappears verbatim but misses compositional reads whose observation is a
transformation of earlier ones (an `ls` of a directory after a move, an `acc.dat` after appends).
If most of the eval's depth lives in those, the term fires on the wrong rows and the result lands
near the parent rather than below it.
