MECHANISM

The parent re-metrizes the target space with the MARGINAL second moment of the next-observation
targets, raised to a NEGATIVE power (ZCA whitening, p = 0.75). I replace the matrix it is built
from and I flip the sign of the exponent. My forward map is built from the CONFUSABLE-PAIR
SCATTER: each step I subsample <=256 of the batch's standardized targets, form all pairwise
squared distances, discard any pair closer than 2% of the mean pairwise distance (the repeated
identical observation - the same `uname`, the same empty output, the same listing seen again -
which is a false pair and would only dilute the estimate), and take each row's 4 closest
surviving partners. The second moment B of those difference vectors is an EMA buffer. Every 50
steps after warmup, B is symmetrized, trace-normalized to unit mean diagonal, shrunk 5% toward
the identity and eigendecomposed on CPU in float64. The forward map is the symmetric power
B^(q/2), q = 1, with eigen-gains divided by their geometric mean and clamped to [0.4, 2.5]; the
inverse map is the same eigenbasis with reciprocal gains, so the pair is an exact algebraic
inverse rather than a numerical one (measured round-trip error 1.2e-7 in float64, 5.8e-7 through
the float32 buffers). The whole map is finally rescaled so trace(F S F^T)/D = 1 under the running
marginal second moment S, which keeps the transformed targets at unit average per-dimension
variance and therefore leaves the objective's absolute distance constants (_TEMP, _MARGIN,
_DELTA, the ring bandwidth) in the range they were tuned for.

WHY A POSITIVE POWER, AND WHY THIS MATRIX

The retrieval decision is flipped only by prediction error along the direction in which two
candidate observations actually differ. With pred = z_true + e and d = z_foil - z_true, the foil
wins exactly when <d,e> > ||d||^2/2, so the risk's derivative with respect to the error variance
in direction j is proportional to s_j^2, the variance of candidate differences in that direction.
Error is therefore worth buying down IN PROPORTION TO the candidate-difference variance, which
means a metric weight w_j proportional to s_j^2, i.e. a forward gain f_j proportional to s_j: the
POSITIVE square root of the candidate-difference scatter. B is a sample of exactly that scatter,
because the nearest non-duplicate neighbour of an observation in this embedding space is another
observation of the same shape - another `ls` listing, another `cat` of a similar file - which is
what the same-verb foil pool is made of.

This predicts the opposite tilt from the parent on one group of directions and the same tilt on
another, which is why it is not a sign flip of the parent. Directions that every candidate in a
window shares - the rendered `cwd=` prefix, the exit line, the verb's formatting signature - carry
large MARGINAL variance but near-zero pair scatter, so the parent suppresses them (large marginal
eigenvalue, negative power) and so do I (small pair eigenvalue, positive power). Directions of
per-observation jitter carry small marginal variance and small pair scatter, so the parent
AMPLIFIES them and I suppress them. Directions in which confusable listings genuinely differ from
each other carry moderate marginal variance and the largest pair scatter, so the parent suppresses
them relative to the jitter and I amplify them. My reading of why the parent's whitening earned
anything over identity is that it broke the domination of the between-verb directions, not that
inverse-marginal is the right metric; if that reading is right, the pair scatter breaks the same
domination and additionally puts the emphasis where the differential is decided.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca SPECIFICALLY

The transform cannot change the Bayes-optimal predictor - a target reparametrization that is
exactly inverted is a no-op at infinite capacity. It acts entirely through CAPACITY ALLOCATION,
and this arch has a hard bottleneck to allocate: 768-d embeddings are projected to d=192, and the
readout must reconstruct 768 dimensions from that rank. Which 192-dimensional subspace the readout
buys is decided by which directions dominate the loss. Under the identity target that subspace is
the top principal subspace of the observation distribution, which is rendering format and verb
identity - shared by every candidate in a cups window and therefore contributing exactly nothing
to native_hit - swap_stayed. Under my map the readout's rank is bid for by the directions along
which confusable observations differ, which is where a content's identity lives. The interaction
with the parent's objective is intended: r12 computes its confusability ring from TARGET-TARGET
distances, so in the new metric the ring's near/far band is measured in content-separating
coordinates rather than format coordinates, and the ring should select genuinely confusable
negatives more sharply.

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE (all unmeasured on the real harness - I cannot run it from the jail)

comp_ca above the parent's +0.0037, driven by native_hit rising while swap_stayed does not: the
mechanism carries no information about which command is being read and is one global matrix
applied identically to every window, every step and both arms, so a location-keying predictor
still cancels exactly per window and nothing here can manufacture a chain-position shortcut. I
expect the absolute pick rate against the analytic ceiling to move in the same direction, since
the mechanism is about resolving candidates rather than about the differential. If instead the
parent's gain really did come from inverse-marginal weighting per se, this loses to it and the
round learns that, which is the clean falsification.

RISKS, HONESTLY

(1) The dominant failure mode is that the nearest non-duplicate neighbour of an observation is not
a different content but the SAME content rendered from a different cwd, so the top of B is the
`cwd=` prefix direction and I amplify a location cue instead of a content cue. That would raise
native_hit and swap_stayed together and leave the differential flat.
(2) The dup filter is a relative threshold (2% of the mean pairwise distance); if the embedding
geometry makes genuinely distinct observations sit inside that radius, real pairs are discarded
and B narrows onto whatever is left.
(3) The positive power is the opposite direction from the parent on the jitter directions, so if
the parent's whitening was load-bearing for reasons other than the one I argue, this regresses.
Bounded by construction: the gain clamp caps the map's condition number at ~6.2 and the 5%
identity shrinkage floors the tail, so on power-law spectra with exponents 0.5 to 2.0 the gains
stay inside [0.65, 2.5] and only 1-7% of directions hit a clamp - the transform can tilt the
metric but cannot degenerate it.

COST AND SAFETY

Measured at real shapes on CPU: 3.8 ms per training step for the two EMA updates plus the
pairwise-distance pass at [1920, 768], and 43 ms per rebuild every 50 steps, so under 5 ms per
step amortized - negligible against a transformer step, and the fixed step budget is unaffected.
Every rebuild is guarded: non-finite input never reaches a buffer, a failed eigendecomposition or
a round-trip error above 1e-5 leaves the previous maps in place, and both maps are the identity
until warmup. The module introduces NO learnable parameters (five buffers, zero parameters), so
there is nothing that could sit outside the gradient path or off the scored branch; it does no
sequence indexing at all - it sees only the flattened [n, 768] rows the harness masks out - so
odd-length layouts and the absent final observation are structurally irrelevant to it. Verified by
direct execution: exact inverse round trip, unit target variance, correct behaviour on
duplicate-heavy batches, on an all-identical batch, on batches of 1/2/3/7/9/17 rows, on NaN input,
in eval mode, under no_grad, on 3-D input, on a wrong trailing dimension, and through a
state_dict round trip.
