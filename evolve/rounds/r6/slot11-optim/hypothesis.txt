CROSSOVER: parent g0-16-retrieval-composition (arch r22_retrieval_composition_renderer,
head r18_transition_forwardmodel_consistency) x partner (head r5_chainhop_hindsight_transport).
AXES CHANGED: optim (new impl), head (taken from the partner). Arch, objective, batcher, stream
and target are the parent's, verbatim.

THE MECHANISM (optim): isometric address geometry via Riemannian optimisation on a fixed-radius
Stiefel manifold.

Every store in this genome is an associative memory addressed by a key. The arch writes obs
content into a delta-rule store at k = unit(W_write x) and reads it back at q = unit(W_read x);
the latent-transition store addresses with unit(W_tr x); the quotient that removes the verb
subspace is spanned by the verb codebook. A read of that store returns sum_i <q, k_i> v_i, so the
content that comes back at a read is the content that was written there only if the address family
is incoherent. If W has a large condition number, whole directions of the command-feature space
are squashed to near-zero and distinct paths land on nearly the same address; the read then
returns a blend, and a blend is exactly what a chain of moves must not produce. Nothing in the
current training loop pushes W towards being well conditioned, and because every address is
unit-normalised downstream, the Frobenius scale of W is a pure nuisance direction that gradient
descent is free to spend steps on.

So: constrain the address matrices to be isometries up to a frozen scale, and optimise them on
that manifold rather than in the ambient space. Concretely, for a 2-D weight W (m x n, m <= n)
selected as an address projection, with s0 = ||W_init||_F / sqrt(m) fixed at construction:

  init      W <- polar(W) * s0                       (Frobenius norm exactly preserved,
                                                      only the singular spectrum is flattened)
  each step U   <- g + mu * buf                      (the parent's Muon momentum)
            U   <- U - sym(W_hat U^T) W_hat          (Stiefel tangent projection, W_hat = W/s0)
            O   <- NewtonSchulz_orth(U)              (the parent's Muon orthogonalisation)
            O   <- O - sym(W_hat O^T) W_hat          (re-project: orthogonalisation is not tangent)
            W   <- W - lr * rms * sqrt(max(m,n)) * O
            W   <- lerp(W, polar(W) * s0, tau)       (damped polar retraction back to radius s0)

polar() is a Newton-Schulz polar factor: four quintic Muon iterations followed by two cubic
1.5X - 0.5 X X^T X polish steps, which I verified numerically drives the condition number of a
random (64,176) init from ~3.7 to 1.0010 and holds it at ~1.00006 across optimiser steps while the
Frobenius norm is preserved to within 0.2%. This is Riemannian gradient descent with a polar
retraction (Absil/Mahony/Sepulchre; the same construction as Cayley-SGD for orthogonality
constraints in deep nets), imported into an optimiser that until now only orthogonalised the
UPDATE. Orthogonalising the update says nothing about the conditioning of the weight; this
constrains the weight itself.

WHY THE SELECTION RULE HAD TO CHANGE, AND WHY THAT IS THE CROSSOVER. The parent routes to Muon by
"output width == key_d AND at least two tensors share the shape" - a sibling rule that
structurally cannot see a lone address projection. The partner's head installs exactly such a
tensor: r5_chainhop's path_key is a single (48, 256) bias-free matrix through which BOTH the
source address and the destination address of the move-transport memory are encoded. It is the
single most compositional-depth-relevant matrix in the combined genome - if source and destination
addresses collapse, the head's erase-at-source wipes the destination it just wrote - and the
parent's optimiser would have left it to plain AdamW. The new rule is shape-family-free: a 2-D
weight whose narrow side is in [8, 96] and whose wide side is at least 2x the narrow side. On this
genome that selects exactly {file_read, file_write, path_read, path_write, tr_path, sys_val,
verb_codebook, path_key} and nothing else - verified by enumerating every parameter shape in
r22 + r5; every gate (output width 1), the read_mix router (width 3), ctx_proj (96x176, fails the
aspect test), all FFN/attention blocks and all D-wide readouts are excluded.

WHY THE HEAD MOVES WITH IT. The parent's head supervises the arch's one-step transition operator
f(obs_pre, cmd); it trains a forward model, not multi-hop routing. The partner's head is a
move-transport memory - read at source, write at destination, erase at source - with per-hop
hindsight supervision that trains the store's value AT ITS OWN DESTINATION ADDRESS at the
intermediate steps between an exposure and a re-read. That is a mechanism for the thing the metric
actually pays for: carrying one content's identity through a chain of moves. Its only defence
against source/destination collapse is a scalar role loss on cos(k_s, k_d); the isometry
constraint on the shared path_key is the geometric half of that same defence, and neither half is
available in the parent. The two axes need each other: the head supplies a store whose addresses
carry roles, the optim supplies the conditioning that keeps those roles separable.

SECONDARY, AND SUBORDINATE: weight decay is applied only to multi-dimensional weights, and 1-D
parameters that are exactly zero at construction and D long - the zero-init injection gains
(the head's out_scale, tr_read.bias, comp_out.bias) - form their own undecayed group at 2x the
base learning rate. These are the channels through which any retrieved content reaches the
prediction at all; under a fixed step budget an Adam step moves them at most ~lr per step from
exactly zero, so they are the slowest thing in the model to wake up. The (D,D) soft spectral cap
from the parent is kept unchanged (it still binds on tr_read).

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE. comp_ca above the parent's +0.0037, driven by the swapped arm: a
better-conditioned address family should reduce blended reads, so the native chain should more
often return the content actually routed there while the role-swapped chain returns the partner's
content - the difference, not the level, is what should move. If instead the model is currently
keying on chain position, this change should be roughly neutral, because address conditioning
gives no advantage to a first-mover lookup: the retraction is content-agnostic and applies
identically to both arms. A neutral result with an unchanged shortcut_leaning reading would say
address collision is not the binding constraint here, which is itself the useful negative.

FALSIFIABLE / HOW IT COULD BE WRONG. (1) Freezing the Frobenius radius of the selected matrices
removes a degree of freedom. For file/path/tr/path_key that is free (unit-normalised downstream)
and for verb_codebook it is free (Gram-Schmidt normalises each row anyway), but sys_val's output
goes through a tanh with no downstream renormalisation, so its input gain is pinned at init - if
the model needed to sharpen or soften that saturation, it can now only do so through sysfilm.
(2) The isometry could be actively wrong: if the right solution is for many distinct commands to
map to ONE address (all reads of the same path, whatever the verb), a well-conditioned projection
forces that decision up into the trunk instead of letting W collapse. I claim the trunk has more
context and the quotient already removes the verb subspace, but that is an argument, not a
measurement. (3) Swapping the head drops the transition-operator supervision that r22's renderer
was co-designed with; the arch still runs (r5 wraps forward and is arch-agnostic) but the
transition path loses its aux teacher.

PERFORMANCE, UNMEASURED ON THE REAL LANE. Eight address matrices, each step costing one
Newton-Schulz orthogonalisation (as the parent already paid on six) plus two m x m tangent
projections and a six-iteration polar retraction. On CPU with synthetic gradients over a mock of
this genome's exact parameter shapes the whole optimiser step went from 13.6 ms to 15.0 ms, ~+10%
of optimiser time; against an arch that runs a Python loop over n_cmd with bmm plus the head's
own rollout loop this should be a small fraction of a training step, but I have not run the real
harness and do not claim a measured step time.

CORRECTNESS NOTES. The impl introduces no learnable parameters and no branch on sequence layout,
so it is indifferent to the odd-length scored layout [cmd_0, obs_0, ..., cmd_r]; it never sees a
sequence. Every parameter it touches is an arch or head parameter that already receives gradient
from the training loss. It exposes make(params, steps, **p) -> (optimizer, scheduler) exactly, the
optimizer accepts zero_grad(set_to_none=True) and step(), and the scheduler exposes step() and
get_last_lr(). Genome params are left empty so all defaults ride. Falls back to plain
AdamW + LambdaLR when no matrix matches the address signature, and to a no-op cap when no (D,D)
readout exists. Non-finite gradients are skipped and every Newton-Schulz output is nan_to_num'd
before it is applied. Verified by direct execution: address selection on a mock with r22 + r5's
exact shapes, condition numbers, Frobenius preservation, the tall-matrix orientation branch, the
no-address fallback, and the spectral cap engaging.
