WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH SIDE

From the partner's objective (r22): the exact-target-equivalence quotient. Numerically equal target
rows are one multi-positive class, and every occurrence mass (anchor, candidate, pair, focal) is
divided by class size. This matters mechanically for this data: every `mv` step's observation
renders to the single string "cwd=/ exit=0\n", so a batch contains a large block of bit-identical
targets. Without the quotient that block is both a false-negative source and a mass distortion in
every batch statistic. Verified against evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl: mutating commands
(`mv`, `cat a > b`, `cat a >> b`) carry exit 0 and an empty `output`, so their rendered observation
is identical across the whole split.

From the parent's objective (r12): the band-pass confusability ring over close-but-distinct targets
as the source of importance-weighted negatives, and a repulsion hinge expressed in the eval's own
squared-L2 decision variable rather than in a cosine or an inner-product surrogate.

From the parent's head: the dual-address delta-rule transport memory (r2). From the partner's
stream: the role-bound path-atom codes (r4). Those two are chosen together on purpose. The r2 head
has to derive a source key and a destination key from a single command embedding through two
extractors and one shared path-to-key encoder; the r4 stream writes, into the last 128 coordinates
of that same command token, a role-bound sign code that is literally (role_src * hash(src_path) +
hash(src_path)) + (role_dst * hash(dst_path) + hash(dst_path)). The head's src/dst extractors then
have a linearly separable handle on exactly the two quantities they exist to recover, instead of
having to disentangle two paths out of a mean-pooled sentence embedding. The codes are computed
per command string in isolation, so they stay prefix-causal and identical across the native and the
role-swapped arm for every command that is not one of the swapped moves.

THE NEW MECHANISM (what neither parent computes)

Both parents shape their contrastive geometry from PREDICTION ERROR: a per-dimension precision
weight w_d = (1/(mse_d + eps))^0.5, clamped to [0.25, 4] and mean-normalised, is applied to pred and
to every candidate target before distances are formed. In the standardized latent space a dimension
the model cannot yet predict has mse_d near 1 and a dimension it has already learned has mse_d near
0.05, so that weight hands up to 4x to already-learned coordinates and relatively suppresses the
high-error coordinates. The coordinates that carry which canary content is in a file are exactly
the high-error ones. The parents therefore de-emphasise, inside the contrastive term, the
coordinates the measurement is decided on.

What the measurement is decided on is an identity, not a guess. For a prediction p, a true target
t_i and a foil t_j,

    ||p - t_j||^2 - ||p - t_i||^2  =  ||t_i - t_j||^2 + 2 * e_i . (t_i - t_j),   e_i = p - t_i

(verified symbolically and numerically). The rank decision therefore depends on the error ONLY
through its projection on the candidate-difference direction (t_i - t_j), and on nothing else. Two
things follow, and this candidate implements both.

(1) DECISION-DIRECTION METRIC. Replace the inverse-error precision weight with the ring-weighted
second moment of the unit candidate-difference directions:

    v_d  =  sum_{i,j} r_ij * (t_id - t_jd)^2 / ||t_i - t_j||^2 ,    w_d ∝ (v_d / mean_d v_d)^0.75

with r_ij the (class-measured) confusability ring, clamped to [0.4, 3.0] and renormalised to mean 1.
Because sum_d (t_id - t_jd)^2 / ||t_i - t_j||^2 = 1 exactly, v is a probability-like distribution of
the decision directions over coordinates: w_d asks "how much of the geometry that separates
confusable observations lives in coordinate d". The identity above says the loss should spend its
gradient there. This is computed with two matmuls of the size the parents already pay for; the
algebraic form (2 * [sum_i rowsum_i t_id^2 - sum_ij r_ij t_id t_jd]) was checked numerically against
the direct double sum.

(2) SCALE-FREE PAIRWISE DECISION HINGE. The parents' repulsion compares the true distance to a
ring-mean distance with a fixed absolute margin, in the precision-weighted metric. This candidate
forms, per confusable pair and in the RAW unweighted metric the eval ranks in, the normalised
decision quantity

    slack_ij = (||p_i - t_j||^2 - ||p_i - t_i||^2) / ||t_i - t_j||^2 = 1 + 2 * e_i.(t_i - t_j) / g_ij

built algebraically from e_i . t_i and e_i . t_j (no extra distance matrix), and penalises
softplus((0.5 - slack_ij)/0.2), aggregated across a row's ring by a temperature-0.5 weighted
log-sum-exp so the HARDEST confusable partner dominates instead of the ring average. Normalising by
the pair's own gap is the point: with an absolute margin, far pairs are satisfied for free and near
pairs are unreachable, so the term stops discriminating exactly where discrimination is decided.
The soft maximum is the standard smooth surrogate for the worst-case constraint, and the penalty is
bounded below by 0 because the aggregation weights sum to one and softplus is non-negative.

Consequently every scale in the loss (equivalence threshold, ring width, duplicate suppression,
softmax temperature, gap floor) is now a fraction of the class-measured mean off-diagonal target
distance rather than an absolute constant — required, because changing the coordinate metric changes
the absolute scale of every distance in the batch and the parents' fixed _TEMP = 0.25 / _MARGIN =
0.5 would no longer mean what they meant.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed on the same board, and a window is won only when the
prediction at the withheld read lands nearer to the content the chain actually routed there than to
the other contents on that board. Those competitors are same-format files differing in a canary
hash: their targets are close in the encoder's geometry, and the coordinates that separate them are
a minority of the 768 that the model predicts worst. A loss whose contrastive metric is driven by
prediction error puts its contrastive gradient on the majority of coordinates that all board
candidates agree on, where by the identity above the gradient cannot change any decision. Both
mechanisms here move gradient onto the coordinates and the pairs where the decision is actually
made, without altering what is fed to the model. A model that improves this way must improve
native_hit; it cannot inflate swap_stayed, because nothing in the loss references the move order,
the read location, or any resolved routing — the loss sees only pred, tgt and the batch's own target
geometry.

Nothing here is computed at scoring time. The commands are never parsed, no move chain is resolved
anywhere in my code, and no resolved routing is placed in the model's input; the whole mechanism is
a reweighting of a training loss over (pred, tgt) and disappears after training. That also settles
the withheld-observation hazard: this objective is not called at the scored position at all, and it
has no branch that consults whether an observation is present, so there is nothing here that can
switch itself off exactly where the candidate is judged. WANTS_CTX is deliberately not set — no part
of the mechanism needs the command embedding or the previous observation.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL

- If the coordinates with high ring-direction second moment are encoder NOISE coordinates (dimensions
  where the frozen encoder is unstable across near-identical texts) rather than content coordinates,
  then w_d aims the gradient at noise and both the absolute fit and comp_ca get worse. The [0.4, 3.0]
  clamp and the retained raw squared-error anchor bound how far this can go, but do not remove it.
- If the decision directions are close to isotropic over the 768 coordinates, v_d is flat, w_d is
  ~1, and mechanism (1) is a no-op; the candidate then rests on the hinge alone and probably lands
  within noise of the parent. That is the benign failure and it is detectable: the loss reduces to
  the quotient contrastive with a different repulsion.
- The gap-normalised hinge gives near pairs a gradient proportional to 1/g_ij. If the duplicate
  band-pass and the gap floor (10% of the mean gap) do not clip the smallest gaps hard enough,
  a few pairs could dominate the gradient and destabilise training; the harness's grad-norm clip at
  1.0 is the last line, and instability would show as a degraded health reading, not as a silent win.
- The r4 stream is a real added risk on top of the objective: if the delta-rule head's advantage
  came from something the hashed path codes disturb (they overwrite the last 128 of 768 embedding
  coordinates of every command token), the head could get worse even though the objective is better,
  and the two effects are not separable from this one candidate's score alone.
- Cost: the loss adds two n-by-n-by-D matmuls and roughly five extra n-by-n float32 buffers over the
  parent at n ≈ batch_size * steps_per_sequence. This is the same order the parent already pays and
  is expected to be a small fraction of a training step, but I could not run it here — no torch in
  the jail — so treat that as unmeasured rather than as a profiled figure.
