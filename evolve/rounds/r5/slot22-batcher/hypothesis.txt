CANDIDATE: batcher = r5_readaddr_collision_blocks_depth_strata
AXES CHANGED: batcher only. Everything else is the parent genome verbatim.

WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH PROGRAM

From the parent (r25_routing_depth_systematic_curriculum): the idea that the COMMAND TEXT of a
trajectory is a routing graph you can parse before training, and the Madow systematic
probability-proportional-to-size draw over a depth-sorted, randomly tie-broken frame, which makes
the batch's depth composition hit its target with near-zero step-to-step variance instead of the
multinomial jitter an iid draw gives. Both survive here: `_systematic` is that draw, and every
part of the batch is filled through it.

From the crossover partner (r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum): the block idea — a ramped share of the
batch drawn from ONE system image, so the in-batch negative pool is same-system rather than a
spread over eight unrelated filesystems. That survives here as the `n_sys` tier, drawn exactly the
partner's way (multinomial over image pools by size, then fill).

The child is not the union of the two; the new tier sits between them and is the point of the
candidate.

WHAT IS NEW, MECHANICALLY

1. The parse targets a different quantity. The parent scores a sequence by the longest chain
ANYWHERE in its command text. I checked that against the ground truth in
evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl: each trajectory carries exactly one deep routed read, and its
own meta records the depth of the content that read actually returns. The parent's max-over-board
number and that depth disagree badly on real sequences (one sample: max chain 6, depth of the
supervised read 2), because the deepest chain in a trajectory usually belongs to a content that is
never read. `_deepest_routed_read` instead walks BACKWARDS from every `cat P` through the mv/cp and
redirection writes that landed on P earlier in the same trajectory, and returns the hop count of
the deepest read, the address that read names, and the path that content started at. On the 24
verbatim training sequences in this jail that hop count equals the recorded depth of the supervised
read on 23; the single disagreement is a redirection chain that is genuinely 2 hops deep and is not
the cups read. So the depth this batcher tilts on is the depth of the tracking problem the
sequence actually supervises.

2. Read-address collision blocks. A sequence whose deepest routed read is at least `deep_min` hops
is keyed by that read's address with the trailing move index stripped
(/tmp/w/cups/usr/share/misc/magic.txt.8 -> /tmp/w/cups/usr/share/misc/magic.txt). A ramped
`addr_share` of the batch is filled from a few of those keys. Every row inside such a key poses a
near-identical read command — same directory, same base name, only the final move index differs —
with a DIFFERENT answer, because the boards are different trajectories. Neither parent composes
this: the parent's image block puts same-system sequences together, but their test reads name
different files, so the command still predicts the answer inside the block.

3. Origin-family balance inside a block. Each keyed sequence also carries a CROSSED flag: true when
the name family of the path the content started at differs from the name family of the address it
is read from. In the training sample that flag reproduces the generator's own routed-vs-name index
comparison exactly wherever a cups read is the deepest read. A key's slots are split between its
crossed and its aligned members at `cross_frac`, so inside a block the read address carries no
information about whether the answer is the content that originally lived under that name.

4. Explicit two-stratum allocation on hop count for the unblocked remainder: the share of the batch
whose supervised read is at least `deep_min` hops ramps from the corpus rate to `deep_frac_max`,
rather than being left to a power tilt whose realised composition depends on the corpus depth
histogram. The power tilt is kept INSIDE each stratum so 6 hops still outranks 3.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY

The scored quantity is native_hit minus swap_stayed: it is positive only for a model whose answer
CHANGES when the same board is re-chained so a different content lands at the read location. Two
strategies score exactly zero by construction — answering from the read address, and answering from
the name family of the address — and both are cheap for the model to learn under the current
training distribution, because the objective's in-batch negatives almost never contain two rows
that share a read address and disagree about the answer. In a uniform or image-blocked batch, the
command at a routed-read row is very nearly a unique key for its target, so minimising the listwise
contrastive loss does not require the trajectory at all. The collision tier removes that: within a
key, the command is constant to within one digit and the targets differ, so any predictor that is a
function of the command alone puts the same point on several rows with different labels and pays
for every one of them; the only remaining way to lower the loss on those rows is to read the move
chain. The crossed/aligned balance removes the second zero-scoring strategy the same way. The
corrected depth parse then concentrates that pressure on the depth band the metric is eligible on
(3 hops and deeper), which the parent's max-over-board score does not reliably do.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL

- If the training split's routed reads land on mostly unique addresses, address pools stay below
  `min_block`, the collision tier is empty and the candidate degrades to the partner's image
  blocking plus a corrected depth stratification — a small change, likely inside the noise. The
  sample says roughly two thirds of deep routed reads land in a small recurring pool of core file
  names shared across images, which is where the pools come from; if the real split is dominated by
  the per-sequence random f####.ext names instead, this fails.
- If a key's pool is small, the block repeats a few sequences. Exact duplicate rows are harmless
  here (the objective's ring gives near-identical targets ~0 negative weight) but they consume
  slots, so a corpus with pools of 2-3 buys little.
- If the model's failure is not shortcut-taking but capacity — if it already ignores the command
  and simply cannot carry identity through 4 hops — then removing the shortcut changes nothing and
  may cost the general fit that the shallow and non-routing rows were providing.
- If `deep_frac_max` starves the model of the shallow and non-routing supervision it needs to
  ground observation prediction at all, the trunk gets worse everywhere and the differential falls.

WHAT THIS DELIBERATELY DOES NOT DO

Nothing parsed here reaches the model. The parse produces three per-sequence facts — a hop count, a
read address string, a boolean — and all three are consumed by the sampler to choose INDICES. No
tensor handed to collate, the loss or the head depends on them, and the batcher is never called at
scoring time. It also reads no observation, no exit code and no target: `_deepest_routed_read`
takes only `s["cmds"]`, so there is nothing in this mechanism that could switch off at the scored
position, where the observation is withheld.

I did not run this against the real harness — there is no torch in the jail. What I did check: the
parse against the recorded ground truth on all 24 verbatim training sequences, and the sampler
itself under a stand-in tensor library for exact batch length, index bounds, determinism given the
seed, seed sensitivity, and the degenerate corpora (one sequence, one image, no mv/redirection at
all, empty command lists). No performance claim is measured.

ON THE OTHER AXES

I kept the parent's arch, head, objective, optim, target and stream unchanged. The partner's
distinguishing pieces are on arch and head, and neither is checkable from inside this jail — the
partner's trunk and the parent's trunk are different families, and the parent's arch builds on a
base module that is not present here, so I cannot tell whether the parent's head aux is even active
against it. Swapping a trunk blind is a coin flip large enough to swamp the batcher effect in both
directions, so the crossover is carried on the axis where I could verify both parents' mechanisms
against the data.
