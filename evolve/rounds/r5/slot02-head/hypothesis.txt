CANDIDATE: head = r5_routed_read_address_binding   (axes changed: head only)

WHAT I READ IN THE DATA FIRST
-----------------------------
evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl, row 1 (alpine:latest, 26 steps). A cups window is laid out
as: N `cat <path_i>` exposures whose observation IS the content; then `mv <src> <dst>` steps whose
observation is EMPTY (`output: ""`, exit 0) so that render_obs gives literally "cwd=/ exit=0\n";
then one `cat <final_loc>` test whose observation is the content that the chain routed there. The
cvar arm has the same shape with `cat s > out.dat` redirections instead of mv. Two facts follow
that the whole design rests on:

  (1) Every mutating step's observation embedding is the SAME vector (empty output, same cwd), so
      mutating steps are the steps whose observation is a near-duplicate of many others. In that
      row 8 of 26 steps are mutating (cluster size 7); every real content appears at most 3 times
      (cluster size <= 2). A cluster-size threshold separates them with margin.
  (2) A routed read is a step whose observation duplicates an EARLIER step's observation while its
      COMMAND differs, with at least one mutating step in between. `cat /tmp/w/cups/g53/f149.tmp`
      (expose) -> `mv /tmp/w/cups/g53/f149.tmp /tmp/w/cups/usr/share/misc/magic.txt.1` ->
      `cat /tmp/w/cups/usr/share/misc/magic.txt.1` (test) is exactly that pattern, and so is
      `cat g3/s1.dat` -> `cat g3/s1.dat > g3/out.dat` -> `cat g3/out.dat`. A same-command re-read
      (`cat g0/s1.dat` twice) is excluded by the command-difference test, which is the point: that
      one is solvable by name lookup and is not what is scored.

Everything my aux uses is mined from the batch's own embeddings by these two tests. Nothing parses
a command, nothing resolves a chain in Python, and nothing is added to the model's input at any
time. The mined indices only choose WHICH training positions get a loss and what that loss's
endpoint is.

WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH PROGRAM
------------------------------------------
The two programs I was given share the same head impl, so the crossover has to be expressed
between their distinctive arch-side mechanisms, lifted into one arch-independent head:

  from the parent (r23_dual_address_transport_pointer): the DUAL ADDRESS - one shared nonlinear
  layer over a command's raw 768-d embedding producing two separate unit keys, a SOURCE address
  read from and a DESTINATION address written to, with a delta-rule content store.

  from the partner (r18_pathstate_latent_transition_worldmodel): the LATENT TRANSITION - the value
  written back is not a raw copy but a command-conditioned affine edit of the content just read,
  s_pre * (1 + tanh(gamma(cmd)) * gscale) + beta(cmd), the same FiLM form as that arch's
  `_transition`.

In `_AddressTransportStore.run` these are one recurrence: read s_i at a_src[i], form
edited = s_i*(1+gamma_i)+shift_i, write move_i*edited + (1-move_i)*obs_i at a_dst[i] under the
delta rule (v - current) with a decay, inject sigmoid(read_gate)*out_proj(read) into the arch's
command-position prediction. out_proj is zero-initialised in both weight and bias, so the wrapped
net's forward output is bit-identical to the unwrapped net's at step 0.

WHAT IS NEW - THE MECHANISM NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES
---------------------------------------------------
Both parents' head aux supervises CONTENT: it asks a transition operator to reconstruct a future
observation (cosine + MSE). Nothing anywhere in either program supervises ADDRESSING. That is the
gap I am attacking, and here is why I think it is the binding one. The command embeddings are
frozen mean-pooled encoder vectors. emb("cat p") and emb("mv p q") are two very differently shaped
pooled vectors, and mean pooling largely destroys the order of p and q inside "mv p q". For a
transport memory to work at all, the network must learn a nonlinear map that pulls the FIRST path
out of a two-path command into a key that coincides with the key it builds from the single-path
command naming that same path, and the SECOND path into a key coinciding with the destination
read's key. Under MSE-to-next-observation (and under a contrastive objective on content) there is
no term that says so. The address heads exist and are never told what to mean.

The new term says it, weakly supervised, endpoint-only. For each mined pair (i = the earlier step
whose observation is the content, j = the routed read), the loss runs a soft state-tracking
recurrence over the intervening steps IN ADDRESS SPACE:

    f <- a_dst[i]                                      (where i's content was stored)
    for t = i+1 .. j-1, over valid steps:
        g_t = sigmoid( ( <a_src[t], f> - thr ) / gate_temp )     (does step t pick up what f holds?)
        f   <- unit( (1 - g_t) * f + g_t * a_dst[t] )            (if so, f moves to t's destination)
    loss_fwd = cross_entropy( <a_src[.], f> / addr_temp , target = j )

and the mirror recurrence backwards from a_src[j] toward a_dst[i]. The candidate set of the
cross-entropy is every valid step's address in the same row, so the negatives are the other
locations that window actually touched.

Three properties I want a reviewer to check in the code:

 * It is depth-correct. The recurrence composes an arbitrary number of hops, so a depth-3 or
   depth-4 chain p->q->r->s is satisfied by opening the gate at each of the three moves in turn.
   A single-hop "which mutation moved it" formulation would be wrong here - it would drag the
   address of an intermediate location onto the address of the final one - and I rejected that
   version for exactly this reason. The eligible slice is reported as d3 60 / d4+ 29, so
   single-hop supervision would have been supervising the wrong thing on almost every window.

 * The degenerate solution is structurally penalised, not just discouraged. The lazy way to
   satisfy loss_fwd is to leave the gates shut and instead make a_src[j] equal a_dst[i]. But the
   cross-entropy's candidate set includes position i itself, and the separate agreement term
   (self_addr_weight) pulls a_src[i] toward a_dst[i] on every contentful step, so under the lazy
   solution the logits at i and at j are equal and the loss floors at log 2. Beating that floor
   requires f to actually move away from a_dst[i], and the only parameterised route for f to move
   is through g_t, which in turn requires <a_src[t], a_dst[i]> to grow for the step t that really
   is the move. That is the gradient I want.

 * Steps between i and j that are not moves are harmless. A read command has a_src == a_dst (the
   agreement term), so if its gate opens the update f <- unit((1-g) f + g a_dst[t]) is a no-op.
   That is why the recurrence ranges over all valid intervening steps rather than over the
   mutation-classified ones - the classifier is used only to decide that a pair is routed at all
   (n_mut >= 1), never to build the chain.

Alongside it, on the same mined positions, a within-sequence squared-L2 InfoNCE in exactly the
eval's geometry: logits = -||q - cand||^2 / (D * cand_temp) over the contentful observations seen
so far in that row, positives = the duplicate set of the answer. It is applied to the arch's final
prediction (pred_weight) and to the store's read (read_weight), with a small MSE anchor. Examples
are weighted by the number of mutating steps between i and j, so deeper routings dominate the
gradient. This is the second thing neither parent has: the training loss is MSE to a 768-d
observation, in which the handful of directions that distinguish content A from content B is a
negligible share of the variance, while the score is a top-1 discrimination among the other
contents on the same board. The InfoNCE turns the loss at precisely the scored kind of position
into that discrimination.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY
------------------------------------------
comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed on the same board with the same destinations and the same
depth, differing only in which source path sits in which move command. A model that answers from
the name being read is exactly zero. A model that answers from move ORDER cancels only in
expectation. The only thing that survives both arms is a source-path-to-content binding carried
through the chain. Both terms above act on that binding and on nothing else: the address loss
teaches the source/destination decomposition of a move command to agree with the addresses of the
single-path commands naming those paths, and the contrastive loss makes the retrieved content
discriminable from the co-resident contents. Neither term can be satisfied by reading the name at
the query position, because the query position's own address is the same in both arms.

WHERE IT IS MEASURED, AND WHY IT IS NOT SWITCHED OFF THERE
----------------------------------------------------------
The scored position is a command whose observation is withheld. Nothing in the forward path is
gated on an observation being present at the position being predicted. In the store's recurrence
the read at step i is taken BEFORE step i's write, so read_i depends only on obs_0..obs_{i-1}; the
move/store gates and `live` consume obs_i but affect only the write at i, hence only reads at
i+1 and later. The injected contribution at step i is sigmoid(read_gate(cmd_feat_i)) *
out_proj(read_i) - both factors are functions of the command embedding and of strictly earlier
observations. This is also what makes the stream's leakage probe pass: corrupting obs_3 cannot
change the prediction at command positions 0..3. The store runs with n_cmd = (L+1)//2 and
n_pair = L//2 and pads the observation side with zeros, so an odd-length stream ending on a
command is handled without the last position needing an observation. The aux loss is train-time
only; wrap registers the module on the net before the optimizer is built, holds the base net only
through the saved bound `orig_forward` in a closure (never as a submodule, so `.to(device)` cannot
recurse), and re-points net.forward once, guarded against double-wrapping.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL
-----------------------------------------
 1. If a training row contains fewer than max_dup+1 = 4 mutating steps, their observation cluster
    is too small to cross the threshold and no pair is mined in that row. The row I read has 8, but
    I have read one row. If most rows in the composed blend carry only two or three mv steps, the
    mined set shrinks and the aux degenerates toward zero - a silent no-op, not a corruption.
 2. If the frozen pooled embedding of "mv p q" genuinely carries no recoverable ordering between p
    and q, no loss on the address heads can create it, and the address term will settle at a
    partial optimum where a_src and a_dst of a move command both point at a mixture of the two
    locations. The chain recurrence would then track a blurred address and the endpoint CE would
    plateau near its lazy-solution floor.
 3. The InfoNCE could be satisfied by a positional shortcut - "answer with the content of the
    slot that moved first" - which does not cancel across the two arms and would inflate the score
    without the capability. I cannot prevent a trained model from finding it; I can only note that
    the address term supplies a competing solution that is a strictly better fit to the mined
    endpoints across windows with different move orders, and that the reported analytic band is
    what this reading has to be checked against.
 4. Two transport stores now inject into the prediction (the arch's and the head's) with a total of
    roughly 1.1M extra head parameters and an extra sequential recurrence per step. If the fixed
    step budget is tight, the added optimisation load could cost more than the added signal buys.
    I could not run anything from this jail, so this is unmeasured.

INTERACTION WITH THE OPTIM I INHERITED
--------------------------------------
r18_spectral_capped_transition_readout routes to Muon any 2-D parameter of shape [key_d, x] with
x not in {key_d, D} that has an identical-shape sibling. I set key_d = 64 (its default) and
addr_hidden = 256 deliberately, so addr_src.weight and addr_dst.weight are [64, 256] siblings and
receive the orthogonalised addressing update the optim was written for; out_proj.weight is [768,
768] and falls under the spectral cap, which is a no-op at its zero initialisation (the power
iteration on a zero matrix yields sigma = 0, below the cap).
