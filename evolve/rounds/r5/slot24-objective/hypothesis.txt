WHAT THE CROSSOVER IS

The parent program's objective (r3_fwl_orthogonalized_ring_contrast) is a BATCH-level metric: every
row's negatives are the other ~2000 rows of the minibatch, reweighted by a confusability ring and
geometrically orthogonalized against the command embedding. The second program's distinctive
mechanism is not its objective (r12 is a strict ancestor of the parent's objective — same ring, same
focal listwise, same repulsion hinge, minus the orthogonalization and the duplicate quotient); its
distinctive mechanism lives in its head, r2_dualaddress_move_transport, which mines — label-free,
from the batch alone — steps whose observation exactly duplicates an EARLIER step's observation
under a different command with at least one content-free step in between, and trains a
WITHIN-SEQUENCE, PREFIX-RESTRICTED, multi-positive InfoNCE against the observations seen so far.

This candidate keeps the parent's batch geometry verbatim and lifts the second program's mining rule
and prefix-restricted discrimination OUT of the head and INTO the objective, where it supervises the
model's actual scored prediction rather than a head-private memory read, and adds a route-depth
weighting that neither program has. The arch is kept at the parent's r23, which already inherits the
second program's r22 prefix-content cross-attention and already carries a dual-address transport
memory natively — so taking the second program's head as well would instantiate a redundant second
transport memory and a duplicate of the aux this candidate now expresses in the loss. Every other
axis is the parent's.

WHAT THE NEW TERM COMPUTES, AND HOW

Everything the new term needs it derives from the three matrices the contract already hands it —
pred, tgt and ctx["prev"] — with no side channel and no command parsing.

1. SEGMENTATION. The harness flattens cmd rows in row-major (sequence, step) order and builds
   prev = strict-causal shift of tgt, zero-filled at each sequence's first step. So within a
   trajectory prev[r] is bit-identical to tgt[r-1], and at a trajectory boundary prev[r] is exactly
   the zero vector. _segment_ids marks a boundary where prev[r] differs from tgt[r-1] beyond 1e-5 OR
   prev[r] is all-zero, and cumsums. I checked this reconstruction against the real training sample:
   rebuilding a 6-sequence batch from evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl and running the rule
   recovers the true sequence ids exactly. Failure is safe in both directions: a missed boundary only
   adds cross-sequence negatives, a false boundary only shortens a prefix. With a non-identity target
   module the equality prev[r] == tgt[r-1] breaks, every row becomes its own segment, and the term
   switches itself off rather than misfiring; the genome pins target=identity.

2. CONTENT IDENTITY BY EXACT TARGET EQUALITY. render_obs is "cwd=... exit=...\n" + output, so the
   observation text of a read does NOT contain the path read. Reading the same file content from a
   different location after a chain of moves therefore produces a bit-identical observation string
   and, through the frozen encoder and the fixed standardization, a bit-identical target vector.
   Inside a segment, exact equality classes of tgt ARE content identities. Confirmed on the sample:
   in each trajectory the class-size profile is one class of 9-14 (every filesystem-mutating command
   shares the empty-output observation) and everything else of size 1-3, with each size-2/3 class
   being one content exposed and then re-read after its moves. The term uses a strict threshold
   (1e-5 of the batch mean target distance, floored at 1e-6) — 100x tighter than the parent's
   duplicate threshold, which exists to suppress false negatives and would pool distinct-but-similar
   contents that here must stay distinguishable.

3. MUTATION VS CONTENT, AND ROUTE DEPTH. In-segment class size >= 4 marks a mutating step, <= 3
   marks a contentful step (9-14 vs 1-3 in the sample, so the threshold sits in a wide gap). A row is
   an ANCHOR when it is contentful, its target repeats an earlier contentful row in the same segment,
   and at least one mutating row lies strictly between them; its ROUTE DEPTH is the count of mutating
   rows in that gap, computed as a difference of a global cumsum (valid because the earlier
   occurrence is in the same segment). I re-implemented the exact tensor algebra of _prefix_route_term
   in plain Python and checked it against a straightforward per-sequence ground truth on the sample:
   the anchor set and every depth match exactly. Anchors are 8-11% of rows, mean prefix candidate set
   11.6, depths spread over 1..12.

4. THE LOSS. Each anchor is scored by a multi-positive listwise softmax of -d2/temp over the targets
   of every contentful row at or before it in its own segment (positives = every candidate with a
   numerically equal target, which always includes itself). Distances are plain precision-weighted
   squared L2 on the RAW targets — the eval's own decision variable — not the orthogonalized
   residuals term A uses, so that two occurrences of one content are literally the same point and the
   positive class is exactly a content identity. The temperature is 0.15 of the mean anchor-to-
   negative target distance, so the softmax sharpness is scale-free. Anchor mass is
   1 + 3*(1 - exp(-depth/2)), normalized to a weighted mean. Total loss = parent's loss (unchanged,
   term for term) + 2.0 * this.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY

The measurement is a forced choice among the contents on one board, at a read whose answer depends on
where a silent chain of moves put them. Two things about the parent's loss are wrong for that.

First, mass. The rows that carry the capability — a content read back after it was moved — are ~10%
of rows, and the parent's exact-duplicate quotient actively DOWN-WEIGHTS them: a carried read is by
construction in a duplicate class with its own earlier exposure, so inv_class gives it 1/2 while a
boring unique read gets 1/1. That mechanism is correct for its purpose (dedup across images) and
backwards for this one. The new term restores those rows to essentially all of its mass and tilts
further toward the deep ones, which is where the eligible slice lives (d3 and d4+).

Second, and more important, WHICH negatives. The parent's negatives are drawn from the whole batch
and reweighted by target-space proximity; nothing guarantees that the specific content a
location-keyed predictor would wrongly return is among them. The prefix candidate set guarantees it
structurally. Concretely, in a real training window: s1.dat is exposed with content A, s0.dat is
exposed with content B, `mv s0.dat s1.dat` runs silently, and s1.dat is read. The prefix set at that
read contains BOTH A and B. A predictor that keys on the location returns A; the listwise term
charges it exactly for that, against the same board, under the same read command. That is the same
contrast the score takes — and a model that answers by name rather than by routing is penalized
per-anchor, every step, instead of being competed against whatever the batch happened to supply.

I expect the effect on comp_ca to be through native_hit rising while swap_stayed does not, because
nothing in the term rewards any function of the read location alone: for a location-keyed predictor
the term's gradient is maximal, and such a predictor is exactly the one whose per-window difference
is structurally zero.

CHECKS AGAINST THE KNOWN TRAPS

- Nothing resolved is put into the model's input. The term reads pred, tgt and prev, and only shapes
  a loss; the model's tokens, the arch, the stream and the eval path are untouched.
- The term never runs where the scored observation is withheld. It is computed inside loss() during
  training only; at scoring time the loss is not called at all, so there is no mechanism that
  switches off at the measured position. The one thing it consumes from ctx, prev, is used only to
  cut the flat rows into trajectories during training.
- Anti-collapse: the parent's untouched raw squared-error anchor is retained, and a constant
  prediction gives every anchor an equal distance to every candidate, i.e. nll = log(|cand|/|pos|)
  > 0, so a constant is not a minimizer of the new term either.
- Causality of the candidate set: candidates are restricted to rows at or before the anchor in its
  own segment, so the term only ever asks for a content the model has already been shown.
- Cost: one extra [n_anchor, D] x [D, n] matmul (n_anchor is ~10% of n) plus a handful of [n,n]
  boolean reductions, against a batch that already pays an [n,n] distance matrix and a 1024x1024
  ridge solve. It should not threaten the step budget, though I could not run the harness from this
  jail and have not measured wall-clock.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

- If the class-size split does not separate on the full training root the way it does on the sample
  (a trajectory with fewer than 4 mutating steps, or a content read 4+ times), rows get misclassified.
  Both directions degrade to "fewer or trivially-easy anchors", not to a wrong training signal.
- If the model can satisfy the prefix term with a positional proxy — e.g. "answer with the most
  recently exposed content" — it would gain on the term without gaining routing. Such a proxy is
  move-order-blind and so contributes zero to the score rather than inflating it, but it would also
  mean the term bought nothing. The depth weighting is the defence: at depth>=2 the recency proxy is
  wrong most of the time in the training data.
- If a weight of 2.0 is too large, the general next-observation fit degrades enough that native_hit
  falls faster than swap_stayed. This is the main tuning risk and it is a one-constant risk.
- If the encoder does not in fact produce bit-identical embeddings for bit-identical observation text
  (it is a frozen deterministic encoder run once per split and cached, so it should), the equality
  classes fragment and the anchor set empties — the term then contributes nothing and the candidate
  reduces to its parent.
