CANDIDATE: target = fisher_localdelta_discriminative_target, head = erasing_dualaddress_transport_batchhard
(objective / arch / optim / batcher / stream inherited from the parent unchanged)

WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH SIDE

From the parent I keep the thing that made it a parent on this axis: the model is not trained in the raw
standardized observation space but in a globally re-metricized one, built from a running second moment of
the targets, applied as one matrix identical for every step and every window, and undone exactly by the
matching inverse before retrieval. I also keep its arch (r22 prefix-content cross-attention) and its stream
(r3 src/dst hashslot coding), because that stream is what puts a path's identity into the command token as
one recoverable sparse pattern in a source block and the same pattern in a destination block — the substrate
a two-address transport memory needs to make "the destination of move k" and "the source of move k+1" the
same key.

From the second program I keep its head: erasing_dualaddress_transport_batchhard, in place of the parent's
r2_dualaddress_move_transport (which the parent additionally ran with pred_weight = 0.0, so only its memory
read was supervised). Three properties of that head are the reason: (a) after the destination has taken the
value read at the source, the source itself is pushed toward a gated erasure of its own current value, so a
location that has been moved away from stops returning what it used to hold — that is exactly the failure a
depth-3/4 chain punishes; (b) every gate is a function of the command embedding alone and the observation
enters only as a write VALUE scaled by an observation-present factor, so a command slot whose observation is
absent still READS — the head is not switched off at the scored position, which is a command whose own
observation is withheld; (c) its forward-model term supervises f(obs_pre, cmd) against the later
post-mutation observation, and the parent's arch (r22 inherits r18) exposes transition_from_emb, so that term
is live rather than a no-op.

THE NEW MECHANISM, AND WHAT IT COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PROGRAM COMPUTES

Neither program computes any statistic of the STEP-TO-STEP DIFFERENCE between consecutive observations. The
parent's target keeps one estimate — the second moment of the targets themselves — and whitens by it. The
second program's target is the identity and keeps nothing. This candidate keeps a second estimate and uses
the pair to define a Fisher-style discriminative metric instead of a plain whitening.

Concretely, per training step the module receives z_obs [n,768] and z_prev [n,768] (z_prev is zero on a
sequence's first step). It updates, as before, the EMA second moment S of z_obs. It additionally forms
d = z_obs - z_prev for the rows whose z_prev is nonzero, discards rows whose d is (numerically) exactly zero,
sorts the surviving squared magnitudes and keeps only the lower half, unit-normalizes each kept d, and
accumulates an EMA of E[d_hat d_hat^T] as a second matrix S_d with trace fixed to 768. Every _REFRESH_EVERY
steps, on CPU in float64: S is shrunk toward I and eigendecomposed as V diag(mu) V^T; the local-change moment
is carried into the FULLY whitened frame, A = S^(-1/2) S_d S^(-1/2), and eigendecomposed as U diag(lam) U^T;
each direction gets gain g_i = clamp((lam_i / mean(lam))^(q/2), 1/c, c). The forward map is
F = s * U diag(g) U^T * V diag(mu^(-p/2)) V^T and the inverse is Finv = V diag(mu^(p/2)) V^T * U diag(1/g) U^T / s,
so Finv F = I exactly by construction (U orthogonal, and mu^(p/2) mu^(-p/2) = 1), and s is chosen so
tr(F S_shrunk F^T) = 768, i.e. the transformed targets keep unit average per-dimension variance. With q = 0
the gains are all 1 and the whole thing collapses to exactly the parent's map including its normalizing
constant, so the new mechanism is a strict extension of the mechanism it is crossed with.

WHY THAT SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY

The score is decided by a squared-L2 distance, averaged over all 768 dimensions, between the model's
next-observation prediction and each candidate content's true observation embedding. All the candidates at a
scored read sit on the same board: same image, same cwd, same exit line, same verb, same rendering — they
differ only in the content string that the chain routed there. So only the subspace along which SAME-CONTEXT
observations differ can decide the comparison, while every other direction (image identity, output length,
listing format, the empty-output mode that every mv and every redirect step shares) contributes distance that
is common to all candidates and pure variance to the model's fit.

Whitening by the total second moment is a crude proxy for that: it up-weights low-total-variance directions,
which correlates with content-hash directions only because those happen to be small. The local-change moment
measures the right contrast directly. In the training split, consecutive steps of one trajectory are two
reads of the same board that differ in exactly one content token, or two listings of the same directory that
differ by exactly one moved filename; the difference vector for such a pair IS a content-routing direction.
Consecutive steps that switch format (a content read followed by a mutation's empty observation, or an ls
followed by a cat) produce large differences, and two mutations in a row produce an identically-rendered
observation and hence an exactly-zero difference. The lower-half-of-nonzero selection is what separates those
three populations without labels: the exact zeros are dropped by the nonzero test, the format switches are
dropped by the magnitude cut, and what accumulates is the same-context content-difference scatter. In the
train sample here, exactly-zero-difference consecutive pairs are about a fifth of all consecutive pairs and
same-shape neighbours (short-output read followed by short-output read, long followed by long) are about a
third, so the kept band is populated and is dominated by the pairs I want. Unit-normalizing each kept
difference makes the estimate a direction histogram, so a handful of atypically large kept differences cannot
define the basis.

There is a second, compounding effect on the loss that is worth checking in the code. The inherited objective
(r12 antiretrieval ring negatives) builds its negative weighting from TARGET-TARGET distances in whatever
space make_target hands it, with a band-pass: near-identical targets get ~0 weight as presumed false
negatives, close-but-distinct targets get up to (1+kappa)x weight. Under the raw or merely whitened metric,
two observations that differ only in a content hash are close enough to risk being masked out as duplicates —
that is, the very pairs the eval discriminates between get discounted as hard negatives. Stretching exactly
the content-difference subspace pushes those pairs out of the duplicate band and into the ring, while the
empty-output observations shared by every mutation step stay exact duplicates of each other and keep getting
~0 weight. So the same matrix that fixes the geometry of the regression also re-aims the contrastive term at
the eval's own decision.

SCORE-TIME SAFETY (the failure this design deliberately avoids)

z_prev is used ONLY to accumulate statistics inside make_target during training, under
`self.training and torch.is_grad_enabled()`. It is not an argument of either map: make_target returns
z_obs @ F.t() and to_obs returns pred @ Finv.t(). At the scored position — a command whose own observation is
withheld — nothing about this mechanism is gated on an observation being present, and to_obs behaves
identically whatever z_prev is passed, including zeros. A delta-style target (predict z_obs - z_prev, invert
by adding z_prev back) would have had exactly that gating hazard; this one does not.

It also computes nothing about routing. F is one global matrix, the same for every step, every window, every
candidate and every arm of the role swap, containing only second-order statistics of the training split's
embeddings. No command is parsed, no chain is resolved, nothing derived from the move sequence is placed in
the model's input. Everything about which content ends up where still has to be learned by the net.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

1. If the frozen encoder does not place the content-hash difference in a stable low-dimensional subspace —
   if two reads of the same board differing only in the hex token differ in a direction that varies from pair
   to pair — then S_d converges toward an isotropic matrix, lam is flat, all gains go to 1, and the map
   degenerates to the parent's whitening. In that case this candidate should land at the parent's score, not
   below it. This is the benign failure.
2. If the lower-half magnitude cut selects the wrong population — e.g. if per-dimension standardization makes
   content differences LARGER than format switches, so the kept band is dominated by the pairs I meant to
   exclude — the amplified subspace would be the format axis. That subspace is shared by all candidates at a
   scored read, so it would waste amplification on directions that cancel, and the candidate would score at or
   below the parent. This is the real risk, and it is the assumption a reviewer should attack: I am asserting
   that among nonzero consecutive differences, format switches are the larger ones. I have checked the text
   population that produces them (empty-output mutations render as a single constant line, content reads as a
   short hex token, listings as many lines) but I have not measured the embedded magnitudes, so state it as
   an untested assumption, not a fact.
3. If the gain cap c = 3 combined with the whitening at p = 0.75 conditions the map badly enough that the
   objective's precision weighting and the spectral cap on the (D,D) readout fight it, training could get
   noisier. The trace renormalization holds the average per-dimension variance of the transformed targets at
   1, and both stages are eigenvalue-clamped, which is the mitigation.
4. If the erasing head's erasure gate (initialized at bias -2, i.e. mostly off) never turns on within the
   fixed step budget, the head contributes little beyond the parent's head and the crossover reduces to a
   target-axis change alone.
5. Cost: two 768x768 float64 eigendecompositions plus about six 768-cubed float64 matmuls per refresh, at
   _REFRESH_EVERY = 100 after a 200-step warmup, versus one eigendecomposition every 50 steps for the parent.
   That is the same order of CPU work, not more; the per-step added work is one masked 768x768 gram over
   roughly half the rows. I have not profiled this on the scoring box — it is an estimate from the operation
   counts, not a measurement.
