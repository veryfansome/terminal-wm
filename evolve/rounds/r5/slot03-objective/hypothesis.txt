CANDIDATE: objective = r5_boardlocal_namecollision_quotient_nce
All other axes are the parent's, unchanged.

WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH PROGRAM

From the parent (r12_antiretrieval_ring_negatives): the per-dimension precision weighting of the
distance, the band-pass confusability RING over target-target distance (close-but-distinct
negatives amplified, near-identical ones suppressed), the focal hardness weight, the margin
repulsion hinge stated in the eval's own squared-L2 decision variable, and the squared-error
anchor.

From the partner (r22_exact_target_equivalence_quotient): the exact-target equivalence QUOTIENT.
Numerically equal targets become one multi-positive class; every candidate's occurrence mass is
divided by its class size; the positive term is a logsumexp over the whole class. This is the
mechanism that matters most on this data and I checked why on the jail sample
(evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl, 24 trajectories / 724 steps, rendered exactly as
realenv.seq_worldmodel.render_obs does): 227 of 724 steps (31%) carry the SAME observation string
"cwd=/ exit=0\n" -- every mv and every redirect step, because a command that mutates the
filesystem prints nothing and the rendered observation is only cwd + exit code. 63% of steps have
an observation that is duplicated somewhere in the sample. Without the quotient, roughly a third
of every batch is a block of mutual false negatives that a plain in-batch softmax actively pushes
apart from its own correct answer.

WHAT IS NEW -- WHAT NEITHER PROGRAM COMPUTES

Both programs build their negative weighting from ONE signal: the distance between targets. The
new module keeps that but makes the negative distribution an explicit per-row MIXTURE over four
components with fixed mass budgets, and two of the components are new signals that require
WANTS_CTX (neither parent sets it):

1. SAME-TRAJECTORY (board-local) negatives, budget _RHO_GROUP = 0.35. Rows are cut into
   trajectories by _segment_ids: the harness builds ctx["prev"] as the strict-causal shift of the
   target matrix inside each padded sequence, so the first command row of every sequence -- and
   only those -- has an exactly all-zero previous observation. cumsum over that indicator gives a
   segment id per row. Errors in this segmentation can only SPLIT a trajectory, never merge two,
   because a real standardized observation embedding is not exactly zero.

2. COMMAND-COLLISION negatives, budget _RHO_COLL = 0.25 (_collision). Cosine similarity between
   the anchor's own command input, ctx["cmd"], and every candidate's; standardized to a z-score
   using the batch's own off-diagonal mean and standard deviation; passed through
   sigmoid((z - 2.0)/0.5). This selects the candidates whose COMMAND is a near-duplicate of the
   anchor's command while the ANSWER is different -- gated by (1 - exp(-tt/_DUP_DELTA)) and by
   `distinct`, so a candidate with the same or a near-identical target is never a collision.
   The same statistic also raises the anchor's own weight in the listwise average
   (row_w = inv_class * (1 + _ROW_COLL * max_j collide)).

3. The RING keeps a budget of _RHO_RING = 0.20, and 0.20 stays on the uniform (occurrence-
   quotiented) share. A component with zero mass in a row folds its budget back into the uniform
   share, so every row's negative weights sum to exactly 1 and the mass a component receives is
   independent of batch size and of how many candidates it contains -- unlike an additive
   kappa-boost, whose effective share silently shrinks as the batch grows.

WHY THAT SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed: it pays only for carrying a particular content's identity
through the chain of moves, and a predictor that keys on WHICH PATH is being read contributes
exactly zero. So the training signal that matters is precisely the one that punishes reading a
name instead of tracking content. Component 2 manufactures that signal directly and it has real
mass in this data: on the jail sample, 43 distinct command strings produce more than one distinct
output, and 299 of 724 steps (41%) are steps whose command does NOT determine its observation --
`cat /tmp/w/cvar/g0/s0.dat` alone appears with 21 different contents across trajectories, and
inside a single trajectory 48 steps re-read a path whose content changed under them (13 of 24
trajectories contain at least one). For every one of those rows, the model that answers from the
path text is provably wrong and the model that answers from what its own context routed there is
right. Under both parents those pairs are ordinary negatives at weight ~1 among ~1500 in-batch
candidates and can be ignored at essentially no loss; here they own a quarter of the negative
mass and additionally double the row's weight.

Component 1 makes the softmax resemble the measurement's own question. The cups probe never asks
the model to separate boards -- it asks which of the N contents exposed on THIS board is at the
read location. Cross-trajectory negatives are easy and there are ~1500 of them per batch;
same-trajectory negatives are the other exposed contents of the same board, ~17 distinct
observations per trajectory in the sample. Giving them a fixed 35% of the negative mass turns the
bulk of the training signal into the discrimination the probe scores, instead of leaving it a
~1.5% share.

WHY THIS IS NOT COMPUTING THE ANSWER OUTSIDE THE MODEL

The module is a loss. It never touches the model's input, it never runs at scoring time, and it
never parses a command, a path or a move: it sees only pred, tgt, ctx["cmd"] (the command token
the model itself was fed) and ctx["prev"] (a strictly causal previous observation). It resolves no
move chain; it re-weights which comparisons the gradient pays attention to. The one mechanical
trap named in the brief -- a mechanism gated on an observation being present, which switches off
exactly at the withheld scored position -- does not apply: nothing here is evaluated at scoring
time at all, and the segmentation signal it uses (an all-zero previous observation) is a property
of the training collate, not of the scored window.

ANTI-COLLAPSE

Positive weights per row sum to 1 and negative weights per row sum to 1, so nll = logsumexp(all) -
logsumexp(class) >= 0 and equals 0 only if the prediction is nearer its own equivalence class than
every weighted alternative. A constant prediction gives every row the same distance profile over
candidates, cannot be nearest to each row's own class, and additionally pays the class-balanced
squared-error anchor and the repulsion hinge. I verified the direction on a pure-Python mirror of
the exact algebra (no torch in this jail): perfect prediction -> ~0.0002, noisy -> 0.023, constant
-> 2.15, batch-mean constant -> 2.19; every row's negative weights summed to 1.0 and every row's
positive weights summed to 1.0; nll never went negative; and with the segmentation signal removed
the group budget folded back into the uniform share without changing anything else. That is a
correctness check on a 12-row toy, not a performance measurement -- I have taken no measurement of
this candidate's score.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL

- If near-identical COMMAND embeddings do not in fact select the history-dependent pairs -- e.g.
  if the r3 stream's sparse src/dst hash code dominates the cosine so much that two reads of the
  same path in different trajectories are no longer upper-tail outliers -- component 2 degenerates
  into an ordinary hard-negative miner over whatever the top ~2% of command similarity happens to
  be, and buys nothing. (Identical command strings still give identical codes and cosine 1, so the
  cross-trajectory cvar case survives; the suffix-differing cups case is the one at risk.)
- If a fixed 35% on same-trajectory negatives is too aggressive, training could over-fit
  within-board discrimination and lose the general next-observation fit that the absolute pick
  rate depends on; the 0.20 uniform floor and the 0.05 squared-error anchor are the only things
  holding that.
- If most gradient signal actually comes from the 31% mutation rows, quotienting their occurrence
  mass down to that of a single row removes it. I judge this to be the right trade -- their target
  is a constant string and there is nothing to learn from predicting it -- but it is a real change
  in what the loss spends its budget on.
- Cost: one extra n-by-n matmul per step (the command Gram matrix, ~768 inner dimension) on top of
  the two the parents already form, plus a few more n-by-n elementwise buffers. Unmeasured; I
  could not profile it here.
