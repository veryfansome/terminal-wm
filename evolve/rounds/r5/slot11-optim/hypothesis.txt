1. THE NEW MECHANISM ON MY AXIS: a zero-extra-backward adaptive sharpness step
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT THE PARENT'S OPTIM COMPUTES. AdamW with a warmup-hold-cosine-floor schedule on everything
except the identical-shape (key_d x d) addressing projection pairs, Muon (Newton-Schulz
orthogonalisation of the momentum buffer) on those pairs, and after every update a power-iteration
estimate of the top singular value of each (D,D) readout with a multiplicative projection back to a
cap. Every one of those quantities is a function of the CURRENT parameter point only. It is a pure
descent method: the gradient it applies is always the gradient measured at the point it is applied.

WHAT I ADD, AS AN EQUATION. Let theta_t be the parameters after update t, g_t the gradient the
harness has just produced (already clipped to global norm 1.0 by the harness), and

    m_t = beta * m_{t-1} + (1 - beta) * g_t                      (beta = sharp_beta, default 0.6)
    T_t = |theta_t| + eta                                        (elementwise, eta = 0.01)
    eps_t = rho_t * (T_t * T_t * m_t) / || T_t * m_t ||_2         (norm taken GLOBALLY over all params)

At the END of optimizer step t I set theta_t <- theta_t + eps_t and remember eps_t. The harness then
runs the NEXT minibatch's forward and backward at theta_t + eps_t, producing g_{t+1} = grad L_{t+1}
(theta_t + eps_t). At the START of optimizer step t+1 I subtract eps_t back off, and only then run
AdamW/Muon/cap. So the update actually applied is

    theta_{t+1} = AdamWStep( theta_t ,  grad L_{t+1}(theta_t + eps_t) )

which is Sharpness-Aware Minimization (Foret et al., ICLR 2021) with the ASAM elementwise
|theta|-rescaling (Kwon et al., ICML 2021) that makes the perturbation radius scale-invariant --
except that the ascent direction is a filtered gradient from the previous minibatch rather than a
second backward pass on the current one. That substitution is what makes it FREE: this optimizer
costs one backward per step, exactly like the parent, because the harness never passes a closure and
a true two-point SAM is not expressible under this axis contract. The precedent for reusing the
ascent direction across steps is LookSAM (Liu et al., CVPR 2022), which recomputes the SAM direction
only every k steps (k=5) and reuses it in between with essentially no loss of the SAM benefit; my
staleness is one step, and the EMA with beta=0.6 low-passes the minibatch noise in the direction.

MECHANICAL DETAILS THAT MATTER FOR REVIEW.
  * rho_t is 0 for the first `warm` steps (the same warm as the LR schedule) and then smoothsteps
    up to rho over sharp_ramp_frac * steps. Early training is untouched.
  * The displacement is SKIPPED on the final step (`if self.taken < self.total_steps`). `steps` is
    the number of optimizer steps the harness will take, so training ends, and the probe's forward
    runs, on undisplaced weights. This is the one correctness property the whole mechanism hangs on
    and it is enforced by construction, not by a heuristic.
  * revert() is unconditional at the top of every step, so the descent is always applied at the base
    point and AdamW's decoupled weight decay always acts on the base point.
  * The cap projection runs on the base weights (after the update, before the new displacement), so
    the spectral cap still means what it meant in the parent.
  * rho = 0 makes this file numerically identical to the parent's optimizer apart from a fixed
    latent bug (the parent's _SpectralCap references `v` from the power-iteration loop, which is
    unbound if spectral_iters were ever set to 0; iters is now clamped to >= 1).
  * Non-finite gradients, a zero global norm, or a non-finite displacement all fall through to
    "displace nothing", so a bad step degrades to the parent's behaviour rather than to NaN.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY. comp_ca is measured on windows held out BY IMAGE: the
model has never seen the filesystem it is asked to route on. The gap between "this net has fit the
training systems" and "this net routes correctly on a new system" is a generalisation gap, and
sharpness of the minimum is the single best-supported predictor of that gap. It matters more than
usual for THIS genome specifically: the r23 arch routes content through a bank of sigmoid gates
(move/write/erase, plus the alpha and tp_gate blend) whose decisions are piecewise-constant in the
weights. A sharp minimum in that gate landscape is exactly a minimum in which the gate thresholds
have been fitted to the particular command-embedding statistics of the training images -- a lookup.
A flat minimum is one where the same gate decision survives a |theta|-relative perturbation of the
weights, i.e. a decision made on a margin rather than on a coincidence. Sharpness-aware training
selects the second kind. Because comp_ca is a DIFFERENCE (native minus role-swapped), it is
insensitive to anything the model does that is identical across the two arms; making the gate
decisions robust rather than memorised is one of the few things that is not identical across arms,
because the two arms differ precisely in which content the gates should move where.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THE OPTIM MECHANISM TO FAIL.
  (a) The one-step-stale, EMA-filtered direction m_t is nearly orthogonal to the true local sharpest
      direction at theta_t. Then eps_t is effectively a scaled random weight perturbation: still a
      regulariser, but a much weaker one, and the gain shrinks toward noise. This is the most likely
      failure and it is the honest risk of the design; the objective here is a high-variance
      listwise contrastive, so consecutive minibatch gradients may be less correlated than they are
      in the image-classification settings LookSAM measured.
  (b) The model is under-fitted at this step budget rather than over-fitted. If the bottleneck is
      that the transport pathway has not yet converged, adding an ascent perturbation slows the
      effective descent and costs accuracy on both arms. The ramp (no perturbation until after
      warmup) and the modest rho are the hedge, but they do not eliminate this.
  (c) rho = 0.5 with eta = 0.01 is calibrated for ASAM's usual regime; if the displacement is large
      relative to the zero-initialised readouts (tp_out starts at exactly 0, so T = eta = 0.01 there
      and the displacement on that tensor is tiny by construction) the perturbation lands almost
      entirely on the trunk. That is intended, but it means the mechanism regularises the trunk more
      than the readout.
  I have NOT measured any of this. There is no torch in this jail; nothing here was run.

2. THE CROSSOVER: which mechanism I took from each program, and why
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FROM THE PARENT I KEEP the r23 dual-address transport arch and the r4 trajectory-transport-contrast
objective. r23 is the only arch of the two that separates a SOURCE address from a DESTINATION
address per command and performs an explicit read-move-erase as one rank-2 delta-rule update, which
is the operation the score is about. r4 is the only objective of the two that recovers, inside the
loss and from the batch alone, which rows are TRANSPORTED (target duplicates a non-generic target
seen earlier in the same trajectory under a DIFFERENT command, gated by command cosine distance so a
re-read of the same path does not count) and puts 6x listwise mass plus a restricted-candidate
cross-entropy plus a margin hinge on exactly those rows. The partner's r22 objective is a good
general exact-target quotient contrastive but has no notion of transport. So: parent's arch, parent's
objective.

FROM THE PARTNER I TAKE the r3_occupancy_routed_copy_transport head and the
r2_routed_collision_depth_batcher.

The head is the important one, and the reason is the mechanical fact in the brief: the scored
position is a command whose own observation is withheld. The parent's head,
r18_transition_forwardmodel_consistency, is a TRAIN-ONLY auxiliary that is switched off entirely
unless the net exposes `transition_from_emb` (`cfg["_disabled"] = not callable(getattr(net,
"transition_from_emb", None))`, and every aux_loss path returns 0.0 when it is). It contributes
nothing at eval under any circumstances. r3 does two things instead:
  * It is live in the FORWARD, not only in the loss: wrap() replaces net.forward so the retrieved
    content is added at every command position through a zero-initialised per-dimension scale (so
    the wrapped net is bit-identical to the unwrapped net at initialisation, and the r23
    contribution it sits alongside is also zero-init -- the two additions do not fight at step 0).
  * Its read path is built from the COMMAND EMBEDDING ALONE. It splits the axes deliberately:
    (L+1)//2 read positions, one per command, against L//2 write slots, one per COMPLETED
    (command, observation) pair. A final command whose observation has not happened still issues its
    own query and still retrieves. That is precisely the position comp_ca reads, and it is the
    property the brief warns has already been fatal to a proposal that gated on an observation being
    present. I checked this in the code rather than inferring it: `read_ok = live[:, 0::2]` and
    `write_ok = read_ok[:, :n_write] & live[:, 1::2]`, and the query comes from `cf = gelu(cmd_feat(c))`
    with no observation term.
  * It does not lose the parent head's supervision: r3's aux ALSO supervises
    `net.transition_from_emb` when the arch exposes one (trans_weight = 0.5, cosine + MSE toward the
    duplicated content). So swapping r18 -> r3 is a strict superset of what the parent's head could
    have contributed, in both the disabled and the enabled case.
  * Its value recursion is solved as one unit-lower-triangular system over the write slots, so a
    retrieved value can itself be a retrieval of a retrieval -- depth composes in a single pass
    rather than being bounded by the number of memory writes. The eligible slice is d3 60 / d4+ 29,
    i.e. the majority of it is depth >= 3, so an unbounded-depth read path is the right shape.

The batcher, r2, replays each training sequence's command list through a symbolic mv/cp/redirect
tracker to label each read with how many hops routed its content there, then tilts batch composition
toward buckets keyed by (image, normalised path shape, hop count) with an exp(0.8 * depth) weight.
This is sample selection from parsed commands, which the brief explicitly names as legitimate: no
resolved routing is placed in the model's input, nothing is fed to the net that it did not have to
learn, and the labels are used only to decide WHICH training sequences co-occur in a batch. The
parent's r6 blocks only by image; it densifies same-system negatives but is blind to depth. Since
r4's transported-row boost and its restricted-candidate cross-entropy only fire when a batch
actually contains transported rows and their same-trajectory alternatives, feeding r4 a
depth-tilted, collision-grouped batch stream should raise the fraction of steps on which its
transport terms carry any gradient at all. r6 raises it only incidentally.

INTERACTIONS I CHECKED BY HAND (no torch here, so these are static checks, not runs):
  * Muon routing under the new head. My optim routes to Muon only 2-D params with shape[0] == key_d
    == 64 whose exact shape occurs at least twice. r23 supplies addr_src and addr_dst, both
    (64, addr_hidden=256): a pair, routed. r3's memory adds `addr` = Linear(hid=128 -> key_d=64,
    bias=False), weight (64, 128): a LONE tensor of that shape, so the sibling rule excludes it and
    it goes to AdamW. That is the intended behaviour of the sibling rule and the head does not
    perturb the parent's routing.
  * Spectral cap targets. (D,D) = (768,768) params are r23's tp_out.weight only; r3's head adds no
    (D,D) matrix (its out_scale is 1-D). So the cap means the same thing it meant for the parent.
  * Head wrap ordering. The harness calls head.wrap(net, D) BEFORE net.to(device) and builds the
    optimizer from net.parameters() AFTER, so r3's `copy_transport` submodule is on-device and its
    parameters are in the optimizer, including under my displacement (which iterates the same
    `params` list `make` was given).
  * Cost. The added optimizer work is O(#params) elementwise per step plus one scalar
    device-to-host sync for the global norm -- the same order as the spectral cap's existing
    per-step sync. It adds no forward, no backward, and no allocation that scales with batch or
    sequence length. Memory grows by two parameter-sized buffers (the EMA direction and the stored
    displacement) on top of AdamW's two. This is an unmeasured estimate from reading the code, not a
    profile.
