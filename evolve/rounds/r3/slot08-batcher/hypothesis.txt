WHAT I CHANGED

One axis: batcher. r2_routedepth_tilt_readcollision_blocks -> r3_cuebalanced_routing_blocks.
Everything else in the genome is inherited from the parent unchanged.

The crossover keeps both parents' block machinery and adds a third mechanism that neither has.

Kept from the parent (r2_routedepth_tilt_readcollision_blocks):
  - the mv-edge backward walk that gives each sequence its deepest routed read and the exact hop
    count from the read location back to the file the content started in;
  - the read-collision neighbourhood block: sequences whose deepest routed read has a
    near-duplicate command embedding under a fixed JL projection while their read answers are
    NOT near-duplicates;
  - the annealed Boltzmann tilt exp(alpha*z) on the hop-count score and the annealed hard
    fraction.
Kept from the crossover partner (r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum):
  - the image block is now the POOLED UNION of n_block_images distinct systems drawn without
    replacement, the partner's shape, rather than the parent's single-image block. A batch's
    same-system negatives therefore come from a few systems at once instead of one.

THE NEW MECHANISM: ORDER-CUE DECORRELATION BY RAKING

For each training sequence I now also extract, from the command strings alone, two ORDER RANKS
of the content that the deepest routed read actually returns:

  cue_a = fm_rank. Take the board: every mv in the trajectory, before the read, whose source or
    destination shares the routed content's leading 3-component path prefix (e.g. /tmp/w/cups).
    Sort those moves in time order. fm_rank is the index, in that list, of the EARLIEST move that
    belongs to the walked chain. fm_rank == 0 means the tested content is the one that moved
    first on its board.
  cue_b = orank. Take the distinct files read (2-token cat/head/tail) on that same path-prefix
    board before the first board move — these are the window's exposures. orank is the position
    of the walked chain's origin file in that list. orank == 0 means the tested content is the
    one exposed first.

Measured on the 24 real training trajectories in evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl, these two
ranks are strongly non-uniform: fm_rank is 0 in 13 of 24, 1 in 7, >=2 in 4; orank is 0 in 9, 1 in
10, >=2 in 5. So "answer = the content whose origin moved first on this board" is right about 54%
of the time while the number of contenders on a board is 2..5 (chance roughly 30%), and "answer =
one of the first two exposed" is right about 79% of the time.

The batcher reweights the sequence distribution to destroy exactly those two correlations.
Sequences are stratified by hop count (1-2 / 3-4 / 5+ / no routed read). Inside each stratum I run
iterative proportional fitting (raking, the survey-statistics estimator: alternately rescale the
weight mass of each fm_rank class and each orank class to the stratum mean, 4 sweeps) to obtain
per-sequence weights that drive BOTH rank marginals toward uniform while every stratum keeps its
original total share, so the hop-count marginal is untouched. Weights are raised to cue_gamma,
clipped to a bounded ratio (cue_cap) so effective sample size stays bounded, and renormalized.

Two places consume that weight:
  1. The "uniform" part of every batch is replaced by an inverse-CDF draw from it. This is ON FROM
     STEP 1 and is NEVER annealed — unlike the hardness curriculum, which still ramps.
  2. Inside every block, the pool weight is the raking weight times the annealed hop-count tilt,
     AND the block's slots are dealt round-robin across the three fm_rank classes (rotating with
     the step index) so each individual batch, not merely the long-run average, carries the three
     classes in near-equal number. Per-batch balance matters here because the objective's loss is
     an in-batch listwise softmax: examples in one batch are coupled, so a cue that is balanced
     only in expectation is still exploitable within each step.

WHAT THIS COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES

Both parents compute, at most, hop depth. Neither computes where in the board's move order or
the board's exposure order the answered content sits, and neither does anything to the
distribution of those quantities. The parent's tilt makes deep chains more frequent; it leaves
the first-mover correlation exactly as the data generator produced it. Simulated on a fit built
from the 24 real trajectories (replicated, embeddings randomized, bs=64, 4000-step schedule),
the fm_rank share of drawn sequences is 0.534 / 0.293 / 0.173 for the parent and 0.540 / 0.295 /
0.164 for the partner, versus 0.375 / 0.362 / 0.263 for this impl, and 0.394 / 0.350 / 0.256 in
the late, fully-blocked phase where the depth tilt is at full strength. That is the intended
signature: the deep-chain preference is preserved (late-phase depth>=4 share rises from 0.33 to
0.50) while the first-mover cue is driven to near chance.

WHY THAT SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed, and the swap exchanges the move-position sets of the
routed content and a partner over the same board. A model that answers "the content that moved
first" is right natively whenever fm_rank == 0 and, after the swap, follows the swap to the
partner — so it scores +1 on those windows and -1 on the mirror-image windows where the partner
was the first mover. It cancels only in expectation, over a frozen slice of n=89. It buys nothing
real, and the brief already flags it as a band the score must be read against.

The cost of leaving that cue in the diet is not the band, though: it is that the cue is by far the
cheapest descent direction available early. Detecting "which mv command comes first" needs only
the position embedding and one command token. Detecting "which mv chain terminates at the path I
am being asked to read" needs the model to match the second path string of one mv command against
the first path string of another, repeatedly, through a 768-d frozen mean-pooled sentence
embedding projected to width 192 — the composition the r22 path-state arch's delta-rule file and
path memories exist to perform. When a cue that is 54%-accurate is available for almost no
parameters, gradient descent takes it first and the expensive circuit never has to form; this is
ordinary shortcut learning, and the standard fix is to remove the shortcut's predictive value from
the training distribution rather than to penalize it in the loss (LfF / bias-conflicting
upweighting; group-balanced resampling; raking). Flattening fm_rank to ~1/3 inside each depth
stratum makes the first-mover rule worth about what guessing is worth, on a board with 2-5
contenders. The only remaining way to reduce the listwise loss on a routed read is to route.

That is why I expect a change larger than measurement noise rather than a nudge: the mechanism
does not add a small preference on top of the parent's diet, it removes the alternative solution
the model can currently settle into. If the parent's positive score is partly the frozen slice's
realization of that cue, this candidate should trade that component for a component that survives
both arms; if the parent has already learned some real routing, this should raise the share of
capacity spent on it.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL

  - The order cues are already near-uniform on the full training split. I measured them on 24 real
    trajectories only; if the generator's distribution over larger samples is flatter than that,
    the raking weights collapse toward 1 and this becomes the parent with pooled image blocks.
    Falsifiable directly: print the fm_rank/orank histograms over the full fit.
  - The model was never taking the shortcut. Then removing it costs the small ESS the weight
    clipping implies and gains nothing; expect a wash or a slight loss.
  - My board reconstruction is wrong on the real distribution. fm_rank depends on grouping moves
    by a 3-component path prefix. If the eval-relevant boards are not separated by that prefix
    (or several boards share one), fm_rank is computed against the wrong move list and the
    weights flatten a quantity that is not the cue. Checked against the sample: the prefix rule
    reproduces a hand-computed board-level fm_rank on all 24 trajectories, and separates the
    /tmp/w/cups boards from the /tmp/w/cvar ones; it does merge the several cvar groups, whose
    chains are shallow and rarely the deepest read.
  - Balancing fm_rank also destroys something the model legitimately needs. The chain's first move
    is genuinely informative about depth; I mitigate this by raking INSIDE hop-count strata, so
    the depth marginal is preserved exactly and only the within-depth order correlation is
    removed. If the two cannot be separated, native_hit drops without swap_stayed dropping.
  - Effective sample size loss. cue_cap=4.0 bounds the weight ratio; on the sample the realized
    range was [0.24, 3.03] with mean 1. If the real split produces heavier tails inside a stratum
    the clip binds and the flattening is partial, weakening but not inverting the mechanism.

NO ANSWER IS COMPUTED FOR THE MODEL

The batcher returns a list of sequence indices and nothing else. It never edits a sequence, never
writes a field, never touches z_obs or z_cmd, and never tells the training loop which content is
the right one. Resolving the mv chain in this module is used only to choose WHICH real
trajectories train together — the same use the parent already makes of it for hop depth. The model
sees the identical, unmodified trajectories and must still solve the read from the tokens.

MECHANICAL CHECKS PERFORMED (no real harness available here)

Contract: returns exactly bs Python ints in [0, len(fit)) at steps 1, 10, 500, 4000; bit-identical
across two make_batcher calls with the same seed; different across seeds; the global torch RNG
stream is unchanged after 20 batches; `fit` is unmutated (images and cmds compared before/after).
Degradations exercised: 1 and 2 sequences, bs=1, a single image, sequences with no mv, sequences
with no `cmds` key, a mixed fit of routed and unrouted sequences, hard_frac_max=0, cue_gamma=0.
Speed, on this laptop and therefore not a claim about the scoring box: setup 0.02 s at n=600 and
0.54 s at n=30000 (the O(m^2) collision scan is capped by max_centers=8000, as in the parent);
per-step cost ~0.1 ms, unchanged from the parent's order of magnitude and negligible beside a
training step. No claim about the score itself is measured; all numbers above are distributional
properties of the sampler, not fitness.
