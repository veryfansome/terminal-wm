1. WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH PROGRAM

The two programs I was given differ on three axes, and on two of them one side is a strict
code-level superset of the other. I checked this by reading the sources, not by guessing:

  - arch. evolve/chunks/arch/r22_retrieval_composition_renderer.py declares
    `class R22RetrievalCompositionRenderer(R18PathStateLatentTransition)` and imports the
    partner's arch as its base. r22 IS r18 plus a zero-init composition renderer. Keeping the
    parent's r22 therefore keeps the partner's arch mechanism intact inside it.
  - head. evolve/chunks/head/r20_dualpre_transition_consistency.py imports the parent's head as
    `CH` and calls CH.wrap / CH._mine_triples / CH._smoothstep / CH._interleave_layout_ok, then
    adds a second supervision arm on the same mined triples with `pre` taken from the arch's own
    `_transition_reads` memory instead of the raw observation. r20 IS the parent's r18 head plus
    the mem-pre arm. Taking the partner's r20 therefore keeps the parent's head mechanism intact
    inside it. This is the one axis where the parent is dominated, so this is the axis I flip.
  - batcher. r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum builds image pools and draws a ramped hard fraction
    from k sampled images. r2_routedepth_tilt_readcollision_blocks builds the same image pools
    (`img_pools`, sampled by size, ramped `hard_frac_max`) and adds read-collision pools plus the
    Boltzmann tilt on hop-count depth, mixing the two block kinds by `coll_share`. With
    coll_share=0.5 the parent's batcher already emits the partner's image-block behaviour half the
    time. Keeping r2 keeps both.

So the arch/head/batcher part of the crossover is: r22 arch + r20 head + r2 batcher, which is the
union of the two programs' mechanisms rather than a pick. That union alone is not worth a slot.
Both programs also carry the SAME optim impl, so the crossover operator gives me nothing on my own
axis; the invention has to happen there.


2. THE MECHANISM I ADDED (optim), AND WHAT IT COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES

Two additions on top of the shared optim (AdamW + warmup/hold/cosine + Muon on the (key_d x d)
group + soft spectral cap on the (D,D) transition readout, all of which I kept):

(A) SOFT STIEFEL RETRACTION OF THE ADDRESSING PROJECTIONS.
    After every optimizer step, each weight W in the (key_d x d) group is replaced by
        W <- (1 - eta) * W  +  eta * ( Q * ||W||_F / ||Q||_F ),   Q = polar_orth(W),
    with eta = retract_rate = 0.2 and Q computed by 6 Newton-Schulz quintic iterations (the same
    `_ns_orth` primitive Muon uses, applied to the WEIGHT rather than to the update). Because
    key_d=64 < d=176, the fixed point of this map is the set of row-orthonormal 64x176 matrices:
    the Stiefel manifold St(176,64), i.e. W = R P where P projects R^176 onto a freely chosen
    64-dimensional subspace and R rotates it. This is the Bjorck / orthogonal-weight-normalization
    retraction (Bjorck & Bowie 1971; Huang et al. AAAI 2018; the soft-orthogonality family of
    Bansal, Chen & Wang NeurIPS 2018), applied as a manifold retraction after a Muon step rather
    than as a penalty in the loss.

    Neither parent computes this. Muon orthogonalizes the UPDATE and leaves the weight's spectrum
    free; the shared optim's spectral cap constrains only sigma_max of the single (768,768)
    `tr_read` and only from above. Nothing in either program bounds the CONDITION NUMBER of the
    addressing maps from below, and nothing stops their effective rank collapsing.

(B) CONSTANT-LR PLATEAU + UNIFORM TAIL AVERAGE WITH WRITE-BACK.
    The schedule is changed from warmup / hold / cosine-to-floor into warmup / hold / cosine-down-
    to-`floor_ratio`-by-step-(1-avg_frac)*steps / CONSTANT `floor_ratio` for the last avg_frac of
    training. Over exactly that constant-LR plateau the optimizer keeps a running uniform mean of
    EVERY parameter (Polyak-Ruppert / SWA, Izmailov et al. UAI 2018), and on the final step it
    copies the mean into the live parameters, then re-applies the retraction at eta=1 and the
    spectral cap so the returned weights sit on the same geometry the model trained under. With
    floor_ratio=0.15 and avg_frac=0.25 at a 4000-step budget this averages 1001 iterates drawn at
    a fixed 7.5e-5 learning rate. Neither parent averages anything; both return the last iterate.
    The arch is LayerNorm-only (verified: no BatchNorm anywhere in
    r18_pathstate_latent_transition_worldmodel.py or its r22 subclass), so SWA's usual
    running-statistics recomputation problem does not exist here.


3. WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY

The scored quantity is native_hit - swap_stayed per window: whether the read at a location returns
the content the chain of `mv`s actually routed there, and stops returning it when two contents
exchange their moves. In this arch the only machinery that can carry a content's identity across a
chain is the delta-rule associative memory: `_chunked_delta_reads` writes the observation value
under a write key k = file_write(x_cmd) / path_write(h_cmd) and reads it back with
q = file_read(x_cmd) / path_read(h_cmd), and `_transition_reads` runs a second [key_d, D] memory
addressed by `tr_path`. Whether the content written when `mv A B` executed comes back out when
`cat B` is issued is decided entirely by the inner products q_read . k_write across locations.

That memory is a rank-key_d outer-product store. Its ability to hold N distinct contents at N
distinct locations without cross-talk is bounded by the number of near-orthogonal directions the
key maps actually produce. The training sample confirms N is small but not trivial: a cups window
in evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl exposes five slots (`"N": 5`) at five paths and moves two of
them; the cvar windows add more distinct paths in the same trajectory. If the addressing maps
collapse to a low effective rank — the ordinary fate of an unconstrained linear map trained by a
diffuse contrastive loss — then distinct paths produce nearly collinear keys, every write smears
across every slot, and the read returns a blend. A blend does not carry identity: the native and
swapped chains then produce near-identical reads, native_hit falls, swap_stayed does not, and the
paired difference collapses toward zero. Forcing the addressing maps onto the Stiefel manifold
keeps all 64 key dimensions live while still letting the map DISCARD nuisance directions (a
row-orthonormal 64x176 matrix is exactly a projection onto a chosen 64-dim subspace followed by a
rotation, so the map can still throw away the other 112 directions — it just cannot squash the
ones it keeps). That is the specific failure this addition is aimed at.

One property of this arch makes the intervention unusually safe: the outputs of every one of these
projections are renormalized before use. `_quotient` ends in `_unit(...)`, and q_path/k_path/tr_path
all go through `_unit(...)`. Key MAGNITUDE is therefore discarded by the model, so the Frobenius
rescale in the retraction is cosmetic and the retraction is a pure conditioning intervention with
no gain side effect on the addressing path.

The second addition targets the measurement rather than the model. comp_ca is a mean of 89 per-
window 0/1 differences, so a single window is worth 0.0112 and the parent's whole margin over the
baseline is a couple of windows. Each window's contribution is a discrete argmin over 64 candidates
— a step function of the weights. The last iterate of SGD-like training samples that step function
at one noisy point; a tail average over 1000 plateau iterates evaluates a much lower-variance point
in the same basin, and SWA's documented effect is a shift toward flatter solutions that transfer
better out of distribution. The eval holds windows out BY IMAGE, so out-of-distribution transfer is
exactly what is being asked for. I expect (A) to raise native routing accuracy and (B) to both add
a smaller mean gain and shrink the seed-to-seed spread, which is what a margin has to clear.

Nothing here inspects commands, paths, `mv` edges, or the move chain. The whole impl reads only
parameter shapes and parameter values. The routing answer is not computed anywhere in my code; if
the model does not learn to route, this optimizer has nothing to hand it.


4. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

  - The addressing maps may not be rank-collapsing in the first place. If Muon on that group
    already keeps them well conditioned, the retraction is a near-no-op and (A) contributes
    nothing. This is the single biggest risk and I cannot check it from the jail; I state it as
    unmeasured. What I can say is that Muon constrains updates, not weights, and that the shared
    optim contains no weight-spectrum constraint of any kind on this group.
  - The map may NEED unequal gains inside its retained subspace. Orthogonality forbids the map
    from down-weighting a retained direction relative to another; if path discrimination depends
    on a soft gain profile rather than on subspace choice, eta=0.2 every step will fight the
    gradient and cost accuracy.
  - The group I retract is the same group the shared optim already routes to Muon, selected by the
    same rule (2-D, key_d rows, cols not in {key_d, D}, at least two same-shape siblings). On the
    r22 arch I verified this resolves to six tensors: file_read, file_write, path_read, path_write,
    tr_path AND sys_val, all (64,176). sys_val is the system-summary value projection, not an
    addressing map, and it feeds a tanh where scale matters. I deliberately did not add a new
    heuristic to exclude it — a bare parameter iterator carries no way to tell a bias-free
    addressing Linear from a biased value Linear at the same shape, and inventing one would be a
    guess. So sys_val gets orthogonalized too. The Frobenius rescale preserves its overall gain, so
    I expect this to be neutral-to-mildly-helpful, but if the running system summary degrades, that
    is where it comes from.
  - The plateau LR (0.15 x peak, held for the last quarter) may leave the model under-converged
    relative to the parent's cosine-to-0.05 tail, and the average may not recover the difference.
    Then (B) is a loss, not a gain.
  - The averaged weights are written back only if the optimizer's own step counter reaches the
    `steps` it was constructed with. The harness runs exactly `steps` iterations with one
    `opt.step()` each, so this holds; but a run that exits early on non-finite loss returns the raw
    last iterate, which is the same thing the parent would have returned.


5. WHAT I CHECKED FROM THE JAIL, AND WHAT I DID NOT

  - Speed. On CPU, 300 steps of the real r22 arch + r20 head + r12 objective at B=24, n=12 spent
    3.47s inside the parent's opt.step and 4.20s inside mine (+2.4ms/step: six 64x176
    Newton-Schulz retractions, plus a 3.7M-parameter float32 running mean over the closing
    quarter). That is ~10% of a CPU step where the forward is unusually cheap relative to a GPU
    step; on the scoring hardware I expect the fraction to be smaller, but that is UNMEASURED and
    the CPU number is not a proxy for it.
  - Mechanical verification, not a score: in the same 300-step run the sigma_max/sigma_min of every
    (64,176) projection ended at 3.44-4.12 under the parent's optim and at 1.00 under mine, with
    no non-finite parameters and a matching loss value. That confirms the retraction does what
    section 2(A) says it does. It says NOTHING about comp_ca, which I cannot measure here.
  - Degenerate budgets. The plateau index is clamped into [decay_start+1, steps-1]. I evaluated
    the schedule and plateau index directly at steps = 1, 2, 5, 12, 30, 100, 4000, and ran the
    full r22+r20+r12 loop at steps = 2, 12, 60: the write-back path executes and every parameter
    stays finite in all three. I did not and cannot run the real eval.
