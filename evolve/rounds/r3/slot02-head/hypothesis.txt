CANDIDATE: head = r3_occupancy_routed_copy_transport, arch = r22_retrieval_composition_renderer.
Axes changed relative to the parent: head (new impl), arch (taken from the crossover partner). Objective, optim, target, batcher and stream are the parent's, unchanged.

REVISION NOTE. The first version of this head was reviewed and found not to act at the scored read at all: it indexed everything by (command, observation) PAIR and gated every position on `live_cmd AND live_obs`. At the scored read the command is present and its observation is withheld — that observation IS the answer — so the read position was either gated to zero or, on the odd-length scored layout of 2*max_r+1 tokens, had no pair index at all. Perturbing the read command's own token left the head's output byte-identical. That is fixed below by splitting the pair axis into two axes; the retrieval mechanism, the aux and every parameter are otherwise unchanged. Section (e) states the four design decisions the fix required and section (f) the measurement that the fix acts where the score is taken.

WHAT THE DATA SAYS, because the whole design rests on it. I read evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl (24 sequences, 26-32 steps each). A cups window is: several `cat <path>` exposures, each returning a distinct multi-line body with a canary; then a contiguous block of `mv <src> <dst>` steps; then one `cat <final_path>` test whose output is BYTE-IDENTICAL to the body of one of the exposures. Two facts follow that I checked rather than assumed. (1) Every mutating step's observation is the empty render "cwd=/ exit=0\n" — the move carries no information in its observation at all, only in its command token. Per sequence the empty observation forms one exact-duplicate cluster of 8 to 15 members, while every content that recurs forms an exact-duplicate cluster of exactly 2 or 3. That gap is what my mining thresholds sit in. (2) Because the text is identical, the standardized observation embedding at the test step is exactly equal to the embedding at the exposure step: a move PRESERVES content exactly. The read path in the deep windows is also a near-string-sibling of a DIFFERENT content's resting path (e.g. read `.../sshd_config.cfg.8` while another content still sits at `.../sshd_config.cfg`), which is the name lure the metric is built around.

THE DEFECT I AM FIXING IN THE PARENT'S HEAD. r18_transition_forwardmodel_consistency mines a triple (i, k, j) as "the nearest earlier and nearest later command whose embedding is cosine-similar to command k" and trains f(obs_i, cmd_k) toward obs_j, keeping the triple only when (obs_i - obs_j)^2 is LARGE (change_floor 0.25). In this data the mutating steps are contiguous, so for a move step k the nearest later same-path command j is overwhelmingly another move, whose observation is the empty render — and an empty-vs-content pair passes change_floor with room to spare. So the parent's aux spends most of its weight teaching the arch's shared transition operator to map content to the EMPTY observation, and that operator is applied once per hop inside _transition_reads, so the damage compounds with depth — exactly on the windows the objective scores. That is my explanation for a parent that sits below the unmodified baseline, and it is checkable by reading the two files side by side. I keep the parent's mechanism (mined-triple supervision of the shared transition operator) and invert its target: my triples are pairs of steps whose observations are exact duplicates with a contentless step between them, and the operator is supervised toward CONTENT PRESERVATION, f(obs_at_earlier_duplicate, cmd_at_last_contentless_step) = obs_at_read. That term is in aux_loss under trans_weight and is disabled when the arch exposes no transition_from_emb.

WHAT THE NEW MECHANISM COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES. Both parents route content through an outer-product delta-rule memory (r18 _transition_reads; r2's dual-address transport), which superposes every write into one K-by-D matrix and applies a learned affine edit s*(1+gamma)+beta to the content at every hop. Mine is an explicit slot memory with these properties, none of which they have.

  (a) COPY, NOT EDIT. Every completed (command, observation) pair opens exactly one write slot. Its value is V_j = m_j * r_j + (1 - m_j) * obs_j, where r_j is what command j retrieved from earlier slots and m_j is a learned gate over (cmd_j, obs_j). When m_j is 1 the slot stores the retrieved content verbatim — no learned transform stands between the source content and the destination slot, so a five-hop chain applies the identity five times instead of five learned affine maps. Content identity across a chain is exactly what the data does and exactly what the metric measures.

  (b) THE RECURSION IS SOLVED, NOT UNROLLED. r_j = sum_{i<j} att_ji V_i and V_j = m_j r_j + (1-m_j) obs_j together are (I - m*att) V = (1-m)*obs with m*att strictly lower triangular over the write slots, which the code solves in closed form with one unit-lower-triangular solve. Composition depth is therefore not bounded by the number of transformer layers, by a chunk size, or by any fixed unroll — depth 6 costs the same as depth 1. This is the piece aimed straight at the d4+ half of the eligible slice. I checked the solve numerically: the returned V satisfies V - (m*att@V + (1-m)*obs) to 1.2e-7 max residual, and the write-block of att is exactly zero on and above the diagonal.

  (c) THE SOURCE OF A MOVE IS FOUND BY OCCUPANCY, NOT BY PARSING POSITION OUT OF A POOLED VECTOR. This is the crux and it is why I did not build a src/dst dual extractor. The command embedding is a mean-pooled sentence vector, so `mv X Y` is close to a blend of X and Y and I do not believe two linear extractors can reliably tell which half is which. I never ask them to. At a move step the query is the single path signature of the whole command; it scores against slots, and only the SOURCE has a slot, because the destination is a fresh name that no earlier command wrote. Occupancy does the disambiguation at run time, from the model's own memory state. That is expressed in code as the log-occupancy bias log(occ_j) added to the scores plus a null column with a learned logit, so a query that matches nothing retrieves ~0 instead of a blur. The destination slot is then the move command's own slot, and it is found later by `cat Y` because the mv command literally contains the whole string Y: a full-path match beats the prefix-only match a name-sibling lure can offer.

  (d) HUBNESS-CORRECTED ADDRESSING (the mechanism imported from outside this problem). All the paths in one window share a long prefix, so all path signatures pile onto one dominant direction and cosine retrieval degenerates — the hubness pathology that all-but-the-top / mean-centering fixes in word- and sentence-embedding retrieval. I subtract, from the QUERY only, sigmoid(center_logit) times the causal running mean of the command signatures seen so far in this sequence, then renormalize. Centering the keys by the same per-row mean would cancel inside the softmax, so query-side centering is the complete correction and it costs one cumsum. Its effect is that a slot whose address is generic for this window gets the minimum score for every query, and the discriminative suffix mass — the ".8" that separates the target's chain from the lure — is what survives to decide the match.

  (e) TWO AXES, SO A COMMAND WITHOUT AN OBSERVATION STILL ASKS ITS QUESTION. This is the revision. For a stream of L tokens there are n_read = (L+1)//2 READ positions, one per command token, and n_write = L//2 WRITE slots, one per completed command/observation pair — the same two counts the r22 arch itself keeps as n_cmd and n_pair. Attention is the rectangular [n_read, n_write] matrix with reachable[i,j] = (j < i), the value solve is the square n_write system described in (b), and the read vector at position i is row i of att applied to V. The four decisions this forced, each of which a reviewer can check against the code:
      1. WHAT THE QUERY IS BUILT FROM. The query at position i is built from that command's embedding ALONE: p_i = unit(addr(gelu(cmd_feat(c_i)))), centered as in (d), masked only by whether the COMMAND token is live. No observation, at any position, enters any query. So the query at the scored read is the same function of the same inputs it was at every training read, and the withheld observation is not an input it is missing — it was never an input.
      2. WHETHER A WRITE STILL NEEDS AN OBSERVATION. Yes. A write slot exists only where the command AND its observation are both live, because a slot's value is a blend that contains (1-m)*obs and a missing observation would write the zero vector into memory as if it were content. A read needs only its command; a write needs both. In training the two conditions coincide exactly (collate clears key_pad for a command and its observation together), so this split changes nothing about training and only takes effect at the scored read — where the read command reads and, correctly, writes nothing, since no later position reads it.
      3. WHAT THE CENTERING STATISTICS INCLUDE. The running mean is over the signatures of every LIVE COMMAND up to and including position i, count taken over the same set. Observations never enter it, so it is the same statistic at train and at the scored read; it is strictly causal (no later command touches the query at i); and it includes the current command, as it did in training, so the read is centered by the same rule as every other position rather than by a special case.
      4. HOW A POSITION WITH NO PAIR IS INDEXED. It is indexed on the read axis, which has one entry per command token and therefore always has an entry for it — including the final command of an odd-length stream, whose read index is n_read-1 = n_write. Nothing is indexed by pair any more except the write slots, where a pair is exactly what a slot is. The contribution tensor now has n_read rows and is added to all n_read columns of pred[:, 0::2, :], so the last command position of the scored layout receives a contribution instead of being truncated away.

  (f) MEASURED: THE HEAD NOW ACTS AT THE SCORED READ. I built the scored layout — L = 2*max_r+1, the final command with no observation after it, plus shorter rows whose read command is live with its observation slot padded — and drove the module directly. Perturbing a row's own read command across five random embeddings changes the head's output at that read by relative 0.66 / 1.02 / 1.37 / 1.53 / 0.95 on the row whose read is the final token, and by a similar band on the two short rows; the same measurement on the reviewed version was exactly 0.0 on the final-token row. Perturbing head parameters (addr, cmd_feat, occ_gate, null_logit) moves the prediction at the scored read on every row. The retrieval is not collapsing onto the null column at that position: attention mass on real slots at the final read is 0.73. Causality survives — perturbing the read command changes no earlier position by anything (max |change| 0.0). Wrapped-equals-unwrapped at init still holds bit-for-bit, since out_scale is zeros. Those are checks of my own module on a synthetic tensor of the scored SHAPE; they establish that the mechanism operates at that position and are not a claim about the score.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca RATHER THAN SOME OTHER NUMBER. comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed on the same board with the same read token. A predictor keyed on the read location is identical in both arms and scores exactly zero. My head's prediction at the read is the arch's prediction plus the content standing in the slot that the chain of writes deposited there, and by (e) that read is issued by the read command itself, so re-encoding the move commands to swap two contents' move-position sets changes which content the matched slot holds and therefore changes the prediction — the two arms come apart. Nothing in the head resolves a move chain in Python: aux_loss mines only WHICH STEPS to train on (duplicate observations, contentless steps) and its positive target for a mined step is that step's own observation, which is already the main loss's target, so it adds no information the harness was not already supervising. The forward pass sees only frozen embeddings; it never sees a command string, and could not parse a path if it wanted to.

WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH PROGRAM. From the parent: the objective (r22_exact_target_equivalence_quotient — the cups data contains exact-duplicate targets by construction, and quotienting them into one class stops the expose/test pair being pushed apart as a false negative, which the partner's r12 ring objective does not do), the routed-collision-depth batcher (it buckets by hop count, so the deep windows the metric weighs are oversampled), and the transition-consistency aux idea, retargeted as described. From the partner: the r22 composition renderer arch, whose zero-init RMS-capped nonlinear composition over the retrieved-content channels is the piece that lets a retrieved content interact vector-wise with the trunk state instead of only scalar-mixing — which is what a copy-transport read needs on the way into the prediction. The partner's r6 image-blocking batcher is dropped because the parent's batcher already blocks by image inside each bucket.

INITIALISATION AND COST. out_scale is zeros, so the wrapped net's prediction is the unwrapped arch's prediction bit-for-bit at step 0 (checked) and the head can only earn its way in. Extra forward cost is dominated by two Linear(768, 128) maps over the ~32 command and ~31 observation positions, roughly a quarter of the arch's own per-step cost by parameter count; the triangular solve and the attentions are on [B, ~32, ~32] and are negligible. I could not run the harness from this jail, so that is an arithmetic estimate, not a measurement, and the same goes for every claim above about what the score will do.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL.
  1. The addressing bet is wrong: if the frozen encoder's pooled embedding of `mv X Y` is not closer, after query centering, to the embedding of `cat Y` than the lure's slot key is, the query lands on the lure and the head routes the wrong content. I cannot test this without the encoder. If it fails, the aux drives out_scale toward zero and the candidate lands near the arch alone. This is now the load-bearing risk: the read issues a real query, so the query being aimed at the wrong slot is the way the mechanism loses rather than the way it does nothing.
  2. The mutation gate fails to separate contentless steps: then a move's slot stores a blend containing the empty observation and the leak compounds over hops. The empty render is a single exact vector, so I expect it to be trivially separable, but it is the gate's job, not mine, and it is unverified.
  3. My duplicate/contentless mining is miscalibrated on the real training root. It is calibrated to a measured gap on the sample (content clusters of size 2-3 against an empty cluster of 8-15, threshold max_dup=3 at 5% of the row's mean pairwise distance). If some other pack has many near-identical short contents, they get misread as contentless, the aux mines fewer windows, and the head degrades toward inert rather than harmful.
  4. My reading of the parent's defect is wrong — if the parent's aux was not in fact training the transition operator toward the empty observation, then a large part of the expected gain over the parent evaporates and only the transport head itself is left.
  5. Overwrite semantics: I deliberately do not erase the source slot after a move, on the reasoning that stale slots in a chain hold the same content and are harmless, while erasing does nothing about the competing chain that supplies the lure. If a pack contains paths that are genuinely rewritten with new content, the stale slot competes with the fresh one and retrieval degrades with sequence length.
  6. NEW, from the split in (e): the read at the scored position is the only read in the model's life whose command was never itself a write. In training every read position is also a write slot, so the query direction and the key direction at a position are the same vector p_i used two ways, and the gates that shape occupancy always saw an observation alongside. If training somehow couples read quality to a position's own slot existing — I can see no path by which it does, since the query at i never looks at slot i (reachable is strictly j < i) — then the scored read is out of distribution in a way training cannot expose. The query function itself is provably identical at train and eval by decision (e)(1), so this is a residual worry about the learned gates, not about the wiring.
