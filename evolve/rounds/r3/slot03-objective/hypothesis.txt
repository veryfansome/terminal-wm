1. WHAT THE DATA FORCES, READ OFF evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl

A cups window is: several `cat <path_i>` steps whose observation is content_i (expose); then
one or more `mv <src> <dst>` steps whose observation is EMPTY ("cwd=/ exit=0" and nothing
else, so the mv OBSERVATION token carries no information at all and only the mv COMMAND
token names the two addresses); then a `cat <final_loc>` step (role cups_test) whose
observation is whichever content the move chain routed to final_loc. The same shape recurs
in the cvar windows with redirections instead of mv. So the only route from the read command
to the right answer runs: read-command -> the mv command tokens -> the expose observation
token that holds that content.

Two properties of these observations matter for a loss:
  (a) Every one of them is a `cat` of a small text file. A large part of each target
      embedding is the "generic answer" - the cwd/exit prefix, the file's format, the fact
      that it is a short config-ish blob - and that part is largely predictable from the
      command text alone, per row, with no history. The part that actually distinguishes the
      N contents of a window is a small canary-carrying remainder.
  (b) A large mass of rows (every mv, every redirect op) has a target that is literally the
      same string, hence the same embedding, as many other rows.


2. THE MECHANISM I ADDED, AND WHAT IT COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES

Both programs I was given use r12_antiretrieval_ring_negatives: an in-batch listwise
softmax over squared L2 distances from pred_i to every candidate target t_j, with the
negatives band-pass weighted by target-target distance. Every distance in it is a RAW
distance. Nothing in either program conditions on the command that produced the row.

I opt into the WANTS_CTX extension and add a Frisch-Waugh-Lovell / double-machine-learning
orthogonalization step (Chernozhukov et al.'s partialling-out: regress the nuisance out of
both sides, then estimate on the residuals). Per minibatch, under no_grad:

  - basis = [ctx["cmd"], 1]  ->  [n, D+1].  ctx["cmd"] is the model's own causal command
    input for that row (stream.extract_cmd_input), so this is a per-row function of causal
    inputs only; the no-future-leakage property is untouched.
  - a linear kernel-ridge map basis -> tgt is fit CROSS-FITTED: rows of even global index get
    their fitted value from a map fit on the odd rows and vice versa, so no row's fitted
    value is fit on itself. Ridge lambda = 0.1 * mean(diag(gram)), scale-free.
  - shift_i = rho * fitted_i with rho = 0.9, and BOTH sides are shifted:
        pres = pred - shift,   tres = tgt - shift
    before any distance, ring or logit is formed.

The algebra that makes this the right operation, and which is what a reviewer should check
against the code:

    d_res(i,i) = || (p_i - s_i) - (t_i - s_i) ||^2  =  || p_i - t_i ||^2      UNCHANGED
    d_res(i,j) = || (p_i - t_j) - (s_i - s_j) ||^2                            CHANGED

The positive term is algebraically identical to the parent's, so absolute calibration in the
raw observation space - the space the eval's squared-L2 decision actually lives in - is not
touched. What changes is only the geometry of the NEGATIVES: candidate j is pulled toward
anchor i by exactly the amount of the difference t_i - t_j that a per-row linear map of the
two commands already explains. Two window-mates whose observations differ mainly because
they are cats of two different paths become a near-collapsed pair; the loss can only
separate them on the command-UNEXPLAINED remainder, which is the canary content. That is
precisely quantity (a) above, and it is what neither r12 nor r22 computes: they weight
negatives by how far apart two targets are, this one weights them by how far apart two
targets are AFTER removing what their two commands account for.

I verified the diagonal invariance numerically (max |d_raw(i,i) - d_res(i,i)| = 6e-8 in
float32 on a 1600x768 synthetic) and that off-diagonal distances contract while the diagonal
does not.

Three consequences of the shift are handled explicitly in the code rather than left to luck:
  - The temperature, the ring bandwidth lam, the repulsion margin and its softness tau are
    all written as FRACTIONS of the batch's mean off-diagonal distance in whichever space
    they are measured in (_TEMP_FRAC 0.125, _LAM_FRAC 0.5, _MARGIN_FRAC 0.25, _TAU_FRAC
    0.125). Orthogonalization compresses the distance scale, and with the parent's absolute
    _TEMP = 0.25 that compression would silently raise the softmax temperature. On the raw
    standardized scale (mean off-diagonal ~ 2) these fractions reproduce the parent's 0.25 /
    0.5 / 0.25 exactly, so in the rho -> 0 limit the objective degenerates to the parent's.
  - The duplicate suppression and the multi-positive equivalence classes are keyed on the RAW
    target distance tt_raw, not the residual one, because "these two observations are the
    same string" (property (b)) is a fact about the data. After shifting, two mv rows with
    identical targets no longer sit at distance zero from each other, so a residual-space
    duplicate test would turn them into mutual false negatives. Numerically-equal targets are
    pooled into one class, get inverse-class-size candidate mass and a logsumexp numerator
    over the class (this part is a recombination of r22_exact_target_equivalence_quotient's
    idea, moved onto a raw-distance criterion inside a residualized objective).
  - Rows are weighted as anchors by energy_i = mean(tres_i^2) through the gate
    energy/(energy + 0.25*median(energy)). A row whose observation is entirely explained by
    its own command carries nothing to track, so it stops competing for listwise gradient
    with the rows that do. In this data that reallocates the contrastive budget away from the
    "mv -> empty output" mass and onto the expose/test reads.

The raw untouched squared-error anchor ((pred-tgt)^2).mean() is kept at weight 0.05 and is
the anti-collapse guarantee: a constant prediction cannot minimize it. Measured on a
1600x768 synthetic with a duplicate-heavy target set: constant 9.05, mediocre 5.03,
near-perfect 1.44 - monotone in prediction quality.

Cost, measured: forward+backward at n=1600, D=768 on CPU is 59 ms against the parent's 25 ms
(two ridge solves of ~800x769 plus one extra no-grad n x n Gram for the raw duplicate test).
This is an unmeasured extrapolation to the scoring box, but on a GPU lane those are
milliseconds against a step that already forms two n x n x D products, so I do not expect the
fixed step budget to be at risk. I have not run the real harness.


3. WHY THIS IS A CROSSOVER AND NOT JUST A LOSS TWEAK

The partner's unique contribution is r23_dual_address_transport_pointer: a source/destination
address pair per command, a delta-rule content store that reads at the source, writes the
read value at the destination and erases the source, and a pointer softmax that copies ONE
raw earlier observation embedding verbatim into the command-position prediction through a
zero-init readout. That is a machine built to do exactly the mv chain, and at init it
contributes literally nothing (zero-init tp_out, tp_gate biased to -2).

The parent's unique contribution is the r12 ring-negative listwise family, which is what
makes close-but-distinct window-mates the thing being discriminated at all.

They are complementary rather than merely co-present, and the new objective is the thing that
couples them. Under a raw-distance loss most of the loss magnitude sits in the generic
component of each observation, which the transformer trunk can produce on its own from the
command token; a zero-init copy path has to compete against an already-adequate trunk for a
share of a gradient that is mostly about the generic part, which is a weak reason for its gate
to open. After orthogonalization the generic component is removed from the negatives'
geometry and the down-weighting removes the uninformative rows, so nearly all of the listwise
gradient is about the one thing the trunk structurally cannot supply and the pointer-copy path
structurally can: which earlier observation is the right one to reproduce. The loss is
reshaped to pay for the arch's mechanism.


4. WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

Nothing here resolves a move chain and hands the answer to the model. The nuisance map is
linear, per-row, and its only input is that row's own command embedding plus a bias - it has
no access to any other step, so it cannot represent "which content is at final_loc" even in
principle. Its output is never added to any model input; it is subtracted from BOTH sides of a
distance under no_grad and cancels exactly on the diagonal. The model's prediction, the
targets, and the eval are untouched.

Worth stating because it is the closest thing to a leak: the ridge is fit on the batch's
targets. If a command string appears twice in a batch with the same observation (it does -
`cat /tmp/w/cvar/g3/out.dat` is read twice in the sample trajectory), the map can learn that
pair and subtract most of that row's content. The effect is that such a row loses signal and
is then down-weighted by the energy gate; it cannot gain the model information, because
nothing computed here reaches the model.


5. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

  - If the generic component of a cups observation is NOT well approximated by a linear
    function of the frozen command embedding, the fitted shift is small, the negatives are
    barely re-weighted, and the candidate degenerates to the parent's objective with a
    relative temperature - a null result, not a regression. My synthetic sweep is the honest
    evidence here and it is not the real data: with a fast-decaying command spectrum the
    residual aligned with the content component at cos 0.91, with a slowly-decaying one only
    0.48. Real frozen sentence-encoder embeddings are strongly anisotropic, which is the
    favourable case, but I could not measure it.
  - If the ridge over-fits and removes the content as well as the generic part, the residual
    targets collapse, the energy gate zeroes most rows, and the objective reduces to the 0.05
    raw MSE anchor alone - which would be a real regression. Cross-fitting plus lambda =
    0.1 * mean(diag(gram)) is the guard; the failure signature would be a training loss that
    falls to roughly 0.05 * MSE with no contrastive component.
  - If the eval's discriminative work is dominated by the generic component after all - i.e.
    if the N foils within a window differ mostly in format rather than in canary - then
    deliberately refusing to reward that component costs accuracy.
  - If r23's transport/pointer path never opens its gate for reasons unrelated to the loss
    (optimization, the -2.0 tp_gate bias, the step budget), the coupling argument in section 3
    does not fire and only the objective's own effect remains.
  - The two seeds' worth of run-to-run spread on this measurement is not visible to me, so a
    small positive would not be distinguishable from luck. The mechanism is aimed at a large
    move (it changes which rows and which pairs receive essentially all of the contrastive
    gradient), and if it only moves the number slightly it should be read as not having fired.
