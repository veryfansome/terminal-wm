CANDIDATE: boardpair_swap_difference (axis objective) + r3_cuebalanced_routing_blocks (axis batcher)

WHAT THE CROSSOVER IS

The two programs I was given differ on exactly one axis: the parent trains with
r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum (image-blocked hard negatives, hard_frac_max 0.75, ramp_frac 0.3),
the partner with r3_cuebalanced_routing_blocks (same image-blocked hard-negative mechanism, same
two knob values, plus per-sequence weights that rake the chain-order marginals of the deepest
routed read toward uniform inside hop-count strata, plus read-collision blocks). Read as code,
r3 contains r6's mechanism and adds one: it makes routed reads dense and order-balanced in every
batch. I take r3 for that reason, and keep every other axis of the parent. The invention is on my
own axis, and it is built to consume exactly what r3 supplies: a loss whose new terms only fire on
rows where a content has been re-read after moving, and which therefore gets more signal per step
the denser those rows are. Parent's objective mechanism is kept whole as the backbone.

WHAT THE MECHANISM IS, CONCRETELY

Backbone (unchanged from the parent's r12): per-dimension precision weighting, focal listwise
softmax over in-batch squared-L2 distances with confusability-ring negative reweighting, gated
repulsion hinge, small MSE anchor.

New, and neither parent computes any of it:

1. Exact trajectory segmentation inside the loss. The harness builds ctx["prev"] as
   cat(zeros, tgt[:, :-1]) over the padded [B, maxn] target block and then selects with cmd_mask,
   so the flattened rows are sequence-major and the first row of every sequence has prev exactly
   equal to the zero vector while every other row has prev equal to the preceding standardized
   observation. _segment_ids reads that off with prev.abs().amax(1) == 0 and a cumsum, recovering
   the trajectory partition of the batch exactly. (Verified on synthetic batches: 32 sequences in,
   32 distinct segment ids out.) Every new term below is confined to within-segment pairs. The
   parent has no notion of a trajectory; the closest archived mechanism uses a fixed +/-32 band in
   flattened order, which straddles sequence boundaries and misses long ones.

2. Content-repeat rows. Row i is a content repeat when some earlier row j of the SAME segment has
   an exactly duplicated target (squared distance below 0.5% of the batch target scale) and the
   command embeddings of i and j are not the same command (cosine < 0.999), and when the
   batch-wide duplicate count of i's target is at most 12. In the training data
   (evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl) this selects precisely the cups_test / cvar_test reads:
   `cat /tmp/w/cups/usr/share/misc/magic.txt.1` whose output is byte-identical to the earlier
   `cat /tmp/w/cups/g53/f149.tmp`, and `cat /tmp/w/cvar/g3/out.dat` after
   `cat .../s1.dat > .../out.dat`. The duplicate-count cap is what excludes the huge cluster of
   observations that render as "cwd=/ exit=0" with empty output — every mv and every redirection
   step produces one, so that cluster is hundreds of rows per batch, an order of magnitude above
   the cap, while a genuine content is duplicated two or three times. The same-command test
   excludes plain re-reads of an unmoved path (`cat /tmp/w/cvar/g0/s1.dat` issued twice), which
   are name-keyed memory rather than routing.

3. Board pairs and the two new terms. A pair is two content-repeat rows in the same segment whose
   targets are different contents. For such a pair, with dt = t_a - t_b, dp = p_a - p_b, and
   den = ||dt||^2 floored at 1% of the typical squared row separation:

       diff = relu( 1 - <dp, dt> / den )
       swap = relu( m - <p_a - (t_a+t_b)/2, dt> / den ) + relu( m + <p_b - (t_a+t_b)/2, dt> / den )

   with m = 0.25, each averaged over pairs, each added with weight 0.5.

   `diff` is the regression coefficient of the prediction difference on the target difference,
   hinged at one. `swap` is the eval's own decision rule written out: ||p_a - t_a||^2 <
   ||p_a - t_b||^2 with a margin gamma*||dt||^2 is algebraically identical to
   <p_a - (t_a+t_b)/2, dt> >= (gamma/2)*||dt||^2, so the hinge demands, in the unweighted
   squared-L2 variable the probe actually ranks with, that each of the two predictions land on its
   own content's side of the midpoint between the two contents that share this board.

WHAT THIS COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES, AND WHY IT SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

The division by den is the point. The frozen encoder maps two canary files built from the same
template to nearly the same vector: the board's contents differ from each other in a subspace whose
scale is small next to the overall spread of observation embeddings. Plain MSE, and any fixed-
temperature distance contrastive including the parent's, allocate gradient in proportion to squared
distance, so almost none of their budget lands in that subspace — the model is rewarded far more for
producing something of roughly the right shape than for producing the right content. Dividing each
pair's inner product by that pair's own squared separation rescales every board pair to unit
importance regardless of how close its two contents sit. Two contents that are nearly identical in
embedding space now carry the same weight as two that are far apart. That is a reweighting of where
the gradient goes, not a new supervision signal, and it is the reweighting the measured quantity
asks for: comp_ca is decided entirely by which of several same-board, same-format contents the
prediction is nearest to.

The projection form also makes the term blind to isotropic prediction error: an unbiased noisy
predictor contributes zero to <dp, dt> in expectation, so `diff` does not double as a second
regression loss fighting the backbone, and once the model does carry the difference (coefficient
>= 1, margin met) both terms hit their hinges and their gradient switches off by itself. Measured
on synthetic batches carrying the same shape as the real windows (96 board pairs): a perfect
predictor scores diff 0.000 / swap 0.000; the same predictor plus half a unit of isotropic noise per
dimension scores diff 0.048 / swap 0.000; a constant predictor and a board-mean predictor both score
exactly diff 1.000 / swap 0.500; and a predictor that has the two board contents interchanged scores
diff 1.516 / swap 1.016, strictly worse than the constant. Anti-collapse safety is therefore
structural on the new terms as well as on the backbone: a constant prediction sits at a fixed
positive value that any content-carrying prediction beats.

Why this should show up in native_hit - swap_stayed rather than in absolute accuracy: the pair terms
never reward being close to the board's contents in general, only being closer to THIS location's
content than to the partner's, and the two rows of a pair carry the same read verb, the same
directory, the same file format and the same board. Everything shared cancels in dt. What is left
for the model to use is which mv or redirection command wrote the path being read, which is the only
thing that distinguishes the two rows, and it is exactly the thing the role-swapped arm re-encodes.
A model that improves on this term has to be reading the move commands; a model that keys on the
read path alone cannot improve on it at all, because within a trajectory the mapping from path to
content is a fresh random draw, so there is nothing about the path to memorize.

COST

Reuses the target-target distance matrix the backbone already forms; no second n-by-n matmul is
taken, and the command embeddings are compared only on the sparse set of candidate duplicate pairs,
never as a full n-by-n product. The extra work is a handful of n-by-n boolean masks, one nonzero()
over a sparse mask, and O(pairs * D) gathers. Measured on CPU at n = 1536 rows: 0.0234 s per
forward+backward for the backbone alone, 0.0323 s with the new terms, i.e. +38% on the loss
function in isolation. The loss is a small share of a training step next to the transformer's own
forward and backward, and the added work is elementwise boolean over n-by-n plus a few
pair-sized gathers, which are the operations a GPU handles best, so I expect the step-budget impact
to be small — but that expectation is unmeasured, and the honest measured figure is the +38% above,
on CPU, on the loss alone.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL

- If content-repeat rows are rare in real batches, the term is starved. In the sample trajectory
  three rows qualify and they carry two distinct contents, giving two pairs per trajectory, so a
  64-sequence batch should give of order 130 pairs; my synthetic runs used 192. If the real number
  is near zero the candidate degrades exactly to its parent's objective on a different batcher, and
  the round learns that instead. If it is much larger than 4096 the pair list is strided down.
- If the two contents on a board are genuinely indistinguishable to the frozen encoder, den falls
  to the floor and the term amplifies a direction that carries no information; the 1%-of-scale
  floor bounds the amplification but cannot make an uninformative direction informative.
- If the weight 0.5 on each new term is too high, general next-observation accuracy degrades and
  native_hit falls faster than swap_stayed, which would push comp_ca down rather than up. On a
  synthetic batch with a partially-correct predictor (pred = 0.3*tgt + noise, 1536 rows, 192 pairs)
  the backbone's gradient wrt pred has norm 0.0079 and the two weighted pair terms contribute
  0.0173, about 2.2x, concentrated on the eighth of rows that appear in a pair. That is deliberately
  aggressive: a timid setting cannot beat the parent by more than measurement noise, and the hinges
  switch the pressure off by themselves once the difference is carried. It is also the main risk,
  and the ratio on real batches is unmeasured.
- If the harness ever supplies a non-identity target chunk, exact target duplication would stop
  identifying a repeated content and the term would select the wrong rows. This genome uses the
  identity target, and the terms are skipped entirely whenever ctx is absent.

NOT DONE, DELIBERATELY: nothing here resolves a move chain and hands the answer to the model. The
loss only ever looks at targets, at command embeddings for an equality test, and at the previous-
observation channel for segmentation, all to decide which rows to weight and how. The model's input
is untouched, and the loss does not run at scoring time at all, so nothing in it is gated on an
observation that the probe withholds.
