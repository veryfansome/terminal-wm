CANDIDATE: r25 — routing-depth systematic curriculum (batcher) x span-occluded closure transport (arch)

WHAT THE TWO PROGRAMS EACH CONTRIBUTE

The two programs I was given share objective, optim and batcher. They differ on arch and head. One
carries ramped stochastic OBSERVATION OCCLUSION over a path-state trunk; the other carries a
REFERENCE-CLOSURE TRANSPORT branch that resolves a whole chain of content references in one
triangular solve, with a head that also supervises the shared transition operator on the arch's own
memory content. Picking either arch throws away the other's mechanism. This candidate keeps both:
the transport branch is the trunk, and the occlusion is applied to it — upgraded, because the
weakness of iid occlusion is exactly what the transport branch is for. It also keeps the strictly
larger of the two heads (r20 contains the r18 head verbatim and adds the memory-pre arm, which is
live on this trunk — I checked that _MEM_ATTRS and pos_scale all resolve on the closure arch).
On my own axis, both programs carry the same image-blocking batcher; that mechanism is kept and a
new one is added on top of it.


MECHANISM 1 — batcher: routing depth read off the commands, then Madow systematic PPS
File: batcher__r25_routing_depth_systematic_curriculum.py

At construction the batcher parses each training sequence's COMMAND STRINGS (s["cmds"], which the
encoder keeps on every fit dict) into content-routing edges: `mv SRC DST` and `cp SRC DST` give an
edge SRC->DST, and any command containing a shell redirection gives an edge from each absolute-path
argument on the left of `>`/`>>` to the file on the right. Scanning the commands in order and
carrying a per-path hop counter (mv moves the counter off SRC onto DST; cp/redirect takes the max
over sources and writes DST) yields ONE number per sequence: the longest content-routing chain the
sequence describes. On the real training trajectories in evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl this
number reproduces the window's own recorded routed depth on 20 of 24 sequences and over-estimates
by taking the max over all contents on the other 4 — it is the deepest chain present, which is the
statistic I want.

That number is the sampling weight exponent base: w_i = (1 + min(d_i, depth_cap)) ** alpha(step),
with alpha smoothstep-ramped from 0 to alpha_max over the first depth_ramp_frac of training. Batches
therefore start uniform and end tilted toward deep chains.

The draw itself is not multinomial. Each step the pool is randomly permuted, then STABLE-SORTED by
depth, the weights are cumulated, and a Madow systematic probability-proportional-to-size sample is
taken: with W the total weight and m the number of picks, a single uniform phase u ~ U(0, W/m) is
drawn and the picks are the elements whose cumulative weight first exceeds u + j*W/m for
j = 0..m-1. This is survey sampling's systematic PPS design, not the ML default; it gives exactly
the same first-order inclusion probabilities as weighted sampling with replacement, but because the
list is ordered by the variable of interest, the number of picks drawn from each depth level is
pinned to within one of its expectation every single step. The random permutation before the sort
keeps WHICH sequence fills a given depth slot fully random, so no pair of sequences is
systematically co-sampled or excluded.

The parent's mechanism is retained around this: a ramped fraction hard_frac_max of the batch is
restricted to n_block_images sampled images so the contrastive objective keeps its dense same-system
in-batch negatives, and that blocked share is drawn by the same depth-weighted systematic rule.

WHAT IT COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PROGRAM COMPUTES: a per-sequence longest-routing-chain statistic, and
a batch whose depth composition is a near-deterministic function of the step rather than a random
variable. Both programs sample sequences iid (uniform, or uniform within a blocked image pool) and
neither looks at the commands at all.

WHY IT SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY: the measurement is a paired difference on windows of
depth 3 and depth 4+, and it rewards only carrying a content's identity through a chain. In the real
training trajectories the routed depth is spread over 1..6, with about half the sequences at depth
<= 2 — chains too short to require carrying anything, since a one-hop move is solvable by local
adjacency. Uniform sequence sampling therefore spends about half of the transport gradient on the
regime the score does not test. Setting alpha_max = 1.5 shifts the sampled depth mass so the mean
sampled depth goes from 3.12 to 3.82 (measured on the jail's 24 real sequences replicated to 240 to
imitate a split, blocking off), i.e. deep-chain exposure roughly doubles relative to depth<=2
exposure, without starving any sequence: a depth-1 sequence keeps ~1/6.5 the selection rate of a
depth-6 one, and a sequence with no routing edges at all keeps ~1/18, so image-holdout breadth is
preserved and every sequence is still seen. The systematic draw is what makes that shift usable
rather than noisy: measured on the same replicated set with
blocking off, the step-to-step standard deviation of the batch's mean routing depth is 0.032 under
this sampler against 0.179 under multinomial sampling with identical weights and 0.168 under the
baseline's uniform iid — a 5.5x reduction in the variance of the very quantity being curricularised.
Under a fixed step budget, a curriculum whose per-step realisation is that noisy is mostly a
different noise process; pinning it is what turns the tilt into a schedule.

NOT A LEAK: the parsed depth is used only to weight WHICH sequences are put in a batch. It is never
written into any tensor, never reaches collate, never reaches the loss, and is not computed at
scoring time at all — the batcher exists only inside the training loop. Nothing in the model's input
differs from what it would be under the baseline batcher; only the frequency with which sequences
appear differs. The rule I am staying inside is the one that permits parsing commands to choose
training SAMPLES and forbids putting resolved routing into the model's input.


MECHANISM 2 — arch: contiguous-SPAN occlusion instead of iid occlusion
File: arch__r25_span_occluded_closure_transport.py

Subclass of the reference-closure transport arch. In training mode only, before the parent forward
runs, a per-row set of spans is drawn over the observation token positions: span lengths from a
clamped geometric law (L = 1 + floor(log U / log q), q = 1 - 1/span_mean, clamped to span_max) and
the span count set to round(p * n_pair / span_mean) so the expected occluded share is p; every
observation token inside a span is zeroed and key-padded. p is smoothstep-ramped from 0 to occ_p
between occ_ramp_start and occ_ramp_end forward passes, and everything is gated on self.training, so
the eval forward is bit-for-bit the parent's. Zero new trainable parameters (verified: parameter
count identical to the unmodified arch), and no change to loss, head or batcher.

WHAT IT COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PROGRAM COMPUTES: gaps. One program occludes each observation
independently, so at its occlusion rate a hole is almost always length 1; the other never occludes
at all. Under this implementation, measured at occ_p = 0.15 with span_mean = 3 over 2000 rows of 31
observation slots, the realised occluded share is 0.159 and 68% of the occluded tokens sit in runs
of 3 or more consecutive slots. The distinction matters because with independent holes the surviving neighbour observation is one
attention hop away, and a trunk can bridge a single hole by local interpolation without ever using
the reference graph. A contiguous gap of several steps removes that shortcut: the only surviving
route across the gap is the command-side reference closure, which is scored from raw command
embeddings and is unaffected by the occlusion. So the occlusion budget is spent applying gradient
pressure exactly to the branch that does the routing (ref_gate / ref_read / ref_own), instead of
being absorbed by the local-copy path.

WHY IT SHOULD MOVE THE MEASURED QUANTITY: at the scored position the model is asked what content
sits at a location after a silent chain of moves; the mv steps that make up the chain carry
observations that are present but content-free, so the answer is only reachable by transporting the
content from the read that exposed it, several steps earlier, through the command graph. Training
under contiguous gaps is training on prefixes where the nearest informative observation is several
steps away, which is the same shape of problem. The score is a paired native-minus-swapped
difference, and only genuine content carriage survives both arms, so a trunk that has been pushed
onto the reference branch rather than onto local copying should gain on the difference and not only
on the native arm.


WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

- If the training split's routing depths are far more concentrated than the jail sample suggests
  (say nearly all sequences already depth >= 3), the depth tilt is close to a no-op and the batcher
  contributes only the variance reduction, which is a second-order effect. This is the single
  assumption I could not check beyond 24 real sequences.
- If deep-chain sequences are systematically correlated with a small subset of images, tilting
  toward depth narrows the effective image diversity and hurts the image-held-out transfer that the
  whole measurement depends on. The (1 + d)^1.5 form with depth_cap = 6 keeps the shallowest
  sequence at ~1/6 the rate of the deepest rather than at zero specifically to bound this, but a
  strong depth/image correlation would still bite.
- Contiguous occlusion destroys strictly more information per unit of budget than iid occlusion:
  when the occluded span covers the ONLY exposure of a content that is read later, that read becomes
  unpredictable and the gradient is noise. If most cups exposures are single-shot (they are), the
  useful fraction of spans is the fraction that lands between an exposure and a read rather than on
  the exposure itself. If occ_p = 0.15 with mean span 3 puts too much mass on exposures, the
  regulariser turns into label noise and this loses to the iid version.
- The image-blocking share is inherited at hard_frac_max = 0.75 with n_block_images = 1, so 75% of a
  late batch comes from one image. If the depth tilt and that blocking interact badly — one image's
  pool being shallow, so the blocked share collapses onto very few sequences — the effective batch
  diversity falls. The systematic draw inside the blocked pool bounds how concentrated it can get,
  but does not eliminate it.
- I could not run the real harness. Everything above about speed and correctness was checked on the
  jail's real trajectories with the real chunk modules (10 full train steps through
  objective + target + optim + head with finite loss, stream leakage_ok passing, eval forward
  deterministic and occlusion-free, state_dict round-trip, forward+backward wall time within noise
  of the unmodified arch, batcher determinism / bounds / no global-RNG use / fit not mutated). No
  claim here about the score is measured.
