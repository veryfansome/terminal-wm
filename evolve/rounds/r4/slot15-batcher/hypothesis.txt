WHAT I MEASURED IN THE DATA FIRST (evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl, 24 sequences, 724 steps)

262 of 724 steps (36.2%) produce an EMPTY observation: every `mv SRC DST` and every
`cat SRC > DST` / `cat SRC >> DST` returns exit 0 with no output. render_obs turns such a step
into exactly one of two strings, "cwd=/ exit=0\n" or "cwd=/data exit=0\n" (cwd takes only those
two values in the whole sample). Identical text through the frozen encoder is an identical
embedding, so after standardization 36% of all training rows carry one of two IDENTICAL target
vectors. Both parents train on this with r12_antiretrieval_ring_negatives, whose listwise term is
a row-vs-row softmax over in-batch targets. For a row inside a clique of k identical targets the
best attainable p_true is 1/k, so its NLL floor is log k, and the focal factor (1 - p_true)^GAMMA
is ~1 for exactly those rows: the loss term that cannot be reduced is also the one that gets the
largest weight. With bs=64 sequences of ~30 steps the batch has ~1900 rows, ~700 of them in that
clique, so a majority of the listwise loss is an irreducible constant whose gradient asks the
model to separate vectors that are equal. That is the single largest thing I found wrong in the
inherited stack, and it is invisible from the impl sources alone.

I also parsed the move chains from the command strings alone. Contents are exposed by `cat P`,
transported by `mv SRC DST` and by `cat SRC > DST` / `cat SRC >> DST`, and read back by a later
`cat P`. Across the sample there are 59 reads of a transported content; their hop counts are
1:36, 2:11, 3:4, 4:5, 5:2, 6:1. Summing the SQUARE of the hop count per sequence gives a
per-sequence transport mass ranging 4..36 (mean 12.5), i.e. a ~9x spread between the sequences
that carry deep-chain supervision and the ones that carry almost none.

WHAT I CHANGED

1. objective -> r4_dupclass_ring_negatives (new).
   The listwise term is recomputed over EQUIVALENCE CLASSES of coincident targets instead of over
   rows. In the no_grad block I form `coincident = (tt < 1e-3) | eye` on the same precision-weighted
   target-target distance matrix `tt` the parent already builds, subtract `log_mult` (the column's
   class size) from every column's logit, zero `log_a` inside a class, and take each row's numerator
   as a log-sum-exp over its own class (`log_num`) rather than the diagonal alone. A clique of k
   identical targets therefore contributes the mass of ONE point to the denominator and is fully
   attainable as a positive, so its NLL goes to ~0 instead of log k, and the focal factor stops
   up-weighting it. Everything else -- precision weighting, the confusability ring `a`, the gated
   repulsion hinge, the anchor MSE -- is byte-for-byte the parent's. When every target in the batch
   is distinct, `coincident` reduces to `eye`, `mult` is all ones, `log_mult` is 0, `log_a` is
   unchanged, and `log_num` is the diagonal logit: the loss is then term-by-term the parent's loss.
   The threshold is deliberately 1e-3 on tt (squared L2 over 768 standardized dims): float32
   cancellation error for a genuinely identical pair lands near 1e-7, while two DIFFERENT canary
   contents in the same file template are orders of magnitude further apart. The failure mode I am
   guarding against is collapsing genuine foils into one class, which would destroy the very
   discrimination the score measures; 1e-3 buys ~4 orders of magnitude of margin against it.
   Neither parent computes any class structure over targets; both treat two identical targets as a
   positive/negative pair.

2. batcher -> r4_distinct_blocked_transport_sampler (new, my assigned axis).
   Two things neither parent's batcher does.
   (a) NO DUPLICATE SEQUENCES IN A BATCH. r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum draws its hard fraction
   with `pool[torch.randint(...)]` -- with n_block_images=1 and hard_frac_max=0.75 that is 48 draws
   WITH replacement from a single image's pool, so a batch routinely contains the same sequence
   several times. Every repeat is ~30 more exactly-identical target rows, i.e. the batcher itself
   manufactures the clique problem described above, and it spends batch slots on negatives that
   carry no new information. Here the blocked part and the global part are both drawn WITHOUT
   replacement (Efraimidis-Spirakis weighted-reservoir keys `log(u)/w`, top-k), and the indices
   already taken by the blocked part are set to -inf before the global part is drawn, so all bs
   indices of a batch are distinct whenever len(fit) > bs.
   (b) TRANSPORT-MASS IMPORTANCE. `_transport_mass` replays the location->hop-count map from the
   command strings only (mv and redirect move the count, a two-token `cat` of a path with a
   positive count scores that read) and sums the SQUARE of the capped hop count. The sampling
   weight is (mass / split-mean) clamped to [1/4, 4], raised to the power chain_alpha * ramp, so
   the sampler is exactly uniform-without-replacement at step 1 and reaches the full tilt at
   ramp_frac * total_steps -- the same ramp the parent uses for its blocking fraction, which is
   kept unchanged (n_block_images=1, hard_frac_max=0.75, ramp_frac=0.3).
   Note on what this is NOT: nothing about the resolved chain enters the model's input. The parse
   only chooses WHICH SEQUENCES go in a batch; the tokens the model sees are the untouched
   cmd/obs embeddings of those sequences, and the parse is never run at scoring time.

3. arch -> r4_fingerprint_conditioned_composition (new; this is the crossover).
   The parent contributes the zero-init RMS-capped nonlinear COMPOSITION RENDERER over the
   retrieved-content channels (r22_retrieval_composition_renderer); the partner contributes the
   CAUSAL SECOND-ORDER FINGERPRINT -- the shrunk diagonal variance of strictly earlier raw
   observations, order-free and mean-free -- and its bias-free low-rank bounded FiLM
   (r22_causal_variance_fingerprint_film). They are coupled, not stacked: `code` is computed once
   in the subclass forward, then (i) injected into the renderer's HIDDEN layer by replacing
   `self.comp_in` with `_FingerprintConditionedProjection`, which adds a zero-initialized
   `code_proj(code)` to the renderer's pre-activation, and (ii) used, unchanged from the partner,
   to drive the capped multiplicative/additive FiLM on the command predictions. So the composition
   of retrieved prefix contents is conditioned on which system it is happening in, which is a term
   neither parent has: the parent's renderer sees only trunk state, retrieval context and the
   salience-pooled system summary, and the partner's fingerprint never touches the renderer because
   the partner has no renderer. Both new decoders (`code_proj`, `variance_out`) are zero-initialized,
   so at init the fused forward is exactly the parent's function, and with empty history the
   fingerprint is structurally the shrinkage prior.
   Live at the scored position: the fingerprint is built from observations STRICTLY EARLIER than
   each command (the cumsums are shifted by one and padded), so at the scored command -- whose own
   observation is withheld -- it is fully populated from the prefix rather than switched off, and
   PAD positions are excluded via `valid`.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca RATHER THAN THE ABSOLUTE HIT RATE

comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed on the same board with the same read token, so anything
that only rescales or recalibrates predictions uniformly cancels. What does not cancel is capacity
spent on discriminating WHICH exposed content the chain routed to the read location. (1) and (2)
both buy that capacity back rather than adding a new shortcut: (1) removes a majority of the
listwise loss that is provably irreducible and, worse, focal-up-weighted, leaving the contrastive
budget on the rows whose targets actually differ -- which are the content reads; (2) stops the
batcher from re-manufacturing that same clique and tilts sampling toward the sequences carrying
deep-chain reads, which is where the eligible slice (d3 and d4+) lives. (3) makes the renderer's
composition system-conditional, which matters because scoring is on IMAGES never trained on: a
composition rule fitted to the training systems' observation manifold has to be re-pointed at an
unseen one, and the fingerprint is the only per-system descriptor available at the scored position.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

- If the coincident-target clique is NOT actually big in the real training root (e.g. the root's
  cwd varies much more than in the sample, so the empty-output observations are not identical),
  change (1) reduces to the parent's loss and buys nothing. It cannot hurt in that case, but it
  cannot help either.
- If the irreducible clique loss was acting as an implicit regularizer -- a constant gradient that
  kept the head from collapsing onto content directions -- removing it could overfit the training
  images and lower transfer. I judge this unlikely because the anchor MSE still supervises the
  empty-observation target, but it is the honest risk of (1).
- If 1e-3 on tt is too loose for this encoder -- i.e. if two different canary contents in the same
  file template really do land within 1e-3 of each other after standardization -- then genuine
  foils get collapsed into one class and comp_ca should fall hard. This is the sharpest single
  assumption in the proposal and it is a property of the frozen embeddings, which I cannot measure
  from inside the jail.
- If len(fit) is not comfortably larger than bs, the without-replacement draw degenerates (the code
  falls back to the with-replacement uniform stream when len(fit) <= bs) and (2a) buys nothing.
- If the deep-chain sequences are a small, low-variance minority of the split, the transport-mass
  tilt is a ~9x reweighting of something that barely varies between sequences, and (2b) is noise.
  On the sample the spread is 4..36, which is enough to matter, but the sample is 24 sequences.
- If the composition renderer is already saturated or its gate stays closed, the extra conditioning
  term in (3) never activates and the arch reduces to the partner's post-hoc FiLM on the parent's
  renderer -- a plain stack, which is the weaker of the two things I claim.
- Speed: (2) costs one torch.rand(len(fit)) and two topk per step on CPU, and (3) adds three
  cumsums over the observation tokens plus two small matmuls per forward. Both are unmeasured here
  (no torch in the jail); I expect them to be negligible against the transformer forward, but that
  expectation is not measured.
