=====================================================================
1. WHAT THE CROSSOVER IS
=====================================================================

The two programs I was given differ on exactly two axes:

  parent   arch r22_observation_occlusion_denoising   head r3_rename_registry_occupancy_routing
  partner  arch r18_pathstate_latent_transition       head r2_dualaddress_move_transport

and they share optim r18_spectral_capped_transition_readout.

arch: r22 IS r18 (it subclasses R18PathStateLatentTransition and only adds ramped observation
occlusion in training mode; its eval forward calls super().forward unchanged). Selecting r22
therefore keeps the partner's whole latent-transition world model AND the parent's occlusion
regime. Nothing is dropped on that axis.

head: this is the only axis where one program's mechanism would otherwise have to be discarded.
So the head I propose runs BOTH memories on one trunk rather than choosing:

  - the registry memory (from the parent) contributes the mechanism where a move RE-ADDRESSES a
    slot: the slot's content vector is left byte-identical and only its address key is rotated
    toward the destination key, with source/destination roles decided at run time by which key
    currently indexes more occupancy;
  - the transport memory (from the partner) contributes the mechanism where source and
    destination keys are produced by two extractors sharing ONE path-to-key projection, so both
    keys land in a single address space by construction, and a move writes the value READ at the
    source key into the destination key of a delta-rule outer-product store.

Both inject their read at command positions through a per-dimension scale initialised to zero
(_RenameRegistry.out_scale, _DualAddressTransport.out_scale), so the composed forward is exactly
the bare arch forward at initialisation and the composition cannot break the arch. The wraps
chain cleanly: the registry wrap replaces net.forward first, the transport wrap captures that as
its own orig_forward, and the two use disjoint attribute names (rename_registry /
_rename_registry_state vs dualaddr_transport / _dualaddr_transport_state), disjoint stashes, and
disjoint parameter sets. Both auxiliaries run and are summed; the transport's aux_weight is set to
0.5 (_TRN_OVERRIDES) so it acts as a second supervision signal rather than an equal competitor to
the registry's.

The two auxiliaries mine DIFFERENT positions from the same batch, which is the point of keeping
both: the registry mines "bridges" in the command-similarity graph (a command strongly similar to
one earlier and one later command that are mutually dissimilar), the transport mines steps whose
observation duplicates an earlier step's observation under a different command with at least one
content-free step in between. On this data those are different families of move chain. In
train_sample.jsonl an mv step's observation is EMPTY (output "", rendered as "cwd=/ exit=0\n"), so
a move carries no content in its observation and the two miners can only key on commands and on
observation duplication - neither of them resolves a move chain, and neither puts anything into
the model's input.

=====================================================================
2. WHAT THE NEW OPTIM COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES
=====================================================================

Both programs' optim returns the FINAL iterate of a cosine-to-floor AdamW/Muon trajectory. My
optim keeps that base intact (same AdamW betas/wd, same Muon on the (key_d x d) addressing
projections found by the shape+sibling rule, same power-iteration soft spectral cap on the (D,D)
latent-transition readout) and adds two mechanisms that neither program has. Both act on where in
the loss landscape training ends up, not on what the model is fed.

(a) CARRIED LAYERWISE SHARPNESS-AWARE PERTURBATION (_FlatBasinOpt._displace / ._undisplace)

Sharpness-aware minimisation (Foret et al. 2021) replaces grad L(w) by grad L(w + e) with
e = rho * g/||g||; ASAM (Kwon et al. 2021) makes that displacement scale-invariant by measuring it
in units of the weights. Plain SAM needs two backward passes per step; the harness gives me one
(it calls opt.step() with no closure, after a single loss.backward()). I get the SAM gradient with
one backward by carrying the displacement ACROSS steps:

  end of step t:    w <- w + e_t,   e_t^(l) = rho * (||w^(l)|| / (||g_t^(l)|| + eps)) * g_t^(l)
  step t+1:         the harness's forward/backward is evaluated at w + e_t, giving g_{t+1}
  start of step t+1: w <- w - e_t   (exact restore of the stored tensor)
                    AdamW/Muon apply g_{t+1} to the undisplaced w; cap projects; average samples w

So every gradient the optimiser consumes is measured at a displaced, higher-loss point in the
direction of the PREVIOUS step's gradient, while every weight that is updated, weight-decayed,
spectrally capped and averaged is the undisplaced one. Using the previous step's direction is the
gradient-reuse approximation of LookSAM (Liu et al. 2022); it is exact SAM whenever the gradient
direction is stable between consecutive steps, which is the regime AdamW with momentum is in.

The displacement is layerwise and relative: ||e^(l)|| = rho * ||w^(l)|| exactly, i.e. each 2-D
parameter is displaced by a fixed 5% of its own Frobenius norm. That is the ASAM idea (the
displacement is measured in the weights' own scale) in the cheapest form that needs no host
synchronisation: no .item(), no isfinite().all() per parameter, one nan_to_num per parameter to
absorb a zero-norm or non-finite gradient. Only ndim==2 parameters are displaced, so LayerNorm
gains, biases, the zero-init residual out_scale vectors and the scalar memory-decay logit
(logit_decay, pos_scale) are never touched - those are the parameters where a relative
displacement would be meaningless or destabilising.

(b) CONSTANT-LR TAIL AVERAGING WITH TERMINAL WRITE-BACK (_accumulate / _finalize)

The schedule is warmup -> hold at 1.0 -> cosine decay that REACHES floor_ratio at the start of the
tail -> constant floor_ratio for the last avg_frac of steps (parent's schedule instead decays
cosine-to-floor over everything after the hold, so its LR is still moving at the last step). Over
that constant-LR tail the undisplaced parameters of every step are accumulated into a running mean
(Ruppert-Polyak / SWA, Izmailov et al. 2018, in the running-mean form a.lerp_(w, 1/n)), and on the
final optimiser step the mean is written into the parameters and the spectral cap is re-applied.
The model the instrument sees is therefore the tail mean, not the last iterate.

Two facts make this safe here rather than merely plausible. The trunk contains no BatchNorm - only
LayerNorm - so SWA's usual "re-estimate the batch statistics after averaging" step is unnecessary
and its absence is not a bug. And the spectral norm is convex, so sigma(mean of capped weights) <=
mean of sigma <= cap: averaging cannot violate the cap the parent's mechanism maintains, and the
re-projection in _finalize is belt-and-braces.

The step count is known exactly: the harness runs `for step in range(1, steps+1): ... opt.step()`,
so opt.step() is called exactly `steps` times and self.t == steps identifies the last one. The
write-back is guarded by n_avg >= 2 and by isfinite on the accumulator.

=====================================================================
3. WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca
=====================================================================

comp_ca is measured on windows held out BY IMAGE: the net must carry a content's identity through
a silent move chain on a filesystem it never trained on. That is a domain-generalisation
measurement, not an in-distribution one. The flat-minima literature outside this problem's field
says specifically that this gap - not the in-distribution loss - is what flatness buys: SWAD (Cha
et al., NeurIPS 2021) shows that densely averaging iterates inside a flat region reduces the
domain-generalisation gap where the same trajectory's final iterate does not, and SAM's own
transfer results point the same way. The parent optimises hard and hands back one point of a noisy
trajectory; both of my mechanisms change which point is handed back.

The reason to expect this to bite HERE and not be a generic 0.1% tweak: the thing comp_ca scores
is a routing decision made by a recurrent associative memory whose addressing keys are unit-normed
and whose reads are gated. Whether a read lands on the right slot on an unseen image is decided by
narrow margins in key space, so it is exactly the kind of decision that flips under the parameter
jitter of a final iterate and is stabilised by averaging. The training signal is also unusually
noisy for this genome - dropout 0.1, ramped stochastic observation occlusion from the r22 arch,
a hard-negative curriculum batcher, and now two mined auxiliaries - which widens the terminal noise
ball that tail averaging collapses and increases the headroom for the displacement to push the
trajectory into a basin whose bottom is broad rather than a spike that only the training images
occupy.

Neither mechanism touches the metric, the split, the eval, the input, or anything my code knows
about the move chain. The move chain is resolved nowhere in this proposal.

=====================================================================
4. WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL
=====================================================================

- If the gradient direction is NOT stable between consecutive steps at this batch size, the
  carried displacement is an arbitrary direction rather than an ascent direction, and (a)
  degenerates into isotropic weight noise. That is a regulariser, but a weaker and blunter one; at
  rho=0.05 it would most likely be a wash rather than a loss.
- If 5% of each layer's weight norm is too large for this recurrent memory, the delta-rule
  recurrence could be pushed into its clamp/nan_to_num guards on the displaced forward, producing
  gradients that are finite but uninformative. The guard against catastrophe is that the update is
  always applied to the undisplaced weights, so a bad displacement costs one step's gradient
  quality and cannot corrupt the parameters; but a persistent version of this would flatten
  learning. rho is a genome param and is the first thing to lower.
- If the trajectory has not converged by the start of the tail - a fixed step budget with a
  slow-converging memory mechanism is a real possibility - then averaging the last 25% averages
  genuinely different functions and the mean is worse than the endpoint. The constant floor_ratio
  of 0.12 is the lever: it is high enough that the tail explores the basin, low enough that it
  should not be exploring different basins. If eval health shows the averaged net worse than the
  parent's on the absolute pick rate as well as on comp_ca, this is the cause.
- If the two head memories interfere - both are residuals into the same command-position
  prediction, both are trained toward the same next observation by InfoNCE - the composite could
  be worse than either alone. The zero-init injection scales and the 0.5 transport aux weight are
  what makes this a small bet rather than a large one; the failure signature is a health/absolute
  reading no better than the parent with comp_ca unchanged.
- Speed: the composite head adds one more O(n) Python-level recurrent loop per forward and one
  more mining pass per step, and the optim adds ~5 elementwise kernels per 2-D parameter per step
  plus one per parameter over the tail. All of this is unmeasured from here - I cannot run the real
  harness - but none of it changes the step budget, only wall-clock per step.
- If training aborts early on a non-finite loss, _finalize never runs and the returned net is left
  displaced. That run is already discarded by the harness (ok=False), so it cannot be scored.
