CANDIDATE: objective = r4_trajectory_transport_contrast   (only the objective axis changed)

THE CROSSOVER, IN ONE SENTENCE
The parent's contribution is its negative geometry: a band-pass confusability ring over
target-target distances that makes close-but-distinct observations into hard negatives while
zeroing the weight of near-identical ones. The partner's contribution is plain squared error to
the target at full weight -- the exact quantity the eval's decision rule uses, unweighted, over
every row. This design keeps both (ring-importance negatives; MSE at weight 1.0 instead of the
parent's 0.05 anchor, and every distance in the loss computed in the eval's own unweighted
per-dim squared L2 rather than the parent's precision-weighted metric), and adds one new
mechanism that neither program has: the loss recovers TRAJECTORY structure from its own inputs
and spends most of its discriminative pressure on the rows where a content has been carried
across a chain of moves.

WHAT THE NEW MECHANISM COMPUTES

Everything below happens inside loss(), under no_grad, from tensors the contract already hands
over. Nothing is parsed, nothing is injected into the model's input, and none of it runs at
scoring time.

1. Sequence segmentation (_segment_ids). The harness builds ctx["prev"] as a strict-causal shift
   of the target within each sequence, so the first command row of every sequence in the batch
   has prev exactly equal to the zero vector and no other row does. cumsum over that indicator
   gives a segment id per row, i.e. which trajectory each row came from. If fewer than two
   starts are found (no ctx, or a single sequence) the whole trajectory-local machinery switches
   off and the loss degrades to MSE + the ring listwise term.

2. Content equivalence classes. Pairwise per-dim squared distance between target rows; rows
   within thr_eq = clamp(1e-4 * mean_off, 1e-5, 1e-2) are one class. Two steps that returned
   byte-identical observation text get byte-identical frozen embeddings, so their distance is
   ~1e-6 (gram round-off) while the closest genuinely distinct pair of observations in this data
   -- two templated config files differing only in their canary -- sits two to three orders of
   magnitude above the threshold. Checked on the jail's train_sample.jsonl: exact-duplicate
   observation text is common, near-duplicate-but-distinct text is not close to it.

3. Generic rows. A class whose size reaches max(8, 0.01n) is marked generic. On the real data
   this is exactly the set of rows whose observation carries no content: `mv A B` and
   `cat A > B` return empty output, so their observation renders to "cwd=/ exit=0\n" for every
   such step on every system (227 of 724 sample rows), and the boilerplate `ls -1 /` listing is
   the next largest class (35, then 18; the largest genuine content class is 4). Generic rows
   are excluded from the identity structure, and class size biases the softmax partition so a
   400-strong identical-target class is not forced apart from itself -- the parent's InfoNCE,
   with labels = arange, pushes each mv row's prediction AWAY from ~400 vectors identical to its
   own target, which is a large mis-directed gradient on ~40% of every batch.

4. The transported mark (the point of the design). Row t is transported when its target class
   has an earlier occurrence IN THE SAME SEGMENT, both rows non-generic, and every such earlier
   occurrence carried a different command. "Different command" is measured as the minimum cosine
   distance between row t's command embedding and its predecessors' (ctx["cmd"]), smoothed into
   a gate 1 - exp(-cd_min / (0.25 * mean_cd)): a re-read of the same path gets exactly zero, a
   read of a path the content was moved to gets ~1. In the sample data 8.3% of rows are
   transported and 1.7% are same-command re-reads correctly scored zero.

5. What the mark buys. Transported rows get (a) a 1 + 6*gate multiplier on the listwise term,
   (b) a second cross-entropy whose candidate set is restricted to their own trajectory's other
   distinct non-generic contents, and (c) a margin hinge against the nearest of those contents
   in d = mean_i (pred_i - cand_i)^2, the eval's decision variable verbatim. Same-trajectory
   distinct contents also get an additive log-importance bonus in the global listwise term.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca is a paired difference over windows that expose N contents at N locations, chain them
through silent moves, and read one location; the swapped arm re-routes the same contents over
the same board. The candidates that must be told apart at the read are precisely the N contents
of that board, and the swapped arm's answer is one of the other N-1. So the negative set that
matters is "the other contents of this same trajectory", which is what item 5 constructs, and
the positive is "the content that was exposed earlier under a different path", which is what
item 4 selects. Both parents' losses are blind to this: their negatives are drawn from the whole
batch by target-target distance, so the fraction of gradient that lands on a move-chain read
with its own board as competition is roughly its share of rows (~8%), and the parent's
precision-weighted metric is not the metric the pick is made in. Here transported rows carry
55-77% of the gradient norm while the model is still poorly fit, falling to ~26% once it fits
well -- the pressure is self-limiting, and full-weight MSE on every row is untouched underneath
it, so general prediction is not traded away.

There is a second, architecture-specific reason to expect a large move rather than a small one.
The inherited arch (r23) already contains the machinery this loss asks for: a dual-address
transport memory that reads content at a source address and writes it at a destination address,
and a pointer head that copies one verbatim earlier prefix observation into the prediction. Its
contribution enters through a zero-initialised (D,D) readout behind a gate biased to -2, i.e.
it starts switched off and only grows if the loss pays for it. A loss whose gradient is
dominated by generic and single-exposure rows has almost no reason to switch it on, because
those rows are predictable without any transport at all. This loss pays for it exactly where the
copy is the right answer, and punishes copying the WRONG board content, which is the difference
between using the pointer and leaving it dark.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR IT TO FAIL

- If exact-duplicate observation text does not survive to exact-duplicate embeddings (a
  non-deterministic encoder, or a standardization dim with near-zero variance amplifying
  round-off past thr_eq), the transported mark goes empty and the candidate collapses to
  MSE + ring listwise. Failure mode is "no better than the parent", not divergence.
- If the eligible scored windows are dominated by chains whose contents were never exposed
  earlier in the trajectory under a different path, training has nothing analogous to weight and
  the extra terms are inert on the scored slice.
- The mark is a label-space fact, so it cannot be used at scoring time and it does not have to
  be: it only decides which training rows get pressure. But it also means a model can satisfy
  the transport CE by learning "copy the most recently exposed content" -- a position heuristic,
  which cancels only in expectation and buys nothing real. The restricted candidate set is the
  guard against that (the wrong board content is an explicit negative with a margin), and if
  that guard is too weak, the number moves into the analytic band rather than above it.
- Upweighting transported rows 6x could cost enough general fit that the model's embeddings get
  worse everywhere, dragging the eligible windows down with them. Full-weight MSE on every row
  is the counterweight; if it is not enough, the failure shows as a fall in the reported
  pick-rate gate alongside a flat comp_ca.
- Cost, unmeasured on the real harness: one extra n x n gram (command cosines) on top of the two
  the parent already forms. Measured at n = 1984, D = 768 on CPU: 39 ms per forward+backward of
  the loss alone, which is a small fraction of a step on the GPU lane. No loop over rows, no
  linear solve, no per-step allocation that scales worse than n^2.
