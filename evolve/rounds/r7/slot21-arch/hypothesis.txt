MECHANISM (arch only; every other axis is the parent's verbatim)

I replace the parent's backward multi-hop pointer chase with a forward REGISTER FILE whose slot
KEYS are mutable and whose slot CONTENTS are immutable. This is the transpose of every routing
mechanism in the parent lineage. The parent keeps the board static and, at read time, chases
K hops backwards through a soft follow-matrix over commands; content is what moves through the
attention. Here the scan runs forward once: a bank of n_slots registers each holds a unit
location key, a coefficient row over observations, and an occupancy scalar. A `cat X` binds the
observation into a register keyed at address(X). A `mv A B` finds the register whose key matches
address(A) and REWRITES that key to address(B) — the content never moves, its address does.
A later `cat B` matches the rewritten key and pulls the register's coefficient row.

Concretely, per step i (read strictly before write, so state at the read comes only from steps
< i): sim = keys · a_read_i; p = softmax(sim * s_read); w = p * occ; the read is w/sum(w) applied
to the coefficient matrix, so the retrieved vector is exactly a convex mixture of strictly
earlier RAW observation embeddings — never a free-floating latent. The write applies (a) a
relocation, softmax-matching keys against a_src_i, scaled by a confidence sigmoid on the
occupancy-masked peak similarity and by a learned move gate, sliding the matched key toward
a_dst_i and renormalizing to the sphere; and (b) an install, which either updates the register
that already holds a_read_i (occupancy-masked peak similarity says the address is held) or
allocates a fresh one by a straight-through hard argmax over (1 - occupancy) plus a learned
per-slot tiebreak — hard, because a soft allocation over 32 slots smears one content across the
whole bank at the first install and destroys every later address match. Occupancy leaks by a
learned factor, so allocation recycles the stalest register. Read, source and destination
addresses come from three separate nonlinear branches over one shared command-feature trunk, all
passed through ONE shared address projection, so a read address and an earlier command's
destination address are comparable by construction.

WHY IT SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed on the same board. A predictor that keys on the location
being read is identical in both arms and contributes exactly zero. This mechanism cannot key on
the location alone: the answer it returns for a fixed read address depends on which content the
chain of key rewrites parked there, which is exactly what the role swap changes. It also carries
no positional or recency prior at all — the parent's chase is explicitly recency-biased, which is
the family of features that cancels only in expectation and can score on a frozen slice for the
wrong reason; nothing here reads step index.

The two structural advantages over the backward chase are (1) unbounded depth at constant cost —
composition happens by mutating one key in place, so a d=4 chain costs the same as d=1 and there
is no hop budget to exhaust and no product of d softmaxes to attenuate the signal; the parent
needs chase_hops >= depth and multiplies a follow matrix by itself, so probability mass diffuses
with depth exactly where the objective's d4+ windows live. And (2) the retrieval is a coefficient
row over observations, computed as one [B,n,n] x [B,n,D] matmul at the end, so the whole scan is
cheaper in memory and compute than the parent's repeated bmm over [B,n,n] follow matrices.

I verified the recurrence with oracle addresses (a directed test, not a training run): on a
depth-3 and a depth-4 move chain the final read puts 0.98-0.99 of its coefficient mass on the
step whose observation introduced the content; and on a role-swapped pair — same board, same
destinations, two contents exchanging their move sets — the native arm returns content A and the
swapped arm returns content B, 0.977 vs 0.977 on opposite steps. That is the sign structure
comp_ca pays for. It is a statement about the arithmetic only: whether the learned address
branches actually separate the source path from the destination path inside a mean-pooled command
embedding is the open empirical question, and no fitness claim here is measured.

WHY ONLY arch CHANGES

The head (r2 dual-address move transport) is the value-moved / key-fixed memory; mine is the
key-moved / value-fixed one, so they are complementary rather than redundant, and the head's
relocation-mined InfoNCE is applied to the final prediction, which now contains my retrieval —
it supervises this mechanism for free. The optim's spectral cap applies to the new (D,D) readout
exactly as it did to the parent's. Changing the objective, target, stream or batcher would
confound the mechanism swap with a training-signal change, and nothing in the design needs them
to move.

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE

Higher native_hit and lower swap_stayed together, with the gain concentrated in the d4+ windows
where the parent's fixed hop budget and diffusing follow matrix hurt most. The retrieval mass
feature should be near zero on `mv`-type steps and near one on reads of a moved location; if the
gate learns to fire everywhere, the mechanism has degenerated to a prefix copy.

BIGGEST RISK

The install gate must learn to bind on content-bearing reads and not on generic `ls` output, and
the three role branches must separate the two paths inside one mean-pooled `mv src dst`
embedding. If either fails, registers get keyed on junk addresses, the read matches nothing, the
output gate closes, and the candidate lands at the r22 base rather than above the parent. The
straight-through hard allocation is a second risk: it is discrete, so a training run can thrash
between allocation patterns early, and its gradient reaches the allocation sharpness only through
the soft surrogate.
