AXES CHANGED: optim only (rewrite). Every other axis keeps the parent's impl name and params
verbatim. The mechanism is entirely about how the existing parameters are moved, so nothing about
the arch, head, objective, batcher, stream or target needs to change with it; touching them would
only confound the read.

MECHANISM

Two changes, one thesis: give each block the geometry its own parameterization actually has, and
stop using a learning-rate schedule to buy the stability that averaging can buy instead.

(1) Schedule-free AdamW (Defazio et al., "The Road Less Scheduled", 2024) replaces the parent's
warmup-hold-cosine-floor AdamW + Muon. Three sequences, one stored:

    y_k = (1 - b1) z_k + b1 x_k          gradients are taken at y (the live parameters)
    u_k = g(y_k) / (sqrt(v_k) + eps)     v is the usual Adam second moment
    z_{k+1} = z_k - lr_k u_k             the fast iterate takes the whole step
    c_{k+1} = w_k / sum_{j<=k} w_j,  w_k = (max_j lr_j)^2
    x_{k+1} = (1 - c_{k+1}) x_k + c_{k+1} z_{k+1}      the lr^2-weighted running mean

Only y and z are materialized; the impl applies the standard in-place recursion
y <- y + c(z - y) + lr(b1(1 - c) - 1)u, which I verified reproduces the explicit x recursion above
to machine precision over 200 simulated steps, and converts y to x = y + (1 - 1/b1)(z - y) on the
final optimizer step so the net that leaves _train IS the averaged iterate. After a short linear
warmup the learning rate is constant, so with w_k constant the average is uniform Polyak-Ruppert
over the whole trajectory.

(2) A Riemannian sphere group for the addressing projections, an adaptation of AdamP (Heo et al.,
ICLR 2021) sharpened by certification instead of a per-step heuristic. Five of this arch's weight
matrices feed a projection whose output is immediately unit-normalized -- file_read and file_write
through _quotient (linear, then _unit), path_read, path_write and tr_path through _unit -- and the
verb codebook is normalized row-by-row after Gram-Schmidt. For all six the loss is exactly
invariant to W -> cW, so by Euler's theorem <grad L, W> = 0 identically. The impl measures the mean
|cos(g, W)| over the first detect_steps updates and certifies a weight scale-invariant when that
mean falls below sphere_delta/sqrt(numel): for the (64,176) projections that threshold is 9.4e-4,
against a chance-level cosine of 9.4e-3 and a float32 cancellation floor around 1e-5, so the test
has roughly an order of magnitude of headroom on both sides. A certified weight then (a) has the
radial component of its update projected out, (b) receives no weight decay, since decay on a
scale-invariant weight cannot change the function and only shrinks the norm, and (c) has y and z
rescaled by ONE common factor so ||y|| returns to the norm it was certified at. Because both
iterates are scaled together, x scales with them, and since the function depends only on the
direction, the rescale is an exact no-op on the represented model while pinning the norm.

The soft one-sided spectral cap on square (768,768) matrices is kept -- this arch's tr_read is the
only one -- because that readout is injected additively into the command-position prediction and is
scale-SENSITIVE, the exact opposite of the addressing group; it is the one place magnitude, not
direction, has to be controlled. It is now evaluated on the extrapolated point x rather than on y,
and applied as the same multiplier to y and z, so the cap constrains the iterate that is actually
measured and cannot make y and z drift apart.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

The objective needs the model to carry a particular content's identity through a chain of moves on
a system it never trained on. Two things stand between the parent and that.

First, effective learning rate on the routing keys. The delta-rule memory addresses slots by the
direction of the file_read/file_write and path_read/path_write codes; the magnitude of those
matrices is a null direction of the loss. Under the parent's Muon branch each step adds a
fixed-RMS increment, so ||W||_F grows roughly like sqrt(t) while the angular motion per step,
||dW||/||W||, decays like 1/sqrt(t). The addressing geometry therefore freezes partway through a
fixed step budget, exactly when the chain-depth structure the objective cares about is still being
sorted out. Under a fixed-norm sphere step the angular learning rate is constant for the whole
budget (at lr 5e-4 and this arch's initialization it works out near 1.2e-3 radians per step), so
the keys can still reorganize late. Deeper windows -- the d4+ half of the eligible slice -- need
more separation between path codes than shallow ones, so this is where I expect any movement.

Second, variance. The parent's reported per-seed comp_ca values, +0.0112 and -0.0112 on n=89
windows, are exactly plus and minus one window: the whole spread between candidates measured so far
is a couple of windows out of 89. A large share of that is which basin a seed's final iterate
happened to land in. Averaging the trajectory rather than annealing into one point attacks that
component directly, and it is the reason to prefer schedule-free over the parent's cosine-to-floor
schedule instead of merely bolting an EMA onto it: the average is not a post-hoc smoothing of the
same trajectory, the gradients are taken at y, which sits 90% of the way toward the average, so the
trajectory itself is different.

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE

comp_ca above the parent's -0.0075, with the gain concentrated in the d4+ windows, and less seed-to
-seed spread than the parent's plus/minus one window. Both claims are UNMEASURED -- I could not run
the harness from the jail and deliberately did not extrapolate from a miniature run. What I did
verify without torch: the y recursion reproduces the explicit x average to 1e-16 over 200 steps,
the final lerp recovers x exactly, and rescaling y and z by a common factor leaves
x = y + (1 - 1/b1)(z - y) scaled by that same factor, so both the sphere rescale and the spectral
shrink preserve the schedule-free invariant y = (1 - b1)z + b1 x rather than silently breaking it.

FALSIFICATION

If comp_ca lands at or below the parent's, the reading is that on this budget the averaged iterate
is undertrained relative to an annealed one, or that a constant angular learning rate on the
addressing codes keeps them moving so late that the delta-rule memory never settles into a stable
routing. A follow-up that separates the two would keep the parent's cosine schedule and add only
the sphere group, or keep schedule-free and drop the sphere group.

BIGGEST RISK

Schedule-free evaluates gradients at y = 0.1 z + 0.9 x, which lags the fast iterate badly early in
training. On a fixed step budget on frozen encoder embeddings, the averaged iterate can simply be
undertrained relative to the parent's peak-then-anneal trajectory, and if it is, the whole candidate
loses on optimization speed rather than on geometry, and the sphere group never gets a fair test.
The warmup is 5% of the budget and the learning rate matches the parent's peak of 5e-4 rather than
its schedule-averaged ~3.3e-4, which is the mitigation, but it is an untested one here.

CORRECTNESS NOTES

- No new nn.Parameters are introduced, so there is nothing that could sit behind a no_grad block or
  on an unreached branch: this axis only reshapes the updates of parameters the arch already
  trains, and every parameter handed to make() is in the single param group and is stepped.
- The impl never indexes the token sequence, so the odd-length [cmd_0, obs_0, ..., cmd_r] layout is
  not something it can get wrong.
- The class subclasses torch.optim.Optimizer, not nn.Module, and adds only total_steps,
  sphere_delta, detect_steps, readout_dim, spectral_cap, spectral_iters and averaged, none of which
  shadow an Optimizer attribute. LambdaLR's step-counter wrapper binds over step() normally.
- A weight that is exactly zero, or that has no gradient on a step, would make cos(g, W) read 0/0.
  That is the failure that would falsely certify this arch's THREE zero-initialized matrices
  (film_out, sysfilm_out, tr_read) as scale-invariant and then pin their norms at zero, deleting
  them. The certification forces the per-step cosine to 1.0 whenever ||W|| <= 1e-6 or ||g|| <=
  1e-12, so a single such step is enough to push the window mean far above the threshold and the
  weight stays in the ordinary AdamW group.
- Certification is frozen after detect_steps and every subsequent per-step quantity (the tangent
  projection, the norm restore, the spectral shrink) is computed as a tensor and applied
  unconditionally, so the hot loop adds no host-device synchronization; the only syncs are one
  float() per parameter at the certification step and one isfinite check per parameter on the final
  step. The extra per-step cost is a handful of vector norms plus two power-iteration matvecs on a
  single 768x768 matrix, which is negligible against the forward and backward passes -- also
  unmeasured, but it is strictly less work than the parent's five-step Newton-Schulz
  orthogonalization on six matrices, which this design removes.
- If steps < detect_steps the detection window is clamped to steps, so certification always
  resolves; if it resolves negative for everything, the impl degrades gracefully to plain
  schedule-free AdamW rather than doing anything undefined.
