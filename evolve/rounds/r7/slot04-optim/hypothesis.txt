REVISION AFTER REVIEW — WHAT WAS WRONG AND WHAT I CHANGED

The reviewer was right, and the arithmetic reproduces exactly. My first version rescaled the
Bjorck-Schulz output by ||W||_F / sqrt(min(rows, cols)), which is the correct factor ONLY at
convergence. At the configured ortho_iters=6 the iteration is not converged, so that factor was
wrong by a fixed multiplicative amount and the retraction was a geometric contraction applied every
step, not the scale-preserving operation I claimed.

There was no nonzero fixed point at all: the map was a strict contraction of the norm on the very

THREE CHANGES, all in the retraction:

1. The target is rescaled by its OWN MEASURED Frobenius norm, not by the asymptotic sqrt(m):

       T <- A_n * ( ||W||_F / ||A_n||_F )

   so ||T||_F = ||W||_F identically, for any iteration count and any conditioning. On a perfectly
   conditioned W this now gives T = W exactly at every n >= 1, so the manifold is a genuine fixed
   set instead of a repeller: per-step factor 1.000000 rather than 0.962063 / 0.927111 / 0.973444.

2. The blend is renormalized back to the pre-retraction norm. Even with ||T||_F = ||W||_F, a convex
   blend of two matrices that are not parallel is shorter than either; the residual is small and
   self-limiting (it goes to zero as W approaches the manifold) but it is not zero, so I removed it
   rather than argue it away. Measured on the exact singular-value dynamics, the pre-renorm blend
   norm is 0.9835 at cond 4.7 (m=64, rate 0.50) and 0.9751 at cond 10, i.e. corrections of 1.7% and
   2.5% applied once, shrinking to nothing as conditioning improves. After this step the Frobenius
   norm is an EXACT invariant of the retraction, unconditionally.

3. ortho_iters 6 -> 12. With (1) and (2) the norm is safe at any iteration count, so this is now
   purely about the target being the nearest row-isometry rather than a partly conditioned matrix.
   n=12 converges the worst conditioning actually observed in this model. For a (128,768) map with
   a linearly spread cond-4.7 spectrum the smallest singular value of A = W/||W||_F is 0.029013,
   and f^12(0.029013) = 0.99999287 against f^10 = 0.96162; for the (64,*) maps at the same
   condition it is 0.040990 and f^12 = 1.00000000. Degrading further is graceful, not cliffed:
   cond 7 gives f^12 = 0.99863, cond 10 gives f^12 = 0.97980 — the target is then slightly
   under-conditioned in its smallest direction, which weakens the retraction, and cannot touch the
   norm, which is invariant by (1) and (2) regardless of n.

ON THE SECOND FINDING — THE COMPETING TERMS IN THE CLOSURE SCORE

The closure score is q.k/sqrt(ref_d) + ref_diff(mismatch) + ref_keybias(u). The reviewer is right
that the first term was being suppressed relative to the other two, but the cause was defect (1),
not the shape filter, and it goes away with it: ref_q and ref_k now keep their Frobenius norm
exactly, so there is no systematic attenuation of the q.k logit against the untouched biases.

The filter itself is not an exclusion. ref_keybias is (1,768) and ref_diff is (1,64); a matrix with
min(rows, cols) == 1 has exactly one singular value, so it is already a scaled row-isometry and lies
in the fixed set. Under the corrected rescale the retraction returns it unchanged in exact
arithmetic — checked in the dynamics simulation, delta 0.000e+00, and true for the old rescale too
since sqrt(min(1, n)) = 1 — so retracting it would be a no-op that costs kernels and float32 noise
and buys nothing. I changed the predicate from `rows >= 2` to `min(rows, cols) >= 2` so the code states
the actual mathematical condition rather than a shape coincidence. (ref_diff is (1,64), so it is not
in the router group by the cols == D rule and was never a candidate either way.)

What does remain, stated honestly rather than claimed away: preserving ||W||_F preserves the
expected logit magnitude for isotropic inputs, but real command embeddings are not isotropic, so
flattening the spectrum of ref_q / ref_k redistributes response away from their top singular
directions. That can move the data-conditional logit RMS by at most a factor of the condition number
(around 4.7 at init on this arch), once, and it stops as soon as the maps are conditioned — it does
not compound across steps, which is the whole difference from the defect. If the closure routing
collapses onto the bias terms anyway, that shows up as swap_stayed rising with native_hit, which is
in my falsification list below.

WHAT THE PARENT'S GENOME ACTUALLY DOES AT THE ADDRESS SITE

The stream this genome carries writes each command token as fixed coordinate regions computed from
the command string alone: columns [0:224] hold a dense Rademacher (+-1) hash of the command's source
path, columns [224:448] the same hash family applied to its destination path, and the standardized
embedding is zeroed under both. The arch reads exactly those two regions through ONE shared linear
map, addr: R^224 -> R^64 (addr_w=224, src_lo=0, dst_lo=224), unit-normalizes each result, and uses
them as the read address and the write address of the delta-rule transporter memory. Two distinct
paths therefore arrive as two near-orthogonal +-1 codes u, v with |u.v|/224 concentrated around
1/sqrt(224); whether they stay distinguishable after addressing is decided entirely by the singular
spectrum of addr. This is the Johnson-Lindenstrauss situation in its exact textbook form: a map with
orthonormal rows is a partial isometry, so ||Wu - Wv||^2 = ||P(u-v)||^2 concentrates at
(64/224)||u-v||^2 for ANY pair of codes, including pairs the map never saw; a map that has collapsed
to effective rank r << 64 preserves a much smaller and far more variable fraction, and the directions
it kept were chosen by the training paths.

THE GAP THIS TARGETS

addr is the single most objective-relevant tensor in the model and the parent optimizer gives it the
least care of any addressing weight. It is a lone (64, 224) tensor, so the parent's sibling rule
(shape must appear at least twice) deliberately excludes it from the Muon group; it is not (r, 768),
so it is not in the router group; it is not (768, 768), so the spectral cap never touches it. It
falls into `rest` with weight_decay=5e-4 under the cosine-to-floor trunk schedule, and nothing in the
run opposes spectral collapse. Nothing forces it to stay well conditioned, and the training loss has
no reason to keep the directions used only by paths that appear on held-out images.

THE MECHANISM, AS THE CODE NOW IMPLEMENTS IT

After every optimizer step, retract the identity-separating projections toward the nearest
row-isometry, in weight space, at exactly constant Frobenius norm. For each selected W:

  A  <- W / ||W||_F                       (sigma_max(A) <= ||A||_F = 1, by construction)
  repeat ortho_iters times:
      A <- 1.5 A - 0.5 (A A^T) A          (Bjorck-Schulz; sigma -> 1.5 sigma - 0.5 sigma^3)
  T  <- A * ( ||W||_F / ||A||_F )         (measured norm, correct at every iteration count)
  B  <- (1 - rate) W + rate T
  W  <- B * ( ||W||_F / ||B||_F )         (blend renormalization; exact norm invariance)

The scalar map f(s) = 1.5 s - 0.5 s^3 has f(1) = 1, f'(1) = 0, and maps (0, 1] into (0, 1]
monotonically, so from the guaranteed bound sigma_max <= 1 the iteration can only walk singular
values UP toward 1 and can never overshoot or diverge. That bound is why I normalize by the
Frobenius norm rather than by a power-iterated spectral norm: it needs no estimate and it cannot be
wrong. f also compresses ratios — f(s1)/f(s2) = (s1/s2)(1.5 - 0.5 s1^2)/(1.5 - 0.5 s2^2) > s1/s2 for
s1 < s2 — so the condition number improves strictly at ANY iteration count, and the two rescales are
pure scalars that cannot change it. The fixed set is exactly the matrices with all singular values
equal, i.e. the scaled row-isometries. Weight decay and the learning-rate schedule have sole
authority over magnitude, and the closure attention temperature (q.k / sqrt(ref_d)) is carried
through with its scale intact.

Two families are selected, disjoint by shape:
  - address family, rate ortho_rate=0.5: every 2-D weight with rows == key_d and cols not in
    {key_d, D}. On this arch that is addr (64,224) and file_read, file_write, path_read, path_write,
    sys_val (64,176). Every one of these produces a vector that is immediately unit-normalized (or
    quotiented against the verb basis) and then compared by inner product — they are address maps,
    and isometry is the property they are supposed to have. Because their outputs are normalized
    before use, this family is insensitive to scale by construction; the norm invariance matters for
    them not as a downstream effect but because without it the tensors were being driven to zero.
  - closure family, rate router_ortho_rate=0.35: every 2-D weight with cols == D and
    2 <= rows <= router_out_max, i.e. ref_q, ref_k (128,768) and ref_p (64,768) — the maps whose
    inner products decide which earlier command a read chains back through. The router group keeps
    its two-timescale LR treatment unchanged; the retraction is additive to it, not a replacement.

The retraction is re-applied at full strength (rate 1.0) immediately after the Polyak tail copies its
averaged weights into the live parameters, and the spectral cap is re-applied after that as the
parent already did. A uniform average of successive near-isometries that have rotated relative to
one another is systematically ill-conditioned (the average of rotations is not a rotation), so the
parent's tail damages exactly the property this mechanism maintains, at the one moment whose weights
are the ones actually scored. Note precisely what the full-strength pass does and does not do: it
restores the CONDITIONING of the averaged weights and leaves their magnitude exactly as the average
produced it, which is the parent's behaviour and is untouched here.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca RATHER THAN THE THINGS THAT CANCEL

comp_ca is the paired difference between the native chain and a chain in which two contents exchange
their move commands over the same board, same destinations, same depth. The role-swap re-encodes the
move commands, so the +-1 source/destination hash codes that enter addr are exactly the tensors that
differ between the two arms; everything else in the window is identical. A model whose addresses have
collapsed cannot resolve which content went where in either arm and predicts nearly the same thing in
both, which is the zero-difference regime. Keying on the location being read cancels exactly and
keying on move-order cancels only in expectation; neither is helped by conditioning addr, because
neither uses the fine structure of the path codes. What conditioning addr helps is the one thing that
does not cancel: carrying a specific content's identity through several hops so that a read returns
what is actually there. And because the scored windows are held out BY IMAGE, the paths at scoring
time are strings the map never saw, which is precisely the regime where an isometry generalizes and a
rank-collapsed map does not.

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE, AND WHAT WOULD FALSIFY IT

Expected: native_hit rises on the deeper windows (d4+ more than d3, since each additional hop
compounds one more addressing collision) while swap_stayed does not rise with it, so comp_ca goes up
above the parent's +0.6929. I expect the effect to be largest where the parent's addressing is most
strained rather than a uniform lift. Falsified if comp_ca does not move, or if native_hit and
swap_stayed rise together — the latter would say the retraction bought general sharpness without
buying content tracking, which is the outcome the paired metric exists to expose. A specific
falsifier for the closure half: if the final weight norms of ref_q, ref_k, ref_p come back materially
below the parent's, the norm invariance is not holding in the real run and the whole closure result
should be discarded regardless of the score.

PERFORMANCE, STATED AS UNMEASURED: I have not run this and could not; there is no torch in this jail.
The retraction now costs twelve iterations over nine tensors, batched into four same-shape groups:
five (64,176), one (64,224), two (128,768), one (64,768). Counting 2*R*R*C per bmm and two bmms per
iteration that is about 1.58 GFLOP per step, dominated by the two (128,768) maps at 1.21 GFLOP of it,
which is arithmetic noise on a 4090. Batching by shape is why doubling the iteration count does not
double the overhead: the iteration now issues 4 groups * 12 iters * 2 kernels = 96 launches per step
against the reviewed version's 9 tensors * 6 iters * 2 = 108, so the launch count went slightly DOWN.
The extra allocation is one stack and one copy per group per step. I estimate low single-digit
percent wall-clock and have NOT measured it. The retraction is written sync-free on purpose — no
.item(), no Python-level branch on a device tensor, the finiteness guard is folded into a blend
coefficient and the degenerate-norm guard into a torch.where — so it does not stall the pipeline.

CORRECTNESS NOTES CHECKED AGAINST THE STATED FAILURE MODES

- No new trainable parameter is introduced at all, so there is nothing that could receive gradient
  only inside no_grad or only on an unscored branch. Every weight the retraction touches is an
  existing parameter that already receives gradient from the training loss on every step and already
  affects the prediction at the scored read position; the retraction runs after opt.step(), the same
  seam the parent's spectral cap and Polyak tail already occupy.
- The three new genome params are all live on this arch: ortho_rate acts on a non-empty 6-tensor set,
  router_ortho_rate on a non-empty 3-tensor set, ortho_iters on both. ortho_iters is still reachable
  after the fix — it no longer changes the norm, but it changes how far the target is conditioned,
  and therefore the weights, on every step.
- The optimizer never looks at the sequence, so odd sequence length is not reachable from this axis;
  the parity-sensitive code paths in the arch and stream are untouched.
- No method or attribute here shadows an nn.Module internal; _IsometryOpt is a plain object and its
  attribute is named `retraction`, not `state`, `training`, `modules` or `parameters`.
- No stream change, so code_cmds is untouched and the instrument reproduces the parent's tokens on
  both the native and the role-swapped arm bit for bit.
- Numerical guards: division by ||W||_F, by ||A_n||_F and by ||B||_F are all clamped; the target is
  finiteness-checked before it is blended in and only then passed through nan_to_num, so a non-finite
  target yields a blend coefficient of exactly zero and contributes a hard zero rather than a NaN.
  On that path B equals W bit for bit, the renormalization ratio is therefore exactly 1.0, and the
  weight is written back unchanged. A weight with ||W||_F <= 1e-8 takes the same untouched path.
