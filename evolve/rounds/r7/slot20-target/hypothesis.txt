AXES CHANGED: target only (identity -> r7_unit_volume_householder_metric). Every other axis keeps
the parent's impl and params verbatim. The mechanism is a pure change of the geometry in which the
existing loss measures error; it needs no cooperation from the arch, stream, objective, batcher,
head or optim, and touching any of them would confound the one thing being tested.

MECHANISM
A learned linear flow on the target space with |det| pinned to exactly 1:

    make_target(z) = (z R) * g          to_obs(p) = (p / g) R_inv

R is the product of an even number (64) of Householder reflections H(v) = I - 2 v v^T / |v|^2 built
from a trainable [64, 768] matrix; it is orthogonal for every parameter value and R_inv is the same
reflections replayed in reverse order, so the inverse is exact by construction rather than by a
solve. g = exp(a - mean(a)) with a = 1.25 * tanh(log_gain): the logs sum to exactly zero, so the
gains have unit product, and the log range is bounded, so the transform's condition number is
capped at e^2.5 ~ 12. The reflection vectors are initialised as 32 adjacent identical PAIRS and
log_gain at zero, so H(v)H(v) = I and the transform is EXACTLY the identity at step 0 -- the run
starts as the parent and departs only by learning. At that symmetric point the gradients on the two
members of a pair are exact negatives, so each pair separates into a rotation in one learned plane;
32 planes are available. Both parameter tensors get gradient from the training loss (the target is
built inside the autograd graph and the objective's raw_anchor and dist2 terms both differentiate
through it), and both appear again in to_obs, so they act directly on the prediction at the scored
read position.

WHY IT SHOULD MOVE comp_ca
Minimising sum_i g_i^2 r_i^2 subject to prod_i g_i = 1 has the closed-form optimum g_i ~ 1/r_i, at
which the loss equals the GEOMETRIC mean of the per-direction squared residuals instead of the
arithmetic mean. The training signal therefore becomes a relative, scale-free error criterion in a
learned basis: every direction contributes equally in d log L / d log r_i, rather than in absolute
error. That matters here because of what these observations look like. Contents are distinguished by
a few hex characters inside otherwise identical template text (".. job[ed89] canary=ba02a23..",
"# canary 1a689166857cd315", "cv-6e330e2ed64c19c4"), while the bulk of the target variance is
output length / format / cwd / empty-vs-nonempty -- and two thirds of the training steps have empty
output at all. Under an absolute-error criterion the content-identity subspace is a rounding error
in the loss, so the readout is never pressed to place a prediction accurately inside it. The
rotation lets the gain pick out that subspace even though it is not coordinate-aligned (per-coordinate
standardisation has already equalised the marginals, so a low-variance direction only exists in a
rotated basis), and the unit-determinant constraint makes the amplification a genuine reallocation
rather than a free rescaling -- a collapse is not merely scored down after the fact, it is
unreachable.

The eval inverse is what converts that into retrieval. A residual e in transformed space comes back
as (e / g) R_inv, so the model's error is DIVIDED by the same gains training amplified: error shrinks
exactly along the directions the transform judged worth resolving, and grows along the nuisance
directions. Growing the nuisance error is close to free for the metric, because the forced foils on
a cups board are contents of the same template: they differ only inside the content subspace, so the
nuisance part of the error is common-mode across all candidates and cancels out of the ranking,
while the content part is the whole of it.

PREDICTION (unmeasured -- I cannot run the harness from the jail)
native_hit rises because the read prediction lands nearer the content actually routed there;
swap_stayed is not raised by the same amount, because the transform is a fixed function of the
target space and identical in both arms, so anything that keys on the read location still cancels
exactly per window. I expect comp_ca above the parent's +0.0150, most of the movement at d3, and
the paired gap to widen rather than both arms moving together. Concretely falsifiable: if the
learned log_gain stays near zero, or ends up spread but comp_ca does not move, the geometry story
is wrong and an absolute-error target was not the binding constraint.

COST AND SAFETY
49,920 extra parameters, one [768,768] orthogonal matrix rebuilt per step (64 rank-1 updates, then
one matmul; activation memory is independent of batch size). Verified mechanically: identity at
init to 6e-7; round-trip after heavy parameter perturbation exact to 7e-7 relative; R R^T = I to
2e-6 and slogdet(R) = +1; sum of log gains exactly 0 with the log range capped; gradients reach both
tensors; empty input, 3-D input and a wrong last dimension all pass through safely. Under AdamW at
lr 5e-3 on a synthetic per-direction residual, log g converges to -log r with correlation 0.98 and
only 1.7% of coordinates at the cap, so the intended solution is reachable, bounded, and does not
saturate. The reflectors are initialised at N(0,1) scale, not unit norm, so a fixed optimiser step
is a small RELATIVE change to a direction that is normalised inside the forward; decoupled weight
decay shrinks a reflector uniformly and therefore leaves its direction untouched. Shapes are [64,768]
and [768], which the optim's addressing-pair rule (shape[0] == key_d, shape[1] != D) and its (D,D)
spectral cap both skip, so neither post-step operator changes meaning.

BIGGEST RISK
Rich-get-richer. The optimum g ~ 1/r rewards directions the model ALREADY predicts well, so if the
content subspace is the hardest thing in the target it will be shrunk instead of amplified, and the
inverse will then amplify exactly the error that decides the ranking -- the mechanism would move
comp_ca down, not sideways. The variance term should dominate that (for equal relative skill,
g ~ 1/sqrt(var), and the content subspace is the low-variance one), and the cap of 12 bounds how
badly it can go either way, but the sign of the effect is an empirical question this run answers.
A second, smaller risk: the objective's own per-dimension precision weighting (w ~ 1/mse_d, clamped
to [0.25, 4]) partially counteracts the gain, leaving the net effect linear in g rather than
quadratic, so the measured effect may be smaller than the transform's nominal range suggests.
