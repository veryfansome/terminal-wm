AXES CHANGED: optim only (r18_spectral_capped_transition_readout -> r7_isotropy_retracted_addressing_transport). Every other axis is the parent's impl and params verbatim. The mechanism is entirely optimizer-side — it changes the geometry of weights that both the parent's and the crossover partner's architectures already contain — so no other axis has to move with it, and moving one would confound the measurement.

THE CROSSOVER. Parent and partner select the SAME optim impl, so there is nothing to recombine on this axis directly. What the partner contributes is an architectural prior that I lifted into the optimizer. The partner's arch (r25_role_addressed_transport_store) explicitly initialises the (D,D) transport readout tr_read as a scaled identity, 0.1*I — a perfect isometry, every singular value equal — whereas the parent's arch line (r18/r22) initialises tr_read and x_out at exactly zero. The partner's designer decided that the map carrying retrieved CONTENT into prediction space should start out as a well-conditioned, content-preserving operator. That is an init, and an init is forgotten. My claim is that it should be a maintained invariant of training, and the optimizer is the only place an invariant like that can live. So this candidate keeps every mechanism both parents share (Muon on the delta-rule addressing projections, the soft spectral cap on (D,D) readouts, the warmup-hold-cosine-floor schedule) and adds one new operator that generalises the partner's isometric-readout prior to both lineages and to a second class of weights.

THE MECHANISM. After each optimizer step, for each selected 2-D weight W, compute the Newton-Schulz polar factor Q of W (the same quintic iteration Muon already uses on its updates, applied here to the weight), rescale Q to W's exact Frobenius norm to get T, blend W <- (1-eta)W + eta*T, then rescale the result back to W's original Frobenius norm exactly. This is a retraction toward the manifold of scaled isometries (Cisse et al.'s Parseval orthogonality step, recast as a stable convex blend with a self-referential target scale instead of a fixed one). Two properties matter and both are structural, not empirical. First, it is unconditionally stable: the target is a convex combination of the current weight and a same-norm matrix, so it cannot diverge for any eta in (0,1), unlike the raw Newton step W <- (1+b)W - (b/s^2)WW'W, which explodes on a low-rank matrix whose top singular value exceeds ~2.2x its RMS singular value — exactly the state these weights are in. Second, it is exactly norm-preserving, so it is a pure CONDITIONING operator: it moves spectral mass out of the dominant singular directions into the starved ones and touches nothing else. Magnitude control stays entirely with weight decay and the inherited spectral cap, which is retained unchanged as a ceiling. On an exactly-zero weight the operator is a no-op by construction (the Frobenius guard fires), so nothing is injected before gradients have moved the weight off zero.

WHERE IT APPLIES AND WHY THOSE TWO SITES. The operator selects (a) every 2-D weight whose output width is key_d — the delta-rule address projections file_read/file_write/path_read/path_write/tr_path, r22's cross-attention xq/xk, and r25's multi-head hashing addr_proj — and (b) every (D,D) weight, i.e. the content readouts tr_read and x_out. These are the two places where a chain of silent moves can lose the identity of what it is carrying. At (a), the address map is a 176-dim command feature compressed to a 64-dim key that is then unit-normalised downstream; if that map is ill-conditioned, two different paths land on nearly the same key and a write to one silently clobbers the other, so the chain's routing collapses into recency. Driving the map toward a row-isometry maximally spreads distinct path features over the key space and is the direct minimiser of address collision. Crucially, these maps are used only after _unit()/_quotient() normalisation, so their scale is already irrelevant to the function — a norm-preserving retraction changes ONLY the thing that matters there and cannot perturb magnitudes at all. At (b), the readout is the map from retrieved content to prediction. Zero-init linear maps trained by gradient descent on strongly correlated inputs learn their singular directions sequentially and stay effectively low-rank for a long time (the deep-linear greedy-learning result); a low-rank readout maps two distinct contents whose difference lies near its null space onto the same prediction. The inherited spectral cap only bounds the largest singular value — it constrains the top and does nothing for the starved bottom, so it does not prevent this collapse. Driving the readout toward a scaled rotation makes it content-preserving by construction.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca SPECIFICALLY. The metric is native_hit minus swap_stayed over the same board. Any predictor that keys on WHICH LOCATION is read predicts identically in both arms and contributes exactly zero per window. The only channel in this architecture that can produce a non-cancelling difference is the one that actually carries a particular content's identity through the moves: address-then-transport. Both ways that channel degrades — key collision at the address map, null-space collapse at the content readout — are failures of CONDITIONING, not of magnitude, and the parent's optimizer regulates only magnitude. That is the gap this candidate closes. If the parent's negative score reflects a routing channel that is present but too degenerate to carry identity, then raising the conditioning of exactly those maps should raise native_hit while leaving swap_stayed roughly where it is, which is the differential.

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE. Unmeasured — I cannot run the real harness from the jail and I am not extrapolating from any miniature run. The prediction I am willing to be wrong about: comp_ca rises above the parent's -0.0037, driven by native_hit rather than by a drop in swap_stayed, and the effect concentrates in the deeper (d4+) windows, where more moves mean more chances for a colliding address or a collapsed readout to lose the content. If the effect instead appears as a fall in swap_stayed with native_hit flat, the mechanism is not doing what I claim and the result should be read as noise on a 89-window slice. Cost is small and bounded: the retraction adds one 5-iteration Newton-Schulz pass per selected matrix per step, dominated by the two (768,768) readouts at roughly 27 GFLOP per step total, which is a small fraction of a training step at this batch size and does not threaten the fixed step budget. eta, the Newton-Schulz depth and the application period are genome params, and eta=0 recovers the parent's optimizer exactly.

BIGGEST RISK. The retraction is norm-preserving, which means lifting the starved singular directions necessarily takes mass OUT of the dominant ones. For the (D,D) content readouts this forces the map to keep directions the model may never have learned anything useful about, and it caps how much the model can amplify the few directions it has learned. If most of the training loss is carried by a genuinely low-rank readout, eta=0.04 may bind hard enough to cost more in fit than it buys in content distinguishability, and the score falls. The address projections are the safer half of the mechanism — they are scale-invariant downstream, so there the retraction has no magnitude side effect at all. A cheaper, more conservative variant of this same design would apply the retraction only to the key_d address maps and leave the (D,D) readouts to the inherited spectral cap.
