AXES CHANGED: objective only (r22_exact_target_equivalence_quotient -> r7_board_conditional_routed_contrast).
All six other axes keep the parent's impl names and params verbatim. The mechanism is entirely
loss-side: it needs no new module, no new parameter, and no change to how tokens are built, so
touching arch / stream / head / optim / target / batcher would only add unreasoned risk.

THE ONE MECHANISM I CHANGED

The parent selects its hard negatives with a batch-GLOBAL band-pass on target-target distance (the
"confusability ring"): every row in the batch is a candidate, and candidates are importance-weighted
by how close their target embedding is to the anchor's. I replace that distance heuristic with the
structurally correct candidate set: the anchor's OWN BOARD. The listwise softmax now runs over the
rows of the same trajectory and nothing else.

The enabling observation is mechanical, not statistical. The harness builds ctx["prev"] as
torch.cat([zeros_like(tgt[:, :1]), tgt[:, :-1]], dim=1) and then flattens with cmd_mask in
row-major order, so the strict-causal previous-observation channel is EXACTLY the zero vector at,
and only at, each trajectory's first command row (targets are per-dimension standardized embeddings;
none is exactly zero in all 768 dims). A cumsum over that indicator recovers the trajectory id of
every flattened row exactly, for variable-length trajectories, with no assumption that the batch is
a rectangle. I verified this reconstruction on a synthetic batch with lengths 26/31/29/32: it
recovers the segment sizes exactly. This is causal batch structure only; it feeds gradients, never
the forward pass, so the no-future-leakage contract is untouched.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

The score is a paired difference over one board: the native chain of moves versus the role-swapped
chain, read at the same location, with the same read token in both arms. Anything that keys on the
read token cancels exactly. The only thing that can survive is separating the contents that are
sitting on THIS board, and the metric's foils are precisely those contents. The parent's global
contrast spends most of its gradient separating things the metric never compares: rows from other
images, other file types, other trajectories. Those negatives are easy, they dominate the softmax
by sheer count (roughly two thousand candidates against about thirty on-board ones), and separating
them is off-target capacity. Conditioning the contrast on the board makes the discriminative task
the metric's task. Outside this domain this is the conditional-contrastive construction (Tsai et
al., conditional contrastive learning): instead of sampling negatives from the marginal p(target),
sample them from p(target | conditioning variable), so the learned representation cannot score by
exploiting the conditioning variable itself. Here the conditioning variable is the board, and the
degenerate solution it forecloses is exactly the one the role-swap cancels out.

The second half of the same idea is the anchor weight. A row earns weight (1 + rho * route_i) where
route_i is 1 when the row has an EARLIER same-board row with a numerically identical target, that
duplicate group is small (<= 4), and the earlier row's command differs (command-embedding cosine gap
above a ramp of 0.02). That is a causal, exact statement of "the content at this location arrived
here through a chain of silent moves and was seen earlier on this board". I checked the real
trajectories in evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl: rendered-observation duplicate groups are
sharply bimodal - 70 groups of size 2 and 4 of size 3 (the read-establish / read-after-move content
pairs), versus groups of size 8 to 15 (the empty-output class produced by mv and by redirects).
The size cap keeps the empty-output class out; the command-gap ramp keeps out the 16 rows that are
plain re-reads of the same path. On a synthetic board carrying all four cases, route fires at 1.0
on exactly the routed re-reads and 0.0 on establishing reads, mv rows, same-command repeats and
noise. Note that only the LATER member of the pair is upweighted: on a held-out image the
establishing read of a random canary is unpredictable, while the read after the move chain is
predictable precisely because the content already appeared as an observation token earlier in the
same context window. Upweighting the establishing read would spend the budget on noise.

WHAT I KEPT FROM THE PARENT

Exact-target equivalence classes as multi-positives with occurrence mass divided by class size (now
computed within the board, which also automatically discounts the large empty-output class), the
class-balanced MSE anchor, per-dimension precision weighting, the focal hardness factor, and the
gated repulsion margin in the eval's own squared-L2 decision variable. The repulsion's confusable
target is now the NEAREST distinct in-board content rather than a ring-weighted mixture, which is
the same statement of the mechanism change. I deliberately left _ANCHOR at the parent's 0.05: the
board-local negative log likelihood is roughly log(2000/30) ~ 4 nats smaller than the global one, so
the MSE anchor's relative weight rises on its own and re-tuning it would confound the comparison.

WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE, AND HOW IT COULD BE WRONG

Prediction: comp_ca above the parent's +0.0037, driven by a higher native_hit with swap_stayed
roughly unchanged, because the added pressure is specifically on resolving which of the board's
contents sits at the read location - the quantity that does not cancel between the two arms. I have
NOT measured this; no number here is an observation. The synthetic checks establish correctness and
cost only: gradients are finite in every degenerate batch I could construct (all boards length 1,
all targets identical, all distinct, n=2, float64), a constant prediction leaves a large positive
loss with nonzero gradient so the objective is anti-collapse safe, an exact prediction drives it to
~5e-4, and swapping the routed rows' predictions to another content on the same board raises the
loss from 0.0006 to 1.21 - the mechanism is pointed at the right rows. Cost is 25 ms per step on
CPU at n=1984 while building strictly fewer n-by-n tensors than the parent, so it fits the fixed
step budget.

Biggest way this fails: the board-local candidate set is small enough that the listwise term
saturates early - most in-board negatives are trivially separable empty-output rows and unrelated
system-file reads, so once those are handled the gradient collapses onto the handful of routed rows
per board, and if the arch cannot carry content identity through six moves at all, that concentrated
gradient becomes concentrated noise and the run ends up below the parent rather than above it. The
focal factor and the routing weight both amplify that concentration, so this design has no fallback
if the routed rows are simply unlearnable under this budget. A second, smaller risk: dropping global
negatives removes all pressure to separate contents ACROSS boards, and if the frozen-encoder space
is poorly conditioned the MSE anchor alone may not keep the global geometry usable.
