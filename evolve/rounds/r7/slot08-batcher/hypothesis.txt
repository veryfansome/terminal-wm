AXIS CHANGED: batcher only (r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum -> r7_image_balanced_depth_rcbd).
Every other axis keeps the parent's impl and params verbatim. The mechanism is a change to which
sequences co-occur in a gradient step; it needs no cooperating change in arch, stream, head,
objective, optim or target, and changing any of those would confound the one comparison this slot
can make against its own parent.

WHAT THE PARENT DOES, AND THE SPECIFIC THING I THINK IS WRONG WITH IT
The parent anneals the batch from uniform to 75% blocked on a SINGLE system image
(n_block_images=1, hard_frac_max=0.75). Its stated reason is negative-pool geometry: densify
same-system in-batch negatives to match the eval's same-verb same-system foils. My claim is that
this buys a property the batch already had, and pays for it with a property the eval directly
punishes.

  - It buys little. The training loss flattens over EVERY command position of every sequence in
    the batch. Trajectories in this root run 26-32 steps (checked in
    evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl), so a batch of B sequences supplies roughly 30B negative
    positions. Even an exactly image-uniform batch of 64 over the 8 images present in the split
    (alpine, ubuntu, debian, node, python, redis, postgres, nginx -- also checked in the sample)
    leaves 8 sequences, i.e. about 240 positions, from each image in EVERY batch. Same-system
    negatives are already dense at one eighth of a batch; going to three quarters of a batch is
    perhaps a 6x on a quantity that was not the binding constraint.
  - It costs the thing that is measured. Windows are held out BY IMAGE, so this is literally a
    domain-generalization problem with system image as the environment label. The parent makes
    75% of every single gradient step come from ONE environment. The step direction is then
    dominated by whatever is idiosyncratic to that image's paths, file formats and output
    shapes; the invariant part of the mv-transport rule is only recovered by averaging ACROSS
    steps, where the optimizer's momentum, the LR schedule and the network's own plasticity sit
    in between and do not average cleanly.

MECHANISM IMPORTED FROM OUTSIDE THE DOMAIN
A randomized complete block design (Fisher's blocking, the standard two-way agricultural/clinical
layout). In an RCBD you have a treatment factor whose effect you want to estimate and a nuisance
factor you want to remove, and you arrange the units so that every treatment level appears an
equal number of times inside every block; the nuisance main effect then leaves the treatment
contrast unchanged rather than having to be averaged out over replicates. The same construction
is what the domain-generalization literature calls a balanced-domain minibatch sampler (every
DomainBed-style method draws an equal number of examples from each environment per step, which
is a precondition for the averaged gradient at each step to be the cross-environment gradient
rather than one environment's).

Translated here:
  TREATMENT = routing depth. Each training sequence is replayed through a symbolic content
    tracker built from its command strings alone: "mv A B" carries the content at A to B and
    destroys A, "cat A > B" carries a copy, "cat A >> B" carries a copy under a max, a bare
    "cat A" reads whatever now sits at A. The sequence's row is the hop count of its deepest
    routed read, capped at 5. I checked this parse against the ground-truth cups metadata in
    evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl: it reproduces the recorded chain depth on 23 of 24
    trajectories (the one disagreement is a deeper cvar redirect chain in the same trajectory,
    which is a real transport chain, not a parse error). Observed depths run 1..6 with the mode
    at 2; the scored slice is d3 and d4+.
  NUISANCE = system image, read straight off s["image"].
  LAYOUT = every step allocates the batch across depth rows by largest-remainder rounding of a
    mixture between the empirical depth distribution and a monotone depth shape (1+r)^1, annealed
    from pure-empirical over the first 30% of training so the opening of training is the natural
    distribution. Each row's quota is then spread over the images occupying that row as an equal
    base share plus remainder units handed out fewest-options-row-first to whichever image
    currently holds the fewest slots in this batch, ties broken by a step-rotating offset. The
    result is exact image balance within the batch and near-balance within each depth row.
  GUARD = each row's quota is capped at rate_cap=6x the visit rate uniform sampling would give
    it, so flattening the depth distribution cannot turn into memorizing the handful of depth-5+
    trajectories. Row cells are drawn from per-cell shuffled epoch cursors, so a cell is
    exhausted without replacement before it reshuffles -- lower variance than iid draws and
    guaranteed coverage of the rare deep cells.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca RATHER THAN THE CAPABILITY GATE
comp_ca is a paired difference between the native chain and the role-swapped chain on the SAME
board. Anything the model knows about a system's surface -- what an nginx config looks like, what
that image's ls output is -- appears identically in both arms and cancels. The only thing that
can produce a positive difference is machinery that binds a particular content's identity to a
particular chain of moves. That machinery is exactly the image-invariant part of what the model
could learn, and it is the part a single-image batch is worst at isolating. So the prediction is
directional and specific: making image a balanced nuisance factor at every step should raise the
differential without needing to raise the absolute pick rate, and it has no route to inflate the
gate reading, because the gate's per-cell ceiling is not a function of batch composition.

FALSIFIABLE PREDICTIONS
1. comp_ca beats the parent's +0.6142 on the same environment and split.
2. The gain shows up disproportionately in the d4+ windows (29 of the 89), because those are the
   windows whose native-vs-swap difference requires carrying identity through the most moves, and
   because the depth shape (1+r)^1 raises the d>=3 share of training from about 50% of sequences
   to about 66% (measured on the sample's depth labels, not on the real split).
3. private.shortcut_leaning should not rise relative to the parent. Nothing in this mechanism
   touches the command tokens, the read position, or move order; it only changes which
   trajectories share a step. A first-mover positional lookup is neither helped nor hindered by
   image balance, so if the differential moves, it should move without the shortcut indicator
   moving with it.
4. If the mechanism is inert rather than wrong, the failure signature is a comp_ca within noise
   of the parent with an unchanged health reading -- which would say the same-image blocking was
   never the binding factor in either direction.

WHAT IS UNMEASURED
I cannot run the harness from the jail, so no fitness claim here is measured. What IS measured, on
the real trajectories in evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl and on replicated synthetic splits
built from them: the batcher returns exactly bs in-range indices for every step including bs=1,
bs=3, total_steps=0 and n=1; it never mutates fit; it is bit-reproducible from the seed; it never
touches global torch RNG; with populated (depth, image) cells it puts exactly bs/8 sequences from
each of the 8 images in every batch, with per-image totals equal to the last unit over 6000 steps;
per-sequence visit counts stay inside a 3.3x band; and every parameter changes the emitted index
stream (rate_cap 1.5 / 6 / 100 moves the deep-row share from 1.6% to 4.7% to 51.9% on a stress
split, depth_shape 0 / 1 / 2 moves the d5 share from 17.8% to 24.3% to 29.8%). Setup parses all
command strings once (0.08s for 5760 sequences) and the per-step cost is a few dozen Python
operations plus one randperm, about 0.9ms per batch of 64 in pure Python with no tensor
allocations in the inner loop -- negligible against a training step.

BIGGEST RISK
The parent's same-system hard negatives may be load-bearing in a way my position-count argument
misses -- if the objective r5_decision_direction_metric_quotient forms its negative pool per
sequence rather than over the flattened batch, then cutting same-image density from 75% to 12.5%
cuts the hard-negative supply almost 6x and the candidate loses on exactly the axis the parent
was tuned for. I cannot read that objective's source from the jail, so this stays a stated
assumption, not a verified one. A second, smaller risk: if the real split is not image-balanced
the way the jail sample is, images with few sequences in a deep row get their members revisited
far more often than images with many, trading the image bias I am removing for a within-image
sampling bias I am introducing; the rate cap bounds this at the row level but not at the cell
level.
