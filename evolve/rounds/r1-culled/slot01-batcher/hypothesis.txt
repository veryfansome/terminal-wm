CANDIDATE: batcher = r23_aliaschain_collision_replay (new), arch = r22_prefix_content_xattention
(taken from the crossover partner). objective / optim / head / target / stream inherited unchanged.

WHAT THE CROSSOVER IS
The two parents differ on exactly one axis: arch. The parent carries
r18_pathstate_latent_transition_worldmodel (per-path delta-rule slots whose written value is a
learned command-conditioned affine transition of the slot's current content). The partner carries
r22_prefix_content_xattention, which is a SUBCLASS of that same r18 module: it calls
super().forward() and then adds one channel — single-head cross-attention from causal command
hidden states over the strictly-earlier prefix OBSERVATION embeddings, injected through a zero-init
(D,D) readout. r22 is therefore the literal union of the two parents' arch mechanisms, and it is
bit-identical to r18 at init, so nothing the parent could express is lost. Both parents already
share the r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum batcher, so the batcher axis has no partner mechanism to
merge and is where I spend my invention; I keep its system-blocking arm as one of two arms inside
the new impl rather than discarding it.

1. MECHANISM (what the code computes that neither parent does)
r23 parses each TRAINING trajectory's command strings once at construction and runs a
last-mutating-touch recurrence over argument tokens (each argument contributes both its full token
and its basename, so a rename/move that preserves the basename links the two steps). For step t,
depth(t) = max over t's argument keys k of reached[k], where reached[k] is the hop count stored the
last time a MUTATING command (mv/cp/rm/... or a `>` redirect) touched k; a mutating step then writes
reached[k] = depth(t)+1 for all its keys. From this it derives, per trajectory, carry = sum over
NON-mutating (read) steps of max(0, depth-1) and max_depth, and sets score = carry + 0.5*max_depth.
Sampling weight w_i = (1 + clamp(score_i, 0, 8))^2. Second, it builds an inverted index over
argument tokens and keeps every posting list with 2 <= document frequency <= 0.2*N as a "collision
group". Each batch is then three parts under a shared ramp (full strength at 30% of training):
(a) a uniform iid remainder (>= 25% of the batch at full ramp), (b) a depth-prioritized arm drawn
from w, and (c) up to half the batch as collision blocks — anchor drawn from w, then the block is
filled WITHOUT replacement from one of the anchor's rare-token posting lists (or, one draw in four,
from the anchor's image group, which is the parent's sysblock mechanism retained).
The parents' batcher computes none of this: it groups only by s["image"] and is blind to command
content, so it can express "same system" but not "same path" and not "this trajectory contains deep
carry chains".

2. WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca
Two distinct levers, both aimed at carrying identity across hops.
(i) Row mix. The loss is a mean over cmd-position ROWS in the batch. The scored eval windows are
d3/d4+ (60 and 29 of 89); shell trajectories are dominated by depth-0/1 steps, so under uniform
sampling deep rows are a small minority of the gradient and the deep behaviour is undertrained
relative to what is measured. Weighting trajectories by carry raises the share of deep rows
directly. On a synthetic trajectory set built for this check the measured deep-row rate went from
0.017 (uniform) to 0.030 (defaults) while index coverage stayed at 900/900 — the ratio on the real
capability-pack root is UNMEASURED.
(ii) Negative geometry. The inherited objective is band-pass: it reweights in-batch negatives by
target-target distance, giving ~0 weight to near-duplicate targets (false negatives) and up to
(1+kappa)=5x weight to close-but-DISTINCT targets. Uniform batches rarely produce that band, and
same-image blocking mostly produces the two useless ends of it — either byte-identical outputs
(zeroed by the dupmask) or unrelated commands (weight ~1). Two trajectories that touch the SAME
rare path token are exactly the close-but-distinct case: the same `cat /b/f.cfg`, the same system,
but different content sitting there because a different chain of moves preceded it. Those rows
cannot be separated by the queried name, by the verb, or by which file moved first or last — the
only separating variable is which file's content the path now holds. So the batcher supplies
precisely the negatives the ring objective was built to upweight and that the metric's role-swap
construction rewards, and it supplies them against anchors chosen for having deep carry chains.
That pairing is what r22's prefix-content cross-attention and r18's transition slots need gradient
on: both read content out of the prefix, and neither gets a discriminative signal unless the batch
contains a row whose content differs from the anchor's at the same address.

3. WHAT I EXPECT IF I AM WRONG, AND THE CHEAPEST CHECK
If wrong, the failure will look like one of these, in decreasing order of likelihood:
- comp_ca stays at ~0 while next-obs retrieval health DROPS versus the parent. That means the
  reweighting cost generalization (fewer effective trajectories, more duplicated addresses) without
  buying compositional depth. Cheapest observation: the reported retrieval-health number next to
  comp_ca; a drop with no comp_ca gain falsifies lever (ii).
- comp_ca stays at ~0 and retrieval health is unchanged. That means batch composition is not the
  binding constraint at all and the arch/head are the bottleneck. Cheapest observation: run the same
  batcher genome against the parent arch (r18) — if both are ~0, the batcher axis is inert here.
- The d3 bucket improves but d4+ does not, or the native-pick rate rises while the swap-following
  rate does not. That would say the model learned "deep windows are special" rather than routing
  content, i.e. it is keying on disturbance, and the paired difference will not move.
A directly checkable prediction about the code itself: on the real train split the distribution of
score_i must be non-degenerate. If nearly all trajectories score 0 (my token parser fails to
recognize the mutating verbs actually present in this data root, or the arguments are not
whitespace-separated paths), then w is uniform, the deep arm collapses to uniform sampling, and the
candidate degrades to sysblock-without-the-image-restriction — a small loss, not a crash. Printing
the score histogram at construction time is the cheapest observation that separates "mechanism ran"
from "mechanism was a no-op".

4. THE RISK I SEE
The parser is a heuristic over command surface strings, and it is the single point on which
everything rests. Two ways it bites. First, over-linking: including basenames as alias keys means
common names (`config`, `x.txt`, `.`) chain unrelated steps and inflate score for shallow
trajectories, diluting the deep arm toward noise; the df<=0.2*N filter protects the collision groups
from this but the depth score has no equivalent filter. Second, sampling concentration: at alpha=2
with a score cap of 8 the weight ratio inside the deep arm reaches 81:1, so if only a handful of
trajectories carry deep chains the deep arm effectively trains on a tiny subset, which is exactly
the wrong thing for a metric held out BY IMAGE. The uniform arm's >=25% floor and the score cap are
the mitigations, but they are calibrated blind — I have not seen the real score distribution, and
the whole design is unmeasured on the real data root. A secondary, smaller risk: prioritizing deep
trajectories correlates with prioritizing long ones, and collate pads to the batch maximum, so the
per-step cost can rise inside a fixed step budget. On the synthetic check maxn was unchanged, but
that set had a narrow length range and the real effect is unmeasured.
