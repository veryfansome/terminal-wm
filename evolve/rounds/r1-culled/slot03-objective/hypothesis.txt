CANDIDATE: partialled-out identity residuals (objective) + a calibrated counterfactual gap (head)

AXES CHANGED: objective, head.  Inherited unchanged: arch (r18_pathstate_latent_transition_
worldmodel), optim (r18_spectral_capped_transition_readout), batcher (r6_sysblock_hardneg_

(baseline_interleave).

WHAT THE CROSSOVER IS.  The two parents share objective, arch, optim and batcher; they differ in
exactly one place - the head.  My parent carries r22_masked_endpoint_trunk_task, which TRAINS the
trunk on the frozen instrument's own obs-missing layout.  The partner carries r21_counterfactual_
history_guidance, which at EVAL time runs the same odd layout twice, once with the prefix present
and once with everything before the last mutation command padded away, and pushes the read-position
prediction along (conditional - no_history), capped at 0.2 RMS.  Neither parent trains the arm the
other one relies on: r21 extrapolates along a direction the trunk was never optimised for, and r22
never exercises the history-masked arm at all.  My head runs both mechanisms and adds the term that
joins them.


1. MECHANISM - WHAT THE CODE COMPUTES THAT THE PARENTS DO NOT

(a) objective r23_partialled_identity_residual_contrast (WANTS_CTX = True).  From ctx["cmd"] - the
causal command embedding of each command row - it builds, under no_grad, a leave-one-out
Nadaraya-Watson estimate of the command-conditional mean target:
    q = softmax_j( cos(cmd_i, cmd_j) / 0.08 ),  diagonal set to -inf
    mhat_i = sum_j q_ij tgt_j
    tightness_i = ( sum_j q_ij cos(cmd_i,cmd_j) )^4  in [0,1]
    centre_i = tightness_i * mhat_i
and subtracts the SAME centre_i from both pred_i and tgt_i.  The focal listwise L2-InfoNCE, the
repulsion hinge and a new residual-direction cosine term all run on those residuals; the per-row
diagonal is algebraically unchanged (pred_i - centre_i) - (tgt_i - centre_i) = pred_i - tgt_i, so
what moves is only the geometry of the NEGATIVES and the direction term.  Two further pieces:
in-batch negatives are boosted not only by the parent's target-target confusability ring but by
relu(cos(cmd_i,cmd_j))^2 with kappa 6, so the hardest negatives are the observations produced by
the SAME command in a different history; and rows are reweighted by the conditional variance of the
target inside their own command neighbourhood (cvar_i = sum_j q_ij ||tgt_j - mhat_i||^2 / D,
row_w = 1 + 3 * tightness_i * cvar_i / mean_cvar, capped at 6, mean-normalised), so gradient mass
goes to the rows where the command does NOT determine the answer.  Near-duplicate targets are
masked out of the softmax with -inf so genuine repeats are not punished as negatives.  A raw-space
listwise term (weight 0.35) and the MSE anchor (0.05) are retained.

(b) head r23_calibrated_counterfactual_gap.  r22's aux_loss verbatim, r21's eval-time guidance
wrapper verbatim, plus one new train-time term.  On the same in-batch masked-endpoint layout that
r22 constructs (prefix through the last gated mutation command c_m, obs_m padded away, the later
read command spliced onto position 2m+2), it forwards the trunk ONCE MORE with every position
before 2m additionally padded - exactly r21's counterfactual pad - and regresses that history-
masked read prediction onto a detached leave-one-out command-kernel estimate of E[z_obs | read
command] taken from the batch's own command rows, weighted by the same neighbourhood-tightness
factor.  Ramped over 800 steps, weight 0.25, every second step, at most 48 pairs, deterministic
top-k selection (no RNG draw of its own).  The conditional arm keeps its r22 supervision toward the
true later observation z_j.

2. WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

The score is a paired difference: the same board, the same query, the native move chain versus a
chain in which two files exchanged their moves.  The queried command is IDENTICAL in both arms.
Therefore E[z_obs | read command] is identical in both arms, and the entire scored quantity lives
in the residual  z_obs - E[z_obs | read command].  Everything the parents' loss spends on the
command-predictable component is, by construction, spent on a quantity the metric differences away.
My objective removes that component from both sides of the contrast, boosts the negatives that
share the command, and up-weights precisely the rows whose command neighbourhood disagrees about
the answer - which is the operational definition of "the answer depends on which file got carried
here".  The parents structurally cannot express this: r12's negative weighting is a function of
target-target distance only, so it cannot tell "close because the same command always produces this"
from "close because a different file could be sitting here", and it has no notion of a
command-conditional mean to subtract.

Two loss-surface properties, measured directly on synthetic rows (this is a property of the loss,
not a training extrapolation):
  - Predictor A knows E[t|cmd] exactly and carries zero identity; predictor B has the SAME raw MSE
    (0.8525 vs 0.8530) but its error is isotropic noise around the true target.  Parent separates
    them by 2.20; mine by 7.97.
  - Swapping the targets of two rows that share a command (the metric's own counterfactual) moves
    the parent's loss by 0.0276 and mine by 0.0884 at a fixed prediction.
That ~3x sharper sensitivity to exactly the perturbation the metric applies is the whole claim.

For the head: r21's guided read is pred_c + clip(gain*(pred_c - pred_n)).  It improves the pick iff
(pred_c - pred_n) has positive projection on (z_true - pred_c).  If pred_n is trained to output what
is knowable WITHOUT the history - the command-conditional mean - while pred_c is trained toward the
true z_j, then pred_c - pred_n converges to z_j - E[z|cmd], the history-carried content, and the
extrapolation amplifies the swap-sensitive coordinate rather than an arbitrary difference of two
arms.  This is the same partialling-out identity as the objective, applied to the instrument's own
layout, which is why the two axes are one design and not two edits.  Note the head's term regresses
pred_n onto a finite, well-defined target rather than pushing it away from z_j; there is no
degenerate "make the no-history arm worse" direction.

3. WHAT I WOULD EXPECT TO SEE IF I AM WRONG, AND THE CHEAPEST CHECK

If the mechanism is wrong, the most likely reason is that the command-kernel neighbourhoods inside a
training batch are not the "same command, different history" sets I am assuming, but sets of merely
similar commands, so centre_i is a noisy quantity and subtracting it injects variance into the
negatives instead of removing a nuisance.  The cheapest observation: log, for one training batch,
the distribution of tightness_i and of the top off-diagonal cosine per row.  If tightness_i is
below ~0.3 for the large majority of rows, the residual space is nearly the raw space with added
noise, row_w is nearly uniform, and the objective degenerates to a slightly noisier r12 - the score
should then sit at or just under the parent's +0.0037 rather than above it.  A second, cheaper
signal: next-obs retrieval health (top1_sameverb) should be roughly preserved.  If it drops
markedly, the residual contrastive has decalibrated the raw prediction space that the eval's
squared-L2 decision variable lives in, and the raw-space listwise weight of 0.35 was too small.
For the head, the falsifier is that the score is unchanged relative to a plain r22+r21 merge, which
would say the gap was already well-oriented and training it bought nothing.

4. THE BIGGEST RISK I SEE

Interference between the two arms through shared parameters.  The head asks the SAME trunk to be
maximally history-sensitive on the full-prefix layout and deliberately history-blind (output the
command-conditional mean) on the padded layout.  Those two demands differ only in the key-padding
mask; if the trunk cannot separate them it will regress toward the conditional mean everywhere,
which is exactly the failure the metric punishes hardest, and the candidate could score BELOW its
parent rather than above it.  I have bounded this with a small weight (0.25), a 800-step smoothstep
ramp, a period of 2 and a 48-pair cap, and the term is silent until r22's own mutation gate matures
(gate floor 0.8) - but the weight is unmeasured on the real harness and I cannot rule out that it is
still too large.  Secondary risks: the objective's kernel constants (tau 0.08, tightness power 4,
kappa_cmd 6) are set from the geometry of per-dimension-standardised mean-pooled embeddings and are
untuned; and cost is up (measured on CPU with synthetic batches of B=64: objective 7.3 ms vs the
parent's 3.0 ms, and one extra ~60 ms trunk forward every second step against a ~96 ms main forward,
so roughly +10-15% wall clock - unmeasured on the real harness and its device).
