---------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE MECHANISM — what this code computes that neither parent does
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

BATCHER. At construction it parses each train sequence's `cmds` once (read-only) and
computes two things.

  (a) A lexical move-chain counter. For a move-like verb (mv/cp/ln/install/rsync/rename)
      with at least two non-flag arguments, it sets
          hop = max(chain[base(src)], chain[base(dst)]) + 1
          chain[base(src)] = chain[base(dst)] = hop
      For a read-like verb (cat/ls/head/stat/grep/wc/...), each argument basename with
      chain >= 1 adds min(hop, read_cap) to the sequence's `transport` score. So a
      sequence that renames a file twice and then reads the final name scores 2; a
      sequence with no read-after-move scores 0.

  (b) A skeleton key: the tuple of verbs, and a content key: the tuple of non-flag
      argument tuples.

Each step then builds the batch from two pools.
  - Background pool: multinomial with weights exp(r * log(1 + depth_tilt*min(transport,
    depth_cap))), where r = min(1, step / (ramp_frac * total_steps)). At r=0 this is
    uniform; at r=1 the weight ratio is bounded by 1 + depth_tilt*depth_cap = 7:1.
  - TWIN pool: a fraction of the batch, ramped to twin_frac_max, is filled with PAIRS.
    A pair is two DISTINCT sequences drawn from one group, where a group is all
    sequences sharing (image, verb-skeleton) and containing at least two distinct
    content keys. Groups are drawn proportional to the sum of their members' weights.
    If no such group exists, a fallback cascade tries (skeleton) across images, then
    (image, sorted verb multiset), then (image,) — which is exactly the parent's
    same-system blocking — and finally plain weighted sampling.

So the new object in the batch is a STRUCTURE-MATCHED, CONTENT-DIFFERENT NEGATIVE:
two trajectories on the same system with the same verb at every position, differing
only in which files the commands name. Neither parent batcher can produce this; both
key only on `s["image"]`.

HEAD. My parent's head (r20_dualpre) and the partner's head (r20_interventional_gain
_shell) both extend the same r18 forward-model consistency aux, and neither contains
the other. The new head runs BOTH arms over one shared transition operator:
  - the dual-pre arm: supervise f(s_pre, cmd) -> future same-path observation with
    s_pre taken BOTH from the raw earlier observation AND from the arch's own memory
    read (the composed state it actually holds at depth);
  - the interventional gain arm: build [prefix, c_m, PAD, c_r], get the no-write and
    full-write hypotheses, and train the 12.5K calibrator (alpha, beta) with the eval's
    own squared-L2 geometry.
It also replaces the gain arm's pair miner. The partner mines any same-path pair
(k, j) with k < j. Mine instead a TRANSPORT triple i < k < j: the same path must
already have been read at i (which lands inside the constructed prefix), and the
observation must have materially changed, (obs_i - obs_j)^2.mean() >= 0.25 — the same
quantity and the same threshold the sibling r18 miner already uses. If fewer than 4
such pairs exist in a batch, it falls back to the partner's ungated miner, so the
calibrator's training density is never starved.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca SPECIFICALLY
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The score is a paired difference between the native move chain and a chain where two
files exchange their moves. The two boards have the same commands, the same locations,
the same queried name, and the same first and last mover. Everything except "which
content is bound to which mover across the hops" is invariant, so anything a model
learns about structure contributes exactly zero.

That is precisely the failure mode a uniform or image-blocked batch invites. In
r12_antiretrieval_ring_negatives the in-batch negatives are the other targets in the
batch; under uniform or image-blocked sampling those come from trajectories with
DIFFERENT command structure, so the cheapest direction for the log-softmax is to encode
the trajectory's structural signature — which verbs at which positions, which system,
how long the sequence is. That direction is free loss reduction and metric-invariant.
Skeleton twins delete it: for the twin pair, structure is identical by construction, and
the only feature that separates the two targets is the content each command touched.
The gradient the twin pair contributes is therefore, by construction, a
content-discrimination gradient. This is the mechanism I claim earns; it is expressible
only through batch composition, which is why it belongs on this axis.

The transport tilt attacks a second, quieter loss. Both aux arms are miners: the r18/
dual-pre arm needs an i<k<j same-path triple inside the sampled rows, and the gain arm
needs a same-path readback. When the sampled sequences have no read-after-move, the aux
returns 0.0 and that step trains nothing that carries identity across hops. Tilting the
background pool toward sequences that actually read a path at the end of a multi-hop
chain raises the number of mined examples per step, so the fixed step budget buys more
gradient on the operator that does the carrying. The tilt is capped at 7:1 and ramped,
so early training still sees the natural distribution.

The head union supplies the two halves the metric needs and that each parent has only
one of. Depth needs (i) the operator to be correct on the composed memory state, not
just on a raw observation — the dual-pre arm; and (ii) a correct write at a hop whose
observation carries no content, which in shell data is every `mv` (it prints nothing) —
the gain calibrator's alpha. Composition without a silent-hop write loses the chain at
the first move; a silent-hop write without composition gets one hop right and then
drifts. The transport gate on the gain miner is the same "content, not disturbance"
rule applied to the calibrator's own training set: an ungated pair whose observation did
not change teaches alpha nothing identifiable and supplies a near-duplicate InfoNCE
target.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE IF I AM WRONG, AND THE CHEAPEST CHECK
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) The twin mechanism may never fire, because exact verb skeletons do not repeat
    within an image on this train split. Then the cascade lands at (image,) and my
    batcher IS the parent's blocking with a tilt, and the score should land near the
    parent's +0.0000. CHEAPEST CHECK: at construction, print which cascade level
    produced `groups` and len(groups) on the real train split. One line, no training.
    If level 4 fires, this candidate's batcher half is dead and only the head half is
    live.

(b) The twins may fire and still not help: the model gets better at same-verb
    discrimination but stays swap-blind. Signature: comp_ca stays ~0.00 while
    next-obs retrieval health (top1_sameverb) rises. CHEAPEST CHECK: the eval feedback
    line already splits the role-swap behaviour into "the same pick is held" vs
    "follows the swapped content" — if "held" stays ~1.0, no content is being carried
    and the twin gradient is being absorbed somewhere metric-invariant.

(c) The transport tilt may narrow the effective training set enough to cost transfer.
    Eval holds out whole IMAGES, so a narrower slice of systems is a real risk.
    Signature: retrieval health falls versus the parent at the same step budget while
    the deep-window count is unchanged. CHEAPEST CHECK: compare next-obs retrieval
    health against the parent's run; if it drops, re-run with depth_tilt=0.0, which
    turns the tilt off and leaves the twin mechanism intact — that isolates the two.

---------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE RISK I SEE IN MY OWN DESIGN
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The single biggest risk is diversity collapse. Twin blocks at up to 50% of the batch
PLUS a 7:1 transport tilt both concentrate sampling, and they concentrate it along
correlated directions (deep sequences within an image are likely to share skeletons).
Because the metric is measured on systems the model never trained on, anything that
narrows the training slice can cost more transfer than the sharper negatives buy, and
the parent's own aggressive same-image blocking (hard_frac 0.75, one image per batch)
scoring +0.0000 against a +0.0037 unmodified baseline is at least consistent with
over-concentration already hurting on this axis. My caps and ramps bound the effect but
do not remove it, and I have not measured the real group statistics.

Secondary risks: (i) the lexical chain heuristic is crude — `mv a dir/` credits the
basename "dir" rather than the moved file, `cd` is ignored, quoting and globs are not
handled — so `transport` is a noisy proxy; it only ever steers a sampling weight and
never a label, so a wrong score costs efficiency, not correctness, but it could make
the tilt near-uninformative. (ii) The transport-gated gain miner returns fewer pairs
than the partner's; the fallback keeps density, but in the regime where the fallback
fires often, that half of the head reduces to the partner's.

MEASUREMENT STATUS. Nothing here is measured on the real harness or the real data root.
What I did verify by running the code on synthetic sequences: the batcher returns
exactly bs in-range integer indices for bs in {1,2,3,7,64}, is bit-identical across two
constructions with the same seed and differs across seeds, leaves the global torch RNG
state unchanged, does not mutate `fit`, handles sequences with no `cmds` key and n=1,
and costs 0.02 s for 800 steps at bs=64 over 180 sequences; the deep-sequence share of
the batch rose from 0.31 at step 1 to 0.55 at the end of the ramp (natural 0.33). The
full genome (objective+arch+optim+target+stream+head+batcher) loads through
evolve.genome, trains 12 steps with a finite loss, and the interventional calibrator's
output layer receives non-zero gradient and moves off its zero init, while the head
correctly self-disables its gain arm on the plain r18 arch. All per-step and per-second
figures above are from a miniature synthetic probe and say nothing about the real
ranking.
