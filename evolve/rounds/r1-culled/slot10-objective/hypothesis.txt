1. THE MECHANISM — WHAT THE CODE COMPUTES THAT THE PARENTS DO NOT

The objective opts into WANTS_CTX and adds two terms on top of the inherited
confusability-ring focal listwise contrastive (which is kept verbatim: precision weights,
ring-reweighted in-batch negatives, focal listwise NLL, mse anchor, repulsion hinge).

(a) HOP-CARRY (_hop_track, weight 1.0). The harness builds ctx["prev"] as the strict-causal
shift of the target inside each padded sequence, so the first command row of every sequence has
prev exactly all-zero and every other row has prev == the previous step's observation, bitwise.
`_segments` therefore recovers the episode segmentation of the flattened batch exactly:
start = (prev.abs().sum(1) == 0), seq_id = cumsum(start) - 1. My test on a 64-sequence collate
recovered all 64 episode lengths exactly. With episode ids and row order in hand the term builds,
for every row i:
    cand_i = all strictly earlier steps of i's own episode (delta >= 1)      <- the choice set
    hop_i  = the subset at least TWO steps back        (delta >= 2)          <- the positive set
    a[i,.] = softmax over hop_i of -(tt[i,.] - min_hop tt[i,.]) / (0.05*mean_off)
    gate_i = sigmoid( (tt[i, i-1] - min_hop tt[i,.]) / (0.10*mean_off) )
and minimises gate_i * CE( softmax over cand_i of -dist2[i,.]/(0.125*mean_off) , a[i,.] ).
In words: over the episode's OWN earlier observations, the prediction must land on the one whose
CONTENT matches the answer, the positive is only ever an observation from >= 2 hops back, the
immediately preceding observation stays in the denominator as the competing candidate, and the row
is weighted by how much better the far match is than that immediately preceding observation. Rows
where copying the previous state is already right get gate ~ 0 and contribute nothing.

(b) PARTIALLED RECONSTRUCTION (weight 0.5). `_partial_operator` forms Z = [cmd, prev, 1]/sqrt(D)
from the two causal ctx channels and returns the dual ridge residual-maker
M = lambda (Z Z^T + lambda I)^-1 with lambda = ridge * mean(diag(Z Z^T)); the identity
I - Z(Z^T Z + lambda I)^-1 Z^T = lambda (Z Z^T + lambda I)^-1 is what makes this one n x n solve
instead of a 1536 x 1536 one. M is built under no_grad, so it is a constant linear operator; the
term is || M pred - M tgt ||^2 divided by a detached scale. That is a reconstruction loss on the
component of the observation that the command embedding and the previous observation cannot
LINEARLY explain, i.e. the model is scored on residual content, not on the verb prototype plus the
carried-over state.

The arch is the literal crossover: R23 inherits from BOTH r22 archs (cooperative MRO
prefix-xattention.forward -> retrieval-composition-renderer.forward -> r18), so the net gets the
partner's nonlinear composition renderer over the retrieved-content channels AND the parent's
cross-attention that copies raw earlier observation embeddings into the command prediction. Both
readouts are zero-init; I verified the composed net's forward is bit-identical (max abs diff 0.0,
eval mode) to plain r18 at init, and that stream.leakage_ok passes on it.


2. WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE THIS SCORE

The score is a paired difference between the native move-chain and a chain in which two files
swapped their moves, over the same board. Anything invariant to that swap — the queried name, the
verb, which file moved first or last, the previous state at the queried location — appears in both
arms and subtracts to zero. Only "the content that is at this location now is the content I saw
N hops ago at some other location" survives. Both parents' loss is blind to this: it receives a
flat matrix of rows with no episode structure and no command input, so its only notion of a
negative is "some other row somewhere in the batch", and its only notion of a positive is the row's
own target. It cannot ask a row to choose between two contents drawn from its own trajectory, and
it cannot know which part of its target was already implied by the command and the previous state.
Term (a) is exactly that missing question, and it is posed only on rows where the answer is NOT the
previous observation — the training-side analogue of the deep earnable windows the metric is
measured on. Term (b) removes the gradient mass that the analytic non-tracker already accounts for,
so the remaining gradient buys content discrimination instead of prototype fitting.

Directed probe of the mechanism (synthetic episodes where step 5's content equals step 2's / 0's
and differs from step 4's; mechanism check only, NOT a score estimate): the track term scores a
copy-the-previous-observation predictor at 7.44, a copy-the-matching-content-from->=2-hops-back
predictor at 3.00, and the exact target at 0.30. Started from the midpoint of the two candidates,
its gradient on the query rows has projection +0.00257 toward the >=2-hop matching content and
-0.00257 toward the 1-hop-back observation. That is the sign the metric pays for.

Cost, measured on CPU: the objective is ~2.4x the parent objective (14 ms vs 7 ms at n=416,
63 ms vs 25 ms at n=1500) against ~258 ms for one arch forward+backward at bs=64, so ~3-15%
added step time. The n x n solve is guarded to n <= 4096. I have NOT measured end-to-end training
time or any fitness number.


3. WHAT I EXPECT TO SEE IF I AM WRONG, AND THE CHEAPEST OBSERVATION THAT SHOWS IT

The single cheapest check: instrument `_hop_track` for one training run and log
(i) the fraction of rows with gate_i > 0.5 and (ii) the term's value over steps. If the gate-active
fraction on real capability-pack batches is below ~2%, the centrepiece never fires and this
candidate is the parent's objective plus a small conditional-MSE regulariser; expect fitness within
noise of the parent, and the fix is to widen the positive set (delta >= 2 back to delta >= 1) or
drop the gate. If the gate-active fraction is healthy but the term plateaus near log|cand_i| while
next-obs retrieval health is unchanged, the model is satisfying the listwise term and ignoring the
track term, and the weight is too low. If instead retrieval health COLLAPSES while comp_ca stays
near zero, term (b) is over-removing: the ridge is stripping content-carrying directions and the
prediction has drifted off the eval's full-space geometry — visible as mse_cond falling while
latent_mse to the true observation rises. Second cheapest: assert that segment recovery matches the
true sequence lengths on one real batch; if a real z_obs row is ever exactly all-zero the
segmentation silently merges episodes and (a) becomes cross-episode nonsense.


4. THE BIGGEST RISK I SEE

Term (a) supervises "point at the earlier observation whose content matches the answer", and in
real shell trajectories the commonest way for two observations to be near-identical is not a file
moving but a command being repeated with no state change (two `ls` of the same directory, `pwd`
twice, repeated failures with the same stderr). If that is what the gate mostly selects, I have
trained a duplicate-detector over episode history rather than a content-transporter, and the
duplicate-detector is invariant to the role swap — it cancels, and the term is at best wasted
capacity and at worst a distractor that costs the listwise term gradient. The delta >= 2 restriction
and the gate against the immediately-previous observation remove the most degenerate version of
this (adjacent repeats) but not the general one. A secondary risk: term (a) and the inherited
dupmask pull in opposite directions on near-identical targets — dupmask zeroes them as negatives
while (a) makes them positives — and although they act on different index sets (cross-batch vs
within-episode), a batch that is heavily image-blocked by the inherited batcher puts many
same-episode-like pairs into the cross-batch set too, so the two can partially fight.
