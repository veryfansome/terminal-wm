CANDIDATE: head = r23_routed_content_alignment, arch = r22_prefix_content_xattention
(objective / optim / batcher inherited from the parent unchanged).

WHAT THE CROSSOVER TAKES FROM EACH PROGRAM

The two programs I was given differ in exactly two places. The partner contributes the arch: a
prefix-content cross-attention channel over the strictly-earlier observations, whose values are the
raw 768-d observation embeddings, injected into the command-position prediction through a zero-init
(D,D) readout and a sigmoid gate. Because that arch subclasses the parent's arch, taking it keeps
every mechanism the parent's arch had (delta-rule path memory, latent transition operator,
sysfilm) and adds one. The parent contributes the objective: the exact-target-equivalence quotient,
which puts numerically-equal targets into a single class. That choice is not cosmetic on this data
— in a cups window the test read's target embedding is the SAME vector as the expose read's target,
so the partner's r12 objective would place a correct answer in its own contrastive denominator as a
negative. Keeping the quotient objective and taking the partner's arch is the combination that
keeps the working half of each.

WHAT THE NEW HEAD COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES

Both parents run the same head (r18_transition_forwardmodel_consistency). That head mines triples
by COMMAND-embedding similarity and supervises the latent transition operator to RECONSTRUCT a
future observation, explicitly requiring the mined pre/post observations to DIFFER
(change_floor = 0.25). Nothing in either program supervises an addressing distribution, and nothing
in either program mines the event this benchmark is built out of: the same content reappearing at a
different name.

The new head mines that event directly, from the token stream alone, with no metadata:

  a step j is a TRANSPORT READ if there exists an earlier step i in the same row with
      mean squared distance(obs_i, obs_j) / 768  <=  dup_eps      (same content)
      mean squared distance(cmd_i, cmd_j) / 768  >   cmd_eps      (different command text)

Checked against evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl (24 real trajectories, rendered exactly as
realenv.seq_worldmodel.render_obs / render_cmd render them, with the two degeneracy filters below
applied): the rule fires 70 times over 24 trajectories, and 61 of those 70 hits are steps whose
withheld metadata role is literally cups_test or cvar_test — the routed reads. The remaining 9 are
incidental reads. About 2.9 supervised reads per trajectory, so at bs=64 roughly 190 supervised
reads per training step. The move depths present in those trajectories run 1..6, i.e. the aux is
supervised at the same depths the scored slice measures.

Two filters keep the rule from firing on degenerate content. A command with no stdout renders to
"cwd=<cwd> exit=<code>\n", which is IDENTICAL across every mv and every redirect in a trajectory;
in the sample it is 30-45% of all steps. Filter 1 drops any observation whose within-row exact
duplicate class exceeds max_class (6) — this catches those, including the per-row variants with a
different cwd. Filter 2 finds the single most frequent exact-duplicate class across the whole batch
(probing 64 evenly-spaced valid positions) and suppresses it when it exceeds modal_frac (5%) of
valid positions. Verified on the sample: with both filters the mined set is the routed reads listed
above; on a batch of independent random embeddings the rule fires zero times.

On each mined read j the head then applies a multi-positive log-loss to the arch's OWN prefix
attention row at that command position, recomputed from the hidden states the arch returned (read
through a forward hook on the net) with the arch's own xq, xk, 1/sqrt(xattn_dim) scale and
strictly-earlier mask:

  nll_j = logsumexp_{p in DEN} ( s_jp + log(1+kappa)*ring_jp )  -  logsumexp_{p in POS} s_jp

  POS   = earlier positions with the same content and a different command   (the transported link)
  DEN   = POS  union  { earlier positions whose content differs }
  ring  = the top n_stale (2) positions of DEN\POS ranked by cosine similarity of their command
          embedding to cmd_j — the name-nearest wrong answers.

Prefix positions holding the same content under the SAME command text are in neither POS nor DEN:
answering by name lookup is not rewarded, and a value-correct position is never punished.

Three things follow that neither parent's head does. (1) The supervision target is content identity
across a name change, not observation change; the parent's head structurally excludes exactly these
examples via its change floor. (2) The supervised quantity is an attention distribution, which no
loss on predictions can reach: the objective only ever sees (pred, target), so the addressing that
decides WHICH prefix observation gets copied is unconstrained. It is worse than unconstrained at
init — the partner arch's x_out is zero-initialised, so d(main loss)/d(xq) and d(main loss)/d(xk)
are exactly zero at step 0 and the addressing is trained by nothing until the readout grows. This
aux is the only gradient those projections get early, and the optimizer routes the (64,176) xq/xk
sibling pair to Muon, so they are updated on the orthogonalised manifold. (3) The ring term
translates the partner objective's confusability-ring idea into addressing space, but ranks the
ring on COMMAND similarity rather than target similarity — the name-confusable earlier read (e.g.
an exposed `.../magic.txt` when the query is `.../magic.txt.1`) is precisely the stale answer.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca RATHER THAN THE ABSOLUTE PICK RATE

comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed on the same board. A predictor that answers a read from the
name of the location being read is identical in both arms and contributes exactly zero. The aux
does two things that break that tie in the right direction: it rewards mass on the prefix
observation reachable only through the intervening move commands (the query is built from the
hidden state of the read command, which can only know that content by having propagated the move
chain), and it explicitly raises the bar against the name-nearest content-different candidate,
which is the answer a name-keyed reader would give and is the same answer in both arms. Native and
swapped chains share the read token and differ only in how the moves route content, so improving
the routed link should raise native_hit while leaving, or lowering, swap_stayed.

The head adds no parameters, is train-time only, and does not touch the eval forward: it reads
hidden states through a forward hook that ignores no-grad calls, never re-points net.forward, never
registers the net inside another module, and returns a hard 0.0 whenever the cached hidden state
does not belong to the current batch's tok tensor or the arch has no xq/xk/x_out surface (verified:
on the r18 arch the head reports disabled and returns 0.0 every step).

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

- The mined links may be dominated by short chains and by the cvar copy arm, teaching a one-hop
  "find the earlier identical content" addressing that does not compose over the depth-3 and
  depth-4 chains the scored slice is made of. The eligible reads do come from depth 1..6 windows,
  but nothing in the loss weights depth, and a model can satisfy every mined row with a heuristic
  that only works when a single move separates source from destination.
- The aux backpropagates into the trunk (verified: gradient reaches cmd_proj, obs_proj and the
  transformer, not x_out or head). Forcing the trunk to make read commands and observations
  linkable could cost next-observation accuracy generally, dropping native_hit as much as
  swap_stayed and leaving the paired difference flat. align_w = 0.3 against a main loss of order 5
  with a 300-step smoothstep ramp is a guess at the right size, unmeasured.
- Sharp attention onto the right prefix observation still has to reach the prediction through the
  zero-init x_out and the gate at bias -2, and only the main loss opens those. If the main loss
  never opens the gate, correct addressing changes nothing that is measured.
- The duplicate thresholds are absolute (1e-4 on mean squared distance per dimension, against the
  1e-5 the objective already uses on the same quantity for standardized embeddings). If the pinned
  standardization frame put the observation embeddings at a very different scale, the rule would
  either fire on everything or on nothing; in the latter case the head returns 0.0 and the
  candidate degrades to the arch/objective crossover with no head.

Performance claims here are unmeasured. What was executed locally is correctness only: mining
precision against the real sample trajectories, shape/edge handling (n=1, n=2, all-pad rows,
example cap), gradient routing, finiteness, optimizability of the aux to zero on a synthetic batch,
and about 10-15% wall-clock overhead per step on CPU at B=64, n=32.
