WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS FROM EACH PROGRAM
The two programs I was given differ on exactly one axis: the objective. r22 is not an alternative
to r12 — it contains r12's mechanisms (per-dimension precision weighting, the confusability ring
with the duplicate band-stop, the repulsion margin, the MSE anchor) and adds one thing r12 lacks:
numerically-equal targets are collapsed into one multi-positive class, with occurrence mass divided
by class size. That matters on this data rather than in the abstract. In the sample trajectories a
large share of steps are mutations whose observation renders to exactly "cwd=/ exit=0\n" (every mv
and every `cat a > b` / `cat a >> b` produces empty stdout, exit 0, unchanged cwd), so their frozen
observation embeddings are bit-identical. r12 makes each such step the sole positive of its own row
and every identical sibling a negative, which is an unsatisfiable classification whose gradient
pushes identical targets apart and whose focal term keeps re-weighting toward it. r22 makes that
term exactly zero. Since the mutation steps are precisely the steps that carry the mv chain the
score is about, I keep r22 on the objective axis. Both parents' optim is identical, so the optim
axis is where a new mechanism has to come from, and that is my assigned axis.

WHAT THE NEW OPTIM ADDS THAT NEITHER PROGRAM COMPUTES

(1) A FIXED-NORM (SPHERICAL) CONSTRAINT ON THE ADDRESSING PROJECTIONS.
Both programs run Muon on the sibling-paired (key_d x d) addressing matrices. Muon's update is a
Newton-Schulz orthogonalization of the momentum, rescaled to a FIXED RMS: the step added to W is
-lr * rms_match * sqrt(max(shape)) * O with O orthogonal, so ||dW||_F is a constant of the schedule
and does not depend on the gradient magnitude. No weight decay is applied in that group. Therefore
||W||_F grows monotonically in expectation over training.
Every matrix in that group whose output the arch unit-normalizes is SCALE-INVARIANT: in
r18_pathstate_latent_transition_worldmodel, q_file/k_file are _quotient(file_read(x), Q) and
_quotient(file_write(x), Q) and _quotient ends in _unit(); q_path/k_path are _unit(path_read(h)) and
_unit(path_write(h)); p is _unit(tr_path(x)). For such a W, replacing W by cW (c>0) leaves the
network function bit-identical, and the only thing that moves the function is the ANGLE W travels.
With a constant-length step and a growing norm, the angular step is ||dW||/||W||, which decays as
||W|| grows: the delta-rule key geometry — the mechanism that decides which slot a content is
written into and read back from — freezes progressively over the run, and the LR schedule does not
actually control it.
The new code captures ||W||_F at make() time (make() is called on a freshly built net) and, after
every Muon step, rescales each of those matrices back to that radius: W <- W * (r0 / ||W||_F). This
is exactly function-preserving at the moment it is applied (unit-normalized outputs), so it cannot
change what the model represents; it only makes the per-step rotation angle constant and equal to
lr(t) * rms_match * sqrt(max(shape)) / r0, i.e. governed by the schedule alone. Concretely: the
total angle the addressing geometry travels over the fixed step budget goes up, and it keeps
travelling in the late phase instead of stalling.
Which tensors get the constraint is decided structurally, not by name. The Muon group is selected
exactly as before (2-D, shape[0]==key_d, shape[1] not in {key_d, D}, and at least two tensors of
that shape — the sibling rule). Inside that group the constraint is applied only to tensors that
are NOT the weight of a biased Linear, detected by checking whether the next parameter in
net.parameters() order is 1-D with length shape[0]. This matters here and is not cosmetic: with the
arch's defaults sys_d == key_d == 64, so sys_val.weight is also (64,176) and is already in the Muon
group, but its output goes through tanh() and is NOT scale-invariant — rescaling it would change
the function. The adjacency test excludes exactly it. I verified the selection against the arch's
parameter registration order: Muon group = {file_read, file_write, path_read, path_write, sys_val,
tr_path}.weight (identical to what both programs already route there, so that mechanism is
untouched), sphere set = the same minus sys_val.weight.

(2) A TERMINAL TAIL AVERAGE OF THE ITERATES, WITH THE DECAY SHORTENED TO PAY FOR IT.
The optimizer counts its own steps against the `steps` it was handed (the harness calls opt.step()
exactly once per training step, `steps` times) and accumulates a running sum of every parameter
tensor over the last tail_frac=0.25 of the budget. On the final step it writes the uniform mean of
those iterates into the live parameters, re-imposes the sphere radii, and re-runs the spectral cap.
The net the harness hands back is therefore the tail-averaged net, not the last iterate. Nothing
about the training trajectory is altered — the average is accumulated, never fed back.
Because averaging plays the role a decay-to-zero plays, the schedule is retuned to keep the tail
alive: hold_frac 0.30 -> 0.35 and floor_ratio 0.05 -> 0.20, so the averaged window spans LR
0.50 -> 0.20 of peak instead of collapsing to 0.05, where the iterates barely differ and an average
would be a no-op.

(3) THE SPECTRAL CAP IS KEPT, unchanged, on (D,D) readouts (here exactly tr_read.weight, the
zero-init injection through which the latent-transition memory reaches the prediction). It is what
makes (1) safe: a constant-speed rotation of the key geometry keeps rewriting what the delta-rule
memory stores, and the cap bounds the gain with which that content is injected.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca RATHER THAN JUST THE LOSS
comp_ca = mean over windows of (native_hit - swap_stayed). It is zero by construction for any
predictor that keys on the location being read, and it is only positive for a net that carries a
particular content's identity through the mv chain. In this arch that carrying is done by the
delta-rule associative memories, and which content lands in which slot is decided entirely by the
addressing projections' ANGLES. Mechanism (1) is the only change in this candidate that directly
increases how far those angles can travel inside a fixed step budget, and it does so without
touching the represented function at any instant — it removes an optimization artefact (norm growth
under a fixed-length update with no decay) that silently anneals the routing mechanism to a stop
while the schedule still reports full LR. Mechanism (2) attacks the other half of the objective's
statement: the measurement is a top-1 forced choice over a small eligible slice, so the final
iterate's noise is a direct additive noise on the score; a uniform tail average is the standard
variance reduction for exactly that (Polyak-Ruppert averaging; SWA; LAWA / constant-LR-plus-average
matching a decayed run), and there is no BatchNorm anywhere in the arch, so no statistics need
re-estimating after the swap — LayerNorm only.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL
- If ||W||_F of the addressing matrices does not actually grow much under Muon here (e.g. successive
  orthogonalized updates are close to mutually cancelling), the constraint is nearly a no-op and the
  candidate reduces to schedule retune + tail average, which is unlikely to clear the noise band.
- If the addressing geometry is not the binding constraint — if the memory already addresses well
  and what is missing is the content transition or the read mix — then rotating the keys faster and
  longer buys nothing, and holding a constant late-phase rotation may even prevent the keys from
  settling, costing accuracy on the ordinary next-observation task.
- The averaged window spans LR 0.50 -> 0.20 of peak. If the run is still descending rather than
  oscillating over that window, the average lags the trajectory and is worse than the last iterate.
  The higher floor (0.20) is what makes averaging worth doing and is also what makes this risk real;
  they cannot be separated.
- The sphere set is chosen by a structural test (2-D, key_d-rowed, sibling-paired, not followed by a
  matching 1-D bias). It is exact for this arch, checked against its parameter order. On some other
  arch a bias-free matrix of that shape whose output is NOT unit-normalized would be rescaled, and
  that would change the function; the failure would show up as a training-health change, not
  silently.
- r22 replaces r12 on the objective axis. If the exact-equality classes are rarer at bs=64 than the
  sample suggests, that half of the crossover contributes nothing and the whole result rests on (1)
  and (2).
