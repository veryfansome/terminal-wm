MECHANISM

The new batcher reconstructs, from command text alone, the thing the objective actually measures:
which content is sitting at a location after a chain of silent moves.

At construction it replays each training sequence's `cmds` list through a symbolic content
tracker that maintains one integer per path, the number of hops that routed the content now at
that path:

    mv SRC DST                 hops[DST] = hops.pop(SRC, 0) + 1
    cp SRC DST                 hops[DST] = hops.get(SRC, 0) + 1
    cat SRC > DST              hops[DST] = hops.get(SRC, 0) + 1
    cat SRC >> DST             hops[DST] = max(hops.get(DST, 0), hops.get(SRC, 0) + 1)
    cat SRC   (no redirect)    a READ of SRC at routing depth hops.get(SRC, 0)

Reads with depth 0 are ignored; a read with depth d >= 1 is a "routed read" — the observation the
model must predict there is the content of some *other* file, reachable only by following the
move chain. That is exactly the shape of the scored window (expose, silent chain, read), so the
tracker labels the training steps that carry the capability under test.

Two quantities come out of the replay, per sequence i:

  demand[i]  = sum over routed reads of min(depth, 4)
  sigs[i]    = the set of (image, normalized read path, capped depth) triples of its routed reads,
               where the path is normalized by collapsing long hex runs and digit runs to '#'
               (/tmp/w/cups/g53/f149.tmp -> /tmp/w/cups/g#/f#.tmp).

Sequences are indexed into buckets by signature. A batch is then composed as

  n_hard = bs * hard_frac_max * min(1, step / (ramp_frac * total_steps))

hard slots, filled by drawing whole GROUPS of group_size DISTINCT sequences from one bucket —
bucket chosen with probability proportional to size^0.5 * exp(depth_beta * depth), members chosen
without replacement with probability proportional to 1 + demand — and bs - n_hard uniform slots,
drawn iid exactly as the parent does, with rejection so no index repeats inside a batch when
len(fit) >= bs. The whole batch is permuted before return. Everything is drawn from one private
torch.Generator seeded with `seed`; `fit` is only read.

WHAT NEITHER PARENT COMPUTES

The parent (baseline_uniform) computes nothing about the data: every sequence is equally likely at
every step. The crossover partner's batcher (r6_sysblock_hardneg_curriculum) computes exactly one
property, s["image"], and blocks a few whole systems into a batch; it is blind to whether a
sequence contains a move chain at all, how deep that chain is, and whether any read lands on a
routed location. This impl computes the routing depth of every read in the training split by
symbolically executing the move/copy/redirect commands, which is a property of the chain, not of
the system, and it is the property the eval slices on (the parent's own feedback line reports the
eligible slice as d3 / d4+).

The crossover: the parent's contribution is kept as the uniform base — an unbiased, full-coverage
sample that is the majority of every early batch and never falls below 1 - hard_frac_max, so the
model still sees the whole distribution of systems and verbs. The partner's contribution is kept
in two places: the annealed hard fraction with a linear ramp (uniform first, structured later), and
same-image blocking — my bucket key BEGINS with the image, so every collision group is also a
same-system block, which is a strict refinement of the partner's pool rather than a replacement.
The new part is what makes a pool "hard": not merely same-system, but same-system AND same
normalized destination shape AND same chain depth.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca is native_hit minus swap_stayed. It is positive only for a model whose prediction at the
read follows the content the chain routed there, and it is zero by construction for a model that
keys on the location name, because that model emits the same prediction in both arms. Two forces
act on that:

1. Reweighting. Under any per-example loss, a batcher is an importance sampler: it changes which
   empirical risk is minimized. Routed reads are a minority of steps inside a sequence and their
   density varies across sequences (a sequence carries a variable number of cvar groups and cups
   movers). Weighting buckets by exp(depth_beta * depth) and members by 1 + demand raises the
   effective weight of exactly the steps whose target is only predictable by carrying a content
   identity across a move, at the expense of ambient ls/uname/cat steps that are predictable from
   the command string alone. A location-keying solution has zero loss headroom on those steps and
   nonzero headroom on routed reads, so shifting mass toward routed reads increases the gradient
   pressure that separates the two solutions.

2. Collision. A group of group_size distinct sequences that share image, normalized read path and
   depth presents the network with near-identical surface contexts whose correct targets are
   different contents. Averaging is not a solution for any member of the group, so the update must
   use the only feature that distinguishes them: which expose step the chain routed to that path.
   This is hard-example mining translated from metric learning into this setting, and it is why the
   pool is defined by path shape rather than by system: two sequences from the same image whose
   reads land on differently-shaped paths are not confusable and are not a useful pair.

Force 2 is amplified when the loss couples rows within a batch. That is why this proposal takes the
crossover partner's objective (r22_exact_target_equivalence_quotient) and its arch / optim / head
rather than the parent's plain MSE stack: a batcher that composes a negative pool is only worth its
slot next to a loss that consumes one, and the partner's stack is the one in front of me that does.
Force 1 does not depend on that and would still act under plain MSE.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

- Dispersion. If routing depth and routed-read count are effectively constant across training
  sequences, the demand weights and the depth tilt are near-uniform and force 1 vanishes; the impl
  then reduces to collision grouping alone. I have read one full training sequence verbatim: it
  carries a cups window of depth 1 with two movers and four cvar groups with one to three
  redirect ops each, so within-sequence structure varies — but I have NOT measured the
  across-sequence distribution of depth, and that is the single assumption most likely to be wrong.
- Bucket population. If the normalized path shapes are almost unique per sequence, few buckets
  reach two members, most groups degenerate, and the batch is nearly uniform. The normalization
  collapses the numeric parts of the synthetic cups/cvar paths, which is what should make shapes
  recur, but this is unmeasured.
- Coverage loss. Concentrating half the batch on deep-chain sequences reduces the number of
  distinct systems seen per step. The eval holds out by IMAGE, so if the mechanism trades
  cross-system generalization for chain specialization the number can move down, not up. The
  uniform base and the ramp bound this, but do not eliminate it.
- Wrong home. If the partner's objective does not in fact couple rows within a batch, force 2 is
  inert and only force 1 remains; and if the partner's arch/optim/head are themselves weak, the
  child inherits that weakness regardless of the batcher.
- Parse drift. The tracker assumes whitespace-separated commands with no spaces in paths and no
  pipelines; the sequence I read satisfies this. Commands it cannot parse are skipped, which
  under-counts depth rather than mis-counting it, so the failure is a weakened signal rather than
  a wrong one.

All performance statements here are unmeasured. No miniature run was used to support any of them.

COST

One pass over every training sequence's command list at construction (O(total steps), pure Python
string work), then per step roughly bs/group_size multinomial draws over the bucket table plus one
randint block and one randperm. This is negligible beside a transformer step and does not change
the step budget.
