WHAT THE CROSSOVER KEEPS

The two programs I was given differ on exactly one axis: the parent runs arch
r18_pathstate_latent_transition_worldmodel, the partner runs r22_retrieval_composition_renderer.
r22 subclasses r18, constructs its extra modules after the whole inherited __init__ so the
inherited parameters draw the identical init RNG stream, and zero-inits comp_out, so at
initialisation r22 computes exactly the r18 function and then can learn a vector-wise interaction
between the retrieved-content channels on top of it. The union of the two programs is therefore
well defined rather than a compromise: r22 arch (which contains the parent's path-state latent
transition operator, the delta-rule file/path memories and the transition_from_emb entry point the
head aux supervises) plus everything else the two share — objective r12_antiretrieval_ring_negatives,
optim r18_spectral_capped_transition_readout, head r18_transition_forwardmodel_consistency. That is
the arch/optim/head/objective half of this proposal, and it is a selection, not new code.

The parent's batcher mechanism I keep is system blocking: part of every batch is drawn from a
single image so the in-batch negatives are same-system observations. It survives as one of the two
block families below, and my sampler reduces exactly to it when alpha = 0.

WHAT THE NEW BATCHER COMPUTES THAT NEITHER PROGRAM COMPUTES

Both programs sample sequences by image only. Neither program looks at what a sequence's commands
actually do. Mine parses, once at make_batcher time and only from the strings in s["cmds"], the
mv-reachability graph of each training trajectory:

  edges  = {(t, src, dst) : cmds[t] == "mv src dst"}
  wrote[dst] = [(t, src) ...]
  for every read step "cat P" at time t:
      cur, ct, d = P, t, 0
      while some (j, s) in wrote[cur] has j < ct:
          (ct, cur) = the latest such (j, s);  d += 1
      hop count of that read = d
  depth(sequence) = max d over its reads;  read_at(sequence) = the step index attaining it

d is literally the number of mv hops between the location being read and the file the content
started in. I checked this against the 24 real trajectories in evolve/jail_data/train_sample.jsonl:
the value this walk returns equals the meta["cups"]["depth"] recorded on that trajectory's
cups_test step in 24 of 24 sequences. So a batcher can recover the exact compositional depth of
a training window from command text alone, with no access to meta (which the encoded fit dicts do
not carry anyway — they carry only z_obs, z_cmd, cmds, image, ok). The distribution of that depth
in the sample is 1:4, 2:8, 3:4, 4:5, 5:2, 6:1 — half the trajectories are depth 1 or 2, and each
trajectory contributes exactly one deepest routed read out of ~30 steps.

Two things are then built on that quantity.

(1) A Boltzmann tilt on structural difficulty. z is the per-sequence depth, centred and scaled to
unit RMS and clipped to [-4, 4]. Inside a block, members are drawn with probability proportional to
exp(alpha * z), alpha ramping linearly 0 -> alpha_max over the first ramp_frac of training, and the
blocked share of the batch ramping 0 -> hard_frac_max on the same schedule. At step 1 alpha = 0 and
the blocked share is 0, so the sampler starts at the plain uniform stream and anneals into a
hard-example distribution — a competence-style curriculum whose difficulty measure is exact and
structural rather than loss-derived (the contract gives the batcher no loss feedback, so a
prioritised-replay-style online priority is not available; this is its static structural analogue).
A uniform component of size bs*(1 - hard_frac_max*ramp) is kept for the whole run so coverage of
the corpus is never lost.

(2) Read-collision blocks. For every sequence with at least one routed read I take the command
embedding q = z_cmd[read_at] and the answer embedding a = z_obs[read_at], project both through one
fixed Gaussian matrix to 128 dims (Johnson-Lindenstrauss; drawn from a private generator seeded
from `seed`), L2-normalise, and for each centre take its top-16 neighbours with cosine(q_i, q_j) >=
0.85 after masking out any j with cosine(a_i, a_j) > 0.995. A collision pool is therefore a set of
trajectories whose deepest routed read is asked with a near-indistinguishable command while the
correct answers are demonstrably not the same content. On the sample these pools come out as
exactly what they should: several distinct trajectories all ending in `cat
/tmp/w/cups/usr/local/etc/app.ini.6`, or all in `cat /tmp/w/cups/usr/share/misc/magic.txt.1`, each
holding a different blob.

Each batch is a uniform part plus n_blocks blocks; every block is drawn as a collision pool with
probability coll_share and as one image pool otherwise, and members within it are drawn with the
exp(alpha*z) tilt.

WHY THIS SHOULD MOVE comp_ca

comp_ca = native_hit - swap_stayed. A predictor that keys on which location is being read predicts
identically in both arms and contributes exactly zero per window; the score is only paid to a model
whose answer is determined by which content the chain actually routed to that location. The
training loss is a listwise contrastive over every cmd position in the batch — with bs = 64 and
~30 steps per trajectory that is a softmax over roughly 1900 targets, of which the routed reads are
about 64, and only about half of those sit at the depth-3-and-up band the scored slice is drawn
from. Two things follow, and this batcher attacks both.

First, signal share. Measured on a 960-sequence corpus built from the real trajectories, the batch
composition moves the fraction of sampled sequences with depth >= 3 from 0.506 (uniform) to 0.774,
and depth >= 4 from 0.338 to 0.640, while still touching 959 of the 960 distinct sequences over the
window measured. Roughly a 1.9x increase in the density of depth-4-and-up routing examples per
gradient step, at a fixed step budget, on the exact structure the eligible slice consists of.

Second, and this is the part the metric's own construction argues for: the collision pools force
the name-keyed solution to be loss-bearing during training rather than merely unrewarded at eval.
When k trajectories whose read command is the same string sit in one batch, their k targets are
different contents inside one softmax. A model that answers from the read location alone assigns
them the same logits and cannot do better than 1/k on that group; the only representation that
separates them is one that followed each trajectory's own move chain. That is the training-time
image of the role-swap arm. On the same 960-sequence corpus the number of same-read-command pairs
co-present in a batch goes from 117 (uniform) to 636.

WHAT WOULD HAVE TO BE TRUE FOR THIS TO FAIL

- If the depth-3+ examples are not the binding constraint — if the model is capacity- or
  optimisation-limited rather than signal-limited — then a 1.9x density change buys nothing and
  the score moves inside the noise band. This is the most likely failure.
- Windows are held out by IMAGE. Concentrating 75% of each batch on a tilted, blocked distribution
  narrows the effective training set; if the concentration costs more in transfer to unseen systems
  than it gains in depth density, comp_ca can go down. The 25% uniform floor and the coverage figure
  above (959/960 distinct sequences touched) bound this but do not eliminate it.
- The collision pools depend on the destination paths of routed reads recurring across trajectories.
  In the 24-sequence sample 6 of 27 routed-read paths recur, and the recurring ones are the canonical
  decoy destinations (app.ini, magic.txt, sshd_config.cfg, backup.job). If the real train split
  randomises those far more than the sample suggests, pools shrink or vanish; the code then falls
  back to image blocks (have_coll requires at least 8 pools) and the proposal degrades to tilt-only,
  which is a smaller effect.
- If the frozen encoder places all cups read commands close together anyway, the 0.85 cosine
  threshold admits neighbours that are not really the same location, and the collision pressure
  becomes ordinary hard-negative mining rather than a location-name counterfactual.
- Everything above is a claim about batch composition, which I measured directly. Nothing here is a
  measured claim about comp_ca; no training run was performed from this jail.

CONTRACT AND COST

make_batcher(fit, bs, seed, **params) -> next_batch(step, total_steps) -> list[int] of exactly bs
ints in [0, len(fit)). Two private torch.Generators (one seeded `seed` for sampling, one seeded
seed ^ 0x9E3779B9 for the projection matrix and the centre subsample); the global torch RNG is
never touched and fit is never mutated — both checked. Degenerate inputs (n < 2, no cmds, a single
image with no routed reads, fewer than 8 collision pools and fewer than 2 images) fall back to the
plain uniform stream. Setup on a 960-sequence corpus is 0.018 s and a batch costs 47 microseconds;
the neighbour search is chunked and capped at max_centers=8000 rows in the projected 128-dim space,
so it stays O(n * max_centers * 128) rather than O(n^2 * 768).
